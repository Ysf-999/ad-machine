# Dan Kennedy No B.S. DR Marketing Tactics

Source: *The Dirty Little Secrets About Successful Marketing* — No B.S. Inner Circle Consumer Report

## CORE PHILOSOPHY

**You are not in the dog grooming / carpet cleaning / supplement / ecommerce business.**
You are in the **business of marketing** your product or service. The distinction is everything.

Most owners go to work every morning as employee-bees doing the work of the business. Winners go to their **Marketing Ops HQ** to market the business.

**The fundamental identity shift:** Stop being a practitioner. Become a marketer who happens to sell that thing.

## FRAMEWORK 1: THE MARKETING TRIANGLE (Message–Market–Media)

The most important organizing framework in Kennedy's system. Every marketing decision must pass through all three vertices:

```
        MESSAGE
        /     \
   MARKET ——— MEDIA
```

- **Message** — what you say, the offer, the copy, the angle
- **Market** — who you're talking to (specific, defined, laser-targeted)
- **Media** — the channel used to reach them

**The Rule:** Never use any media that isn't targeted to your specific market, or that interferes with your message being delivered as it must be to be effective. And never deliver one message to all segments — divide customers into sub-groups and talk differently to each.

**DTC application:** Don't run the same Meta ad to all audiences. Different creative for: new buyers, repeat buyers, lapsed customers, lookalike cold traffic. Each is a different market, needs a different message.

## FRAMEWORK 2: THE KENNEDY DIFFERENTIATION TRIANGLES

### Triangle 1: The Marketing Triangle (above)

### Triangle 2: Problem–Agitate–Solve (PAS)
Classic sales structure:
- **Problem** — name it exactly as the customer feels it
- **Agitate** — rub the wound; make the problem feel bigger, more urgent, more costly
- **Solve** — introduce your product as the inevitable answer

### Triangle 3: Star–Story–Solution (Halbert's)
- **Star** — introduce a relatable protagonist (the customer avatar)
- **Story** — the struggle, the journey, the turning point
- **Solution** — your product as the mechanism of transformation

### Triangle 4: Awareness–Decision–Action
Sales funnel in three stages. Every piece of marketing belongs to one of these three jobs. Most DTC brands neglect the Decision stage entirely — they create awareness and then try to get action with no intermediate step.

### Triangle 5: Good Value–Better Value–Best Value
The cowardly lion's way to test price. Present three tiers, let the market tell you where the gravity is. Never force a Yes/No decision; force a Yes/Yes decision (which version?).

### Triangle 6: The Differentiation Triangle — Apart–Above–Alone
Kennedy's most powerful new framework for Category of One positioning:

- **Apart** — Whatever everyone else in your category IS, you AREN'T. Refuse the industry's default identity. If every supplement brand uses clinical white and generic claims — don't. If every competitor runs "salesperson" style outreach — don't. The telltale signs of sameness are visible; strip them.

- **Above** — By unassailable authority, celebrity, mountainous social proof, center-of-influencer endorsements — place yourself above all others in the category. The consumer should feel they could settle for one of those, or, if fortunate, get you.

- **Alone** — Sell in a vacuum and controlled environment. Circumvent search (don't rely on it). Find out-of-category media and sales places. Own channels where you are the only option visible. Offline media often outperforms online for this reason.

**Goal:** Category of One. No comparison possible. Price irrelevant.

### Triangle 7: Countering Skepticism
Three-part shield against disbelief:
1. **Unquestionable Authority** — credentials, experience, social proof volume
2. **Preponderance of Proof** — overwhelming evidence (testimonials, studies, demos, numbers)
3. **Risk Reversal** — guarantee so strong it makes not trying irrational

### Triangle 8: Price Presentation
Three tools to make any price feel reasonable:
1. **Minimization (to Free)** — "pays for itself in 3 weeks," "less than a cup of coffee per day"
2. **Favorable Comparison (Apples-to-Oranges)** — compare to a more expensive or dissimilar item, never a direct competitor
3. **Bundling to Defy Comparison** — package it so comparison is impossible

## FRAMEWORK 3: LEAD GENERATION — THE 5-PART AD STRUCTURE

Instead of advertising your product, advertise FOR the person you want to attract. Offer a free information widget that is irresistible only to your exact ideal customer.

**The 5 Elements of a Great Lead Gen Ad:**

1. **The "Call Out" / Identity Headline** — Names the exact person this is for. By doing so, it simultaneously tells everyone else to ignore it. Most advertisers fear this because they think exclusion = lost sales. Wrong. Exclusion = precision magnetism.

2. **"Dog Whistle" Language** — Words that only carry meaning to the target. Written TO be heard only by certain people. Others don't grasp it; insiders self-identify instantly.

3. **Clarity / Unabashed Specificity** — Who you want to respond, stated with zero ambiguity. "Sophisticated single women aged 40–60, active, well-built, well-kept, non-smokers who enjoy cultural activities." Most advertisers go timid here. Don't.

4. **Only What's Necessary — No Getting Ahead of Yourself** — Zero information about the company, its history, its awards, its process. Lead gen is not the place for brand-building. That comes later. The ad's only job: get the hand-raise.

5. **Response Directions** — One path. Clean. Direct. Do NOT offer Facebook, LinkedIn, YouTube, website, and phone simultaneously. One CTA. One destination.

**Key principle:** It is FINE — even desirable — to intimidate, repel, and offend non-ideal prospects. A lead gen ad should be precision magnetism, not a fishing net.

## FRAMEWORK 4: ROMANCING THE STONE (Making Mundane Products Extraordinary)

Any product can be made to seem unique and extraordinary. The 10-element system (from the EdenPURE heater case study):

1. **Expert/Celebrity Endorsement** — You can't afford Bob Vila? You can afford a local fire chief, a university professor, a doctor in your field. Anybody with title + authority.

2. **Item of Vital Interest to YOUR Buyer** — Find the one thing your specific buyer demographic cares about deeply and weave it throughout. For 50+ buyers: Made in USA. For parents: safety around pets and children. Identify YOUR buyer's version of this.

3. **The Difficulty Invested in Making It** — "U.S. engineers worked hundreds of hours…" The more blood, sweat, and tears invested in getting the product to the customer, the more valuable it feels. If you traveled to source ingredients, describe the trip. If you hand-select inventory, describe the process. Make them feel the effort.

4. **Proprietary / Exclusive Features** — Everything has something proprietary. If your product doesn't have a patent, create proprietary *terminology* for your process, then trademark it. "No one else has [The X Method]" is a legitimate claim if the name is yours.

5. **Why and How It Works Better** — Mechanism. Show the cutaway. Explain the difference. Use visuals and plain language. "Unlike other heaters that burn oxygen and rise to the ceiling, ours…"

6. **Leadership Position** — "Over 1 million satisfied customers." "Nation's leading." "Choice of safety professionals." Accept no imitations = there must be imitations = this is the original. Leadership position is especially persuasive to buyers 50+.

7. **What the Product ISN'T** — Sometimes describing what something won't do is more persuasive than describing what it will. "Will not burn children or pets." "Will not reduce humidity or oxygen." Negative features = safety proof.

8. **Testimonials** — Identified by occupation. Story-driven. Pushing the two or three key objections (safety, savings, ease). Not just quote + name. Scene + transformation + result.

9. **Guarantee** — Not a footnote. A feature. Bold. Specific. "60-day no-questions-asked satisfaction guarantee. We'll even pay return shipping." The guarantee is a selling point.

10. **Long Copy** — Use as much copy as the sale requires. The heater letter was ~1,800 words plus brochure. Long copy isn't a style preference; it's necessary when the product is unknown and the price is significant.

**Critical note:** Do NOT push people to the website to learn more or order. The moment you tell a prospect to "go look online," you've handed them to Google and Amazon. Selling ends with closing.

## FRAMEWORK 5: THE CALL-TO-ACTION (11-Part CTA Structure)

A confused consumer does nothing. Every CTA must include:

1. **Clear Instructions** — Literally tell them what to do, step by step. "Put the order form in the enclosed envelope and mail it today." Not implied — stated.

2. **"Yes" Sentences** — At the top of order forms. Re-state the offer as affirmative agreement. "YES! I want to receive [X] and [Y]…" Restates the sale AND the guarantee.

3. **Summary of Everything Included** — By the time they reach the CTA, they've forgotten what's in the middle of the presentation. Law of Primacy/Recency: they remember the first + last things. Summarize everything.

4. **Offer Options to Choose From** — Never Yes/No. Always A or B. Basic or Deluxe. Good/Better/Best. The question is never "Should I buy?" but "Which version?"

5. **Deadline(s)** — Every CTA must have at least one. Can be a combo: "The first 41 orders, or by midnight tonight, whichever comes first."

6. **Reason for Immediate Action** — When you ask for and reward immediate action, you need a *rational* reason why the best offer is limited. "We only have 312 units at this price because…"

7. **Bonus for Immediate Action** — Extra discount, second unit free, bonus gift, free shipping. Often the bonus gets as much emphasis as the core product.

8. **Upsell** — After the initial purchase decision, introduce an upgrade. Thank-you page, telemarketer, PS in the letter, order form item.

9. **Payment Instructions** — Absolutely, crystal-clearly stated. What they're paying, how, and when. No ambiguity.

10. **Guarantee Restatement** — The guarantee and reassurances made in the body of the presentation are re-summarized here. Bundle it with bonuses: "When you act now, you get [bonus] PLUS our 90-day unconditional money-back guarantee."

11. **Means of Response** — Order and weight the response methods. If one is profoundly better for you (online vs. phone vs. mail), add an incentive to use that one specifically.

## FRAMEWORK 6: BUNDLE vs. CAFETERIA SELLING

**Never cafeteria-sell when you can bundle-sell.**

**6 reasons to bundle:**
1. Boosts average transaction size
2. Confuses and diffuses apples-to-apples price comparison
3. Makes the buying decision easier and faster
4. People pay for convenience
5. Creates interest through the organized "system" or "package" naming
6. Path to differentiation / unique selling proposition

**3 reasons NOT to cafeteria-sell:**
1. People scratch the itch with the cheapest item possible
2. Puts focus on price and easy comparison
3. May slow or discourage the purchase ("I'll buy this now, then find the other thing later")

**Examples:** Pet store "New Dog Starter Kit" — everything at one price. Clothing: Suit + jacket + 2 slacks + 2 shirts + 2 ties — one price. Travel: All-inclusive resort.

## FRAMEWORK 7: INFO-FIRST MARKETING (Anti-Commoditization System)

This is Kennedy's solution to commoditization and price destruction. It's NOT content marketing. It's direct-response-grounded authority building.

**4 strategic purposes:**

1. **Escape From Commoditization** — When Amazon, Angie's List, and portal sites reduce everything to price comparison, Info-First puts you on a completely separate path that consumers access differently. They come to you as a trusted authority, not a commodity supplier.

2. **Resistance Against Price Destruction** — Commoditization carries price destruction like poorly cared-for dogs carry fleas. Info-First lifts you out of shop-by-price competition entirely.

3. **Elevate Your Status** — Publishing, teaching, and demonstrating expertise creates "Trusted Authority" status. The dog walker who publishes a book on dog psychology is not competing with other dog walkers.

4. **Pull, Not Push** — Info-First supports lead generation. Pull: prospect steps forward and asks for information. Push: you pour brand/product/price into a marketplace hoping someone cares. Pull is far more financially efficient. Prospect has "skin in the game" — at minimum, expressed interest and commitment.

**DIRE WARNING:** If you can be commoditized, you WILL be commoditized. Every industry has a disruptor building the infrastructure to destroy pricing. The only practical defense: Info-First.

## FRAMEWORK 8: 10 TIMING-BASED MARKETING OPPORTUNITIES

Never run the same campaign all year. Attach marketing to time:

1. **Seasons** — 4 of them. Tie offers to season-specific desires ("Spring Cleaning Time…")
2. **Other Calendar Moments** — New Year, back to school, etc.
3. **Specific Holidays** — Every holiday is an opportunity. Visit brownielocks.com for obscure ones.
4. **Christmas** — Twist what you sell into a givable gift. Every product can be gifted.
5. **Events** — Local community events, global events, elections, finales — any cultural moment is a hook.
6. **When People Get Money** — Tax refund season. First of month (for welfare recipients). First 2 weeks of January (gift cards). Know when your customer's wallet is fat.
7. **Year-End Tax / Benefits Deadlines** — B2B: depreciation, expense timing. Non-profits: year-end giving. Most businesses never show up in the last 2 weeks of December with a timed pitch. Do it.
8. **Personalized Timing** — Birthdays, anniversaries, benchmark anniversaries, first purchase anniversary. These are relationship moments with automatic emotional charge.
9. **Celebrity Dates** — Birthday, death anniversary, cultural moments tied to a relevant figure.
10. **Your Business's Natural Ebb and Flow** — Identify peak periods and concentrate resources there. Don't advertise equally all year. Weight spend toward high-conversion windows.

## FRAMEWORK 9: CULTIVATED GOODWILL SYSTEM

This is Kennedy's long-game retention strategy, modeled partly on President George H.W. Bush's famous card-file system.

**The core practice:** Small, deliberate, personal gestures — handwritten notes, follow-ups referencing previous conversations, unexpected acknowledgments — accumulate into a "stock in trade" of goodwill that no competitor can easily replicate.

**Joan Rivers' system:**
- Group A: Must be reminded of her existence no less than every 2 weeks
- Group B: No less than every month
- Group C: No less than every 3 months

The practice for businesses: Identify your 200 most important relationships (customers, influencers, partners, prospects). Put them in tiers. Build a rhythm of contact.

**The 10-a-Day Plan (Dr. Edward Kramer):** Block 1 hour every day to hand-write and send 10 thank-you notes. To customers who bought, customers who didn't, vendors, partners, strangers who gave you a useful idea. Ten. Every day.

**DTC application:** Post-purchase handwritten notes (or premium-feeling printed equivalents), VIP customer birthday campaigns, anniversary-of-first-purchase gifts, surprise-and-delight moments for best customers. These cost almost nothing and build the fortress competitors can't breach.

## FRAMEWORK 10: PLACE IDENTITY

People care more about people and places than they do products. They prefer buying FROM someone — a person with a place, a story, a sensibility — not from a nameless, faceless company.

**Vermont Country Store principle:** Everything they sell could be sold from a generic website anywhere. But their entire narrative — the "Letter From Our Family," the story, the sense of Vermont, the worn wood floors, the family proprietors — takes you somewhere. It creates shared sense of place between customer and brand.

**Place Identity is a marketing asset.** Most DTC brands leave this asset completely unutilized. The brand that has a place — a city, a founding story, a physical context — and communicates it consistently creates something no competitor can copy.

**Application:** Know your Place Identity. Use it. It might be a founder's garage in 2018. A specific city. A farm. A methodology born in a specific context. Bring customers into it. Make them feel they are somewhere specific when they buy from you.

## FRAMEWORK 11: GUARANTEED ATTENTION TACTICS (Direct Mail Logic Applied to Digital)

Halbert's core truth: *America sorts its mail standing over a waste basket.* Apply this to every piece of content, every email, every ad. The question isn't "did I send it?" — it's "did I BUY readership?"

**4 Attention Principles (apply digitally):**

1. **The Big Object in the Box** — For direct mail: something that rattles, surprises, creates physical curiosity. For email/DTC: the equivalent is a subject line, a preview image, or a hook so surprising it *earns* the open. What rattles inside your email subject line?

2. **The Personalized Check** — Something that looks personally addressed, personally sent, personally relevant. Mass mail that feels like personal mail. Personalization tokens are the digital equivalent.

3. **Real Money / Something of Tangible Value** — Attach perceived tangible value to the act of opening. A real offer, a real discount, a real gift — not "learn more." Something they'd be foolish to throw away.

4. **Personal Mail from Someone They Know** — The most powerful form. Endorsed mailings, referral programs, influencer endorsements by people the prospect actually follows. Not a celebrity to everyone — a trusted voice to YOUR specific audience.

**Results rule. Period.** Not opinions, not Halbert's ghost, not what looks smart. Only split-test results matter.

## JUNK MAIL LESSON: TWO CONDITIONS THAT OVERRIDE EVERYTHING

A terrible-looking envelope CAN work if:
1. **The list is exactly right** — The medium's existence makes recipients' hearts go pitter-patter (a Backpacker Magazine envelope to committed backpackers)
2. **Business economics are engineered to get rich from minimal response** — This is the secret most DTC brands miss: endlessly optimizing for break-even on customer acquisition LITERALLY PROHIBITS success. You must engineer backend revenue so you can afford to acquire customers at cost or at a loss.

## QUICK-REFERENCE: TARGETING DECISION TRIANGLE

Before spending on any media or list:
- **Can they buy?** — Financial ability to purchase
- **Will they buy?** — Psychological readiness; the desire exists
- **How do they buy?** — The preferred channel, medium, format

All three must be YES before spending.
