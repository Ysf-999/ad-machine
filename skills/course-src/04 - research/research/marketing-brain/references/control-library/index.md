# Control Pattern Library

Purpose: reusable direct-response patterns extracted from high-performing ad analyses. Use these as structural evidence, not wording to copy.

- Controls indexed: 65
- Pattern families: 16

## Non-Slop Rules

1. Pick 1-3 patterns because they match the product's buyer pressure, awareness stage, mechanism, and proof stack.
2. Adapt belief movement and visual grammar; never copy transcript wording, claims, jokes, or fake proof.
3. If a pattern requires proof the product does not have, choose another pattern or mark the script as hypothesis-only.
4. Use the target product's reviews, landing page, constraints, and VOC as the factual floor.

## Pattern Families

### Podcast Myth-Bust (`podcast-myth-bust`)

- Controls: 10
- Evidence quality: pattern-only
- Performance weight: 2.5
- Best for: Categories where education increases conversion: supplements, beauty, language learning, software, financial tools.
- Transferable pattern: A casual authority conversation challenges a common belief, explains why the old method fails, then reveals the better mechanism.
- Do not copy: Do not copy the podcast topic, stat, or authority claim unless substantiated for the product.
- Common hook types: curiosity-gap, direct-callout, confrontation, negative-safety
- Common visual variables: Contrarian Opening, The "Value Density" Variable, Authority via Organization, The "Template" Tease, The Podcast Aesthetic, Polarizing Language
- Adaptation rules:
  - Start with a belief the market recognizes as true.
  - Use the second speaker to make the contradiction feel obvious.
  - Name the failed alternative before naming the product.
  - Make the mechanism visual or metaphorical enough to understand on mute.

### Skeptical Friend Dialogue (`dialogue-skeptic`)

- Controls: 9
- Evidence quality: pattern-only
- Performance weight: 2.25
- Best for: Problem-aware or solution-aware buyers with obvious skepticism, category fatigue, or 'is this just another X?' resistance.
- Transferable pattern: One speaker voices the viewer's objection; the second answers with a sharper mechanism, proof, and low-friction next step.
- Do not copy: Do not copy the transcript, jokes, exact objection wording, or brand-specific claims. Rebuild the skeptic's line from the target buyer's real inhibition.
- Common hook types: direct-callout, confrontation, authority, curiosity-gap, negative-safety
- Common visual variables: Relatable Setting, The "Skeptical Friend" Archetype, Feature-Specific Screen Caps, Podcast Aesthetic, Specific List-Building, The "Tired Agency Owner" Angle
- Adaptation rules:
  - Open with the objection the buyer is already thinking.
  - Use the answerer to create a new belief, not to list features.
  - Let the skeptic ask the next sales objection before the viewer can leave.
  - End with one direct CTA or one clear curiosity bridge.
