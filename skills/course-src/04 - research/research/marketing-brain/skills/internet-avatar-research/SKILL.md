# Internet Avatar Research

## Purpose

Convert avatar research discipline into an internet-only workflow. Do not run interviews, focus groups, recruiting, or manual survey fieldwork unless the user explicitly asks. Use public or user-supplied internet evidence to decide what creative, offer, landing page, or research question should change.

## Read When Needed

- `../../references/avatar-research-dtc-playbook.md` for decision-first research, behavioral/attitudinal/classification buckets, review mining, trigger events, pain ladders, frequency/intensity scoring, source screening, and quote-evidence-implication.

## Decision-First Rule

Start every avatar research run by stating:

- decision to make
- options being compared
- what evidence would change the decision
- what is already known
- what internet sources can answer fastest
- what will be treated as weak evidence

Do not collect generic "pain points." Research should change an ad angle, hook, archetype, offer, objection stack, LP section, or creative test matrix.

## Internet Source Screen

Treat a source as useful when it shows at least one:

- recent buyer or search behavior
- purchase comparison or category-shopping context
- failed alternative
- objection or refund language
- emotionally specific pain
- trigger event that caused active search
- dream outcome in natural language
- competitor complaint or praise

Low-quality sources are allowed only as weak directional evidence.

## Extraction Buckets

Tag every finding as:

- Behavioral: what they did, bought, searched, tried, quit, compared, refunded, or repeated.
- Attitudinal: what they think, feel, fear, desire, resent, doubt, or hope.
- Classification: who or what situation they are in, including life stage, trigger event, purchase stage, problem duration, category experience, and decision-maker role.

## Required Lenses

Use these on internet evidence:

1. Trigger event hunt: what happened right before they became an active buyer or searcher?
2. Pain ladder: surface pain -> practical cost -> emotional/social cost -> identity/status fear.
3. Failed alternative map: what they tried before and why it failed.
4. Objection map: what almost stops purchase, ranked by frequency and intensity.
5. Dream outcome map: what daily life looks like if the product works.
6. Competitor gap map: what competitors promise, what customers praise, what customers complain about.
7. Decision-maker check: user, buyer, researcher, and payer may be different people.

## Scoring

Score each theme 1-5:

- frequency across sources
- emotional intensity
- buyer intent
- source credibility
- creative usefulness
- proof availability

Rank by combined judgment, not frequency alone. A low-frequency phrase with high emotional intensity can be a stronger hook than a common but bland complaint.

## Review Mining

For owned and competitor reviews:

- 5-star reviews: extract trigger, prior skepticism, reason for belief, outcome, surprise benefit, exact phrase.
- 1-star reviews: extract failed expectation, objection, friction, refund issue, product limitation, competitor weakness.
- Mixed reviews: extract realistic promise boundaries and retargeting objections.

Do not turn qualitative review volume into fake percentages. Say "recurring theme" unless a real count/sample is provided.

## Output Format

Return:

1. Decision-first research brief.
2. Source quality notes.
3. Behavioral / attitudinal / classification table.
4. Trigger event map ranked by creative value.
5. Pain ladder by archetype.
6. Failed alternative and objection map.
7. Frequency x intensity theme ranking.
8. Verbatim phrase bank with source references.
9. Quote-evidence-implication bullets.
10. Recommended creative decisions: hooks, angles, LP sections, retargeting, and tests.
11. Research gaps and weak-evidence warnings.

## Operator Rule

The best avatar insight is not "who they are." It is the moment, belief, fear, and failed alternative that makes them ready to click today.
