# Angle Factory

## Standard

An angle is a belief-shifting attack plan, not a headline. It connects an existing desire to a credible mechanism while reducing one major inhibition.

Load `$market-pressure-context` before generating or ranking angles. Each angle should identify the market signal, buyer pressure, belief shift, mechanism weapon, proof stack, and likely creative asset.

## Inputs

Use offer diagnosis, VOC, archetypes, source evidence, constraints, and competitor context. If research is missing, label each angle as hypothesis-only.

When grounding strategy, use the shared master references in `../../references/`: Schwartz for awareness/sophistication, Halbert for starving-crowd and central-idea discipline, Kennedy for offer/proof/CTA, Cialdini for contrast/social proof/authority/scarcity/commitment, and Wundt for excitation/inhibition.

If the user passes `--extreme`, produce bolder angle territories: stronger enemy framing, more visceral cost-of-inaction, harder failed-alternative contrast, sharper status/social comparison, and more urgent curiosity gaps. Do not discard an angle because it is polarizing, uncomfortable, or too direct for conservative brand taste. Rank for scale potential first.

## Angle Types

Generate across:
- problem intensification
- mechanism revelation
- failed alternative contrast
- identity callout
- proof-led
- contrarian reframe
- hidden cost
- speed/convenience
- risk reversal
- social comparison
- seasonal/timing trigger

## Ranking Criteria

Score 1-5:
- desire intensity
- belief fit
- novelty vs. competitors
- proof availability
- compliance safety
- visualizability
- LFS depth
- scaling potential

## Output Format

For each angle:
- angle name
- archetype
- awareness stage
- core belief shift
- hook territory
- mechanism
- proof needed
- objection handled
- example hooks
- recommended format
- risk notes

In `--extreme`, add:
- aggressive version of the hook
- why it could unlock spend
- launch-owner approval note

## DR Rule

Do not create angles that require the market to want something it does not already want. Channel demand; do not manufacture it.
