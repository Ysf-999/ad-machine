# Market Pressure Context

## Core Thesis

Copy is not written. It is engineered from market pressure: what the buyer already believes, what they hate in the market, what proof they trust, what they fear will happen if they wait, and what belief must change before they click.

The goal is not clever copy. The goal is profitable belief movement.

## Operating System

Use this loop for every product:

1. Market Signal
2. Buyer Pressure
3. Awareness State
4. Belief Shift
5. Mechanism Weapon
6. Proof Stack
7. Creative Asset
8. DR Copy-Chief Pass
9. Validation
10. Scorecard
11. Spend Feedback

This is a loop, not a one-way writing process. The ad account decides what survives.

## Creative Hierarchy

The system has one hierarchy:

1. Make the market feel understood.
2. Move one buying belief.
3. Make the product feel like the obvious next move.
4. Use proof and specificity.
5. Keep the factual floor.
6. Validate after the copy is written.

Constraints are not the voice of the ad. They are the guardrails after the direct-response idea exists.

Do not let constraints turn the copy into generic wellness language, legal filler, timid hedging, or "brand-safe" mush. A constraint should change the wording of a risky claim, not remove the pressure, enemy, contrast, urgency, or belief shift.

## 1. Market Signal

Extract what the market is already saying from reviews, Reddit, TikTok comments, forums, competitor pages, comments, support tickets, refund reasons, and ad comments.

Separate:

- behavior from opinion
- repeated pains from isolated complaints
- exact language from strategist inference
- credible proof from promotional noise

## 2. Buyer Pressure

Find the emotional or commercial pressure forcing action now:

- shame
- fear
- guilt
- wasted money
- social comparison
- identity threat
- time pressure
- symptom escalation
- convenience pain
- price shock
- feeling ignored by the market

Pressure is what turns passive interest into a click.

## 3. Awareness State

Use awareness to decide copy length and directness:

- Unaware: lead with editorial curiosity, visible symptom, or familiar moment.
- Problem-aware: intensify the problem and name the hidden cause.
- Solution-aware: attack failed alternatives and reveal the better mechanism.
- Product-aware: prove why this product is the better choice.
- Most-aware: lead with offer, price, guarantee, urgency, and friction removal.

## 4. Belief Shift

Every ad must move one belief.

Format:

```text
Current belief:
New belief:
What must be proven:
What inhibition must be removed:
```

If an ad tries to move five beliefs, it usually moves none.

## 5. Mechanism Weapon

The mechanism is the buyer's reason to believe this is not another failed option.

Mechanism types:

- ingredient mechanism
- delivery mechanism
- format mechanism
- process mechanism
- access mechanism
- price mechanism
- personalization mechanism
- diagnostic mechanism
- proof mechanism
- routine/friction-removal mechanism

The best mechanism explains why the product is the logical next move.

## 6. Proof Stack

Use proof in layers:

- observed customer language
- testimonials and reviews
- demos
- authority
- study or ingredient evidence
- reason-why
- contrast against failed alternatives
- guarantee or risk reversal
- price/value comparison
- specificity

Never let a big claim stand alone.

## 7. Creative Asset

Choose the asset based on awareness and pressure:

- native ad: curiosity, trigger event, emotional recognition
- long-form static: belief change, mechanism, proof stack
- UGC frame: objection handling, demonstration, social proof
- advertorial bridge: education and mechanism expansion
- BOF static: offer, proof, price, urgency

The creative must be easy to visualize before it is easy to write.

## 8. DR Copy-Chief Pass

Before validation, rewrite the asset once as a profit-responsible DR copy chief:

- strengthen the first line
- make the failed alternative more concrete
- make the cost of waiting visible
- remove filler and apologetic language
- replace generic benefits with buyer-specific moments
- move disclaimers and risk notes out of the selling copy unless the user or product category requires them in-copy
- keep factual integrity: do not invent proof, outcomes, authority, scarcity, testimonials, prices, or clinical claims

The copy should feel like a senior media buyer would actually test it.

## 9. Validation

Run `$copy-validator` after copy or prompt generation. Hard checks beat guessing.

Validation checks structure, copy length, CTA, prompt rules, product mentions, prices, and claim-sensitive language.

## 10. Scorecard

Run `$creative-scorecard` after validation.

The scorecard ranks:

- Signal Strength
- Pressure Intensity
- Belief Shift Clarity
- Mechanism Believability
- Proof Density
- Creative Visualizability
- Scale Potential

Use the score to decide launch order and revision priority.

## 11. Spend Feedback

Once performance data exists, use it to update the system:

- high CTR but low CVR: angle is interesting, offer/page may not pay off the promise
- low CTR: hook or visual pressure is weak
- high comments but low clicks: identity or controversy may be stronger than buying intent
- strong BOF but weak TOF: proof/offer is good, cold belief bridge is missing
- one winning angle: build a scaling spiral across hooks, visuals, formats, and awareness stages

Use `creative-ledger.csv` as the source of truth for tested creatives. Use `performance-feedback.md` to decide whether the next move is creative mutation, landing bridge repair, quiz friction repair, checkout/offer repair, or scaling.

## Quality Standard

Good output should explain:

- what the buyer believes now
- what they hate about current options
- which belief must change
- why this mechanism is credible
- what proof is needed
- what creative asset should be built
- what validator and scorecard results say
- what to test first

Weak output is generic persona language, generic hooks, fake proof, unsupported claims, or creative that cannot become a testable asset.
