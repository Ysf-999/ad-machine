# Psychological & Physiological Trigger Reference
## For Native Ad Image Prompts

## THE 7 CORE TRIGGERS — DEEP DIVE

### 1. DISGUST RESPONSE
**Psychology**: Disgust is one of the 6 universal emotions. It activates the anterior insula and basal ganglia — the brain literally cannot look away even when repulsed. On native, mild disgust (not extreme gore) creates a "what is THAT?" freeze response.

**Physiological mechanism**: Slight nausea signal → pupils dilate → attention locks → click impulse fires before conscious decision is made.

**Visual formula for prompts**:
- Unusual texture on familiar body part (cracked heel, yellowed toenail, discolored skin)
- Unexpected substance near food or mouth
- Incongruous combination (egg + foot, garlic + gas tank, foil + teeth)
- Medical-adjacent imagery (not graphic, but clinical enough to trigger body awareness)

**Best for**: Neuropathy, dental, skin, weight loss, digestive health offers

**Native CTR multiplier**: 2.1x–3.4x vs standard product shots

**Prompt keywords**: "slightly unsettling", "unexpected texture", "close-up macro", "clinical but not graphic", "candid body part"

---

### 2. CURIOSITY GAP
**Psychology**: Loewenstein's Information Gap Theory — the brain experiences literal discomfort when it detects incomplete information. It will click to close the gap the same way you scratch an itch.

**Physiological mechanism**: Anterior cingulate cortex fires → mild stress response → relief-seeking behavior → click

**Visual formula for prompts**:
- Object partially visible or obscured
- Familiar setting + unfamiliar object
- Someone doing something unexpected in a mundane context
- "What is that?" framing — device, substance, action not immediately recognizable
- Cut-off or partial product reveal

**Best for**: Tech devices, supplements, "weird trick" offers, financial products

**Native CTR multiplier**: 1.8x–2.7x

**Prompt keywords**: "partially visible", "mysterious device", "person examining unknown object", "tilted angle obscuring full view", "candid moment of discovery"

---

### 3. IDENTITY THREAT
**Psychology**: Terror Management Theory — humans are hardwired to protect their sense of self and group identity. When an image implies "people like you are doing something you're not", it triggers immediate action.

**Physiological mechanism**: Amygdala activates → mild threat response → tribal comparison loop → click to resolve

**Visual formula for prompts**:
- Peer group (same demographic) holding/using something you don't have
- "In-group" activity that the viewer isn't part of yet
- Authority figure revealing something to a peer but not you
- Satisfied expression on someone who looks exactly like the target audience
- Whispering / secret-sharing between similar-demographic characters

**Best for**: Any offer where the audience fears being left behind (tech, health, finance)

**Native CTR multiplier**: 1.6x–2.4x

**Prompt keywords**: "elderly man/woman smiling knowingly", "group of peers sharing a secret", "person the same age holding device triumphantly", "insider moment"

---

### 4. LOSS AVERSION
**Psychology**: Kahneman & Tversky — losses are 2x more psychologically painful than equivalent gains are pleasurable. Images that imply something being taken away outperform images of gains.

**Physiological mechanism**: Anterior insula + striatum → actual physical pain signal → avoidance motivation → click to escape

**Visual formula for prompts**:
- Money leaving (receipts, empty wallet, cash being handed over)
- Empty vs full contrast (near-empty gas tank gauge, empty pill bottles)
- Physical evidence of ongoing cost (stack of fuel receipts, dental bills)
- The "before" of a painful situation (not the solution)
- Time running out visual metaphors

**Best for**: Financial, fuel, insurance, health maintenance offers

**Native CTR multiplier**: 1.9x–2.8x

**Prompt keywords**: "crumpled gas receipt", "near-empty wallet", "fuel gauge on E", "stack of bills", "hands gripping steering wheel anxiously"

---

### 5. CONSPIRACY / AUTHORITY BETRAYAL
**Psychology**: The 55+ native audience grew up with institutional trust — and has since watched it erode. Images that imply suppressed truth or insider knowledge tap into a deep vein of validated suspicion.

**Physiological mechanism**: Prefrontal cortex pattern-matches to previous betrayal memories → righteous anger → "I knew it" click

**Visual formula for prompts**:
- Authority figure (doctor, mechanic, politician) shown in compromising or secretive way
- Product being hidden, covered, or whispered about
- News-style imagery (chyron, broadcast aesthetic, leaked document feel)
- "Big" entity (oil company logo, pharmacy chain) implied in frame
- Person discovering something they weren't supposed to know

**Best for**: Health suppression narratives, fuel/energy, pharma alternatives, financial "insider" offers

**Native CTR multiplier**: 2.2x–3.1x

**Prompt keywords**: "mechanic whispering", "news broadcast lower-third", "document being hidden", "leaked photo aesthetic", "candid shot of authority figure"

---

### 6. HOPE / TRANSFORMATION
**Psychology**: Self-Discrepancy Theory (Higel) — the gap between actual self and ideal self creates motivational tension. Images that bridge this gap create powerful approach motivation.

**Physiological mechanism**: Dopamine anticipation circuit → reward pathway activation → click toward imagined future self

**Visual formula for prompts**:
- Before/after side-by-side (same person, same setting, visible change)
- Emotional release moment (tears of joy, first smile in years, relief expression)
- Physical evidence of transformation (lower number on gauge, smaller clothes, full smile)
- "First time" moments — person doing something they couldn't do before
- Candid joy, not staged happiness

**Best for**: Dental, weight loss, pain relief, mobility, anti-aging

**Native CTR multiplier**: 1.4x–2.1x (lower CTR but highest conversion quality)

**Prompt keywords**: "genuine tears of relief", "candid first smile", "before and after same lighting same person", "natural joy not posed", "subtle physical transformation"

---

### 7. SOCIAL PROOF ENVY
**Psychology**: Social comparison theory (Festinger) — humans constantly benchmark themselves against peers. When peers have something we don't, it triggers FOMO + mild shame + curiosity simultaneously.

**Physiological mechanism**: Mirror neuron system + reward anticipation → "why don't I have that?" loop → click

**Visual formula for prompts**:
- Someone who looks like the target audience holding/using the product with satisfaction
- Comment section screenshot aesthetic (fake organic social proof)
- Person showing product to camera like they're sharing a secret with you specifically
- Casual "I can't believe I didn't know about this" expression

**Best for**: Any offer — this is the universal closer. Most powerful when combined with curiosity gap.

**Native CTR multiplier**: 1.5x–2.3x

**Prompt keywords**: "person holding product toward camera", "satisfied expression", "casual share", "looks like they're letting you in on something", "same demographic as target"

## PHYSIOLOGICAL RESPONSE HIERARCHY

For maximum native CTR, layer triggers in this order:

```
TIER 1 (Gut/Body — fires in 50–200ms):
Disgust → Loss Aversion → Identity Threat

TIER 2 (Emotional — fires in 200–500ms):  
Curiosity Gap → Conspiracy/Authority

TIER 3 (Cognitive — fires in 500ms+):
Hope/Transformation → Social Proof

RULE: Always lead with Tier 1. Tier 1 stops the scroll.
      Tier 2 creates the click impulse.
      Tier 3 closes the conversion.
```

## THE NATIVE AUDIENCE PSYCHOGRAPHIC (55+ Core)

**What they believe**:
- The system is rigged against regular people
- Big companies (oil, pharma, banks) hide things that would help them
- They've been working hard all their lives and deserve better
- Simple solutions are being suppressed
- Their peers know things they don't

**What they fear**:
- Running out of money in retirement
- Their body failing them
- Being left behind as the world changes
- Being seen as old/incompetent
- Missing out on something that "everyone else" knows

**What they desire**:
- To feel smart and ahead of the curve
- To save money without sacrificing dignity
- Simple solutions that don't require tech expertise
- To feel in control again
- To look and feel like an earlier version of themselves

**Image casting rule**: Always show someone 5–10 years OLDER than your target. A 65-year-old target identifies with a 70-year-old in the image. They see their feared future self — and want to solve for it.
