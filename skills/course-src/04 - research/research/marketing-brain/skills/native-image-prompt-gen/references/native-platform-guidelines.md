# Native Platform Creative Guidelines
## By Network — What Works, What Gets Rejected

---

## TABOOLA

**Audience**: 45–75, news readers, high household income, US/UK/AU/CA
**Mindset**: "I'm taking a break from real content"
**CTR benchmark**: 0.1%–0.4% healthy range

### What Works:
- News-adjacent imagery (looks like it belongs on CNN)
- Before/after facial transformations
- Hands holding devices/products at real locations
- Emotional faces (surprise, relief, concern)
- "Forbidden knowledge" aesthetic
- sometimes Exposed skin below collarbone works


### Editorial Style Rule:
Image should look like it could be a photo accompanying a real news article. Ask: "Would CNN use this photo in a real story?" If yes, it likely passes.

---

## OUTBRAIN

**Audience**: 35–65, lifestyle/editorial readers, slightly younger than Taboola
**Mindset**: "I'm reading something interesting"
**CTR benchmark**: 0.08%–0.35%

### What Works:
- Lifestyle photography aesthetic
- Product in natural context
- Slightly higher production quality than Taboola
- Testimonial-style photos


### Editorial Style Rule:
Should look like it belongs on Bon Appétit, Popular Mechanics, or AARP Magazine editorial spreads.

---

## MGID

**Audience**: 30–60, international (EU, LATAM, Eastern Europe heavy)
**Mindset**: Varies heavily by publisher vertical
**CTR benchmark**: 0.15%–0.6% (higher variance)

### What Works:
- More aggressive imagery than US networks
- Pattern interrupt works stronger here
- Celebrity adjacent (not real celebrities — lookalikes)
- Stronger "weird trick" imagery acceptable



### Editorial Style Rule:
More tabloid-adjacent. Think Daily Mail sidebar energy.

---

## REVCONTENT

**Audience**: 45–75, similar to Taboola but more conservative publishers
**Mindset**: Reading conservative news content
**CTR benchmark**: 0.1%–0.5%

### What Works:
- Authority imagery (doctors, mechanics, experts)
- American flag / patriotic aesthetic for US offers
- "Government doesn't want you to know" angles
- Rural/suburban settings
- Pickup trucks, garages, farmhouses

## UNIVERSAL CREATIVE RULES ACROSS ALL PLATFORMS

### The 3-Second Rule:
Your image must communicate ONE clear idea in 3 seconds at thumbnail size (150×150px on mobile). Test by shrinking your image to 150px wide — can you still understand the concept?

### The Squint Test:
Squint until your image blurs. Is there still a focal point? Is there contrast? If the image disappears into grey mush when squinted, CTR will be low.

### The "Scroll Speed" Test:
Native users scroll at roughly 300ms per content unit. Your image competes with actual editorial content. Ask: does this image create a visual interruption strong enough to break that rhythm?

### Platform Safety Tiers by Aggressiveness:

```
CONSERVATIVE (Taboola Premium, MSN, Yahoo):
→ Tier 2 + Tier 3 images only
→ No disgust triggers
→ No conspiracy aesthetic
→ Product in natural context preferred

MODERATE (Standard Taboola/Outbrain, lifestyle publishers):
→ All tiers acceptable
→ Mild disgust triggers OK
→ Curiosity gap strongest performer

AGGRESSIVE (MGID, Revcontent, smaller networks):
→ All tiers including heavy pattern interrupt
→ Conspiracy/authority betrayal works well
→ Stronger emotion OK
```

## COMPLIANCE CHECKLIST BEFORE LAUNCH

Before submitting any native image creative, verify:

- [ ] No text overlaid on image (headline goes in separate field)
- [ ] No brand logos visible in image
- [ ] No before/after with exposed body parts (waist up only if face shown)
- [ ] No fake urgency visual elements (countdown timers, red alert banners)
- [ ] No real political figures or celebrities
- [ ] Image makes sense at 150×150px
- [ ] Single clear focal point
- [ ] Authentic/UGC aesthetic (not stock photo look)
- [ ] Image aligns with landing page content (traffic quality protection)
- [ ] No misleading visual claims (showing miracle transformation that product can't deliver)
