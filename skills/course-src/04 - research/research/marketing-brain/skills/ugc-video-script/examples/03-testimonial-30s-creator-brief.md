### Do's

- Improvise within the directions. The whole point of `creator_brief` is your delivery, not a memorized read.
- Keep it in bed or in your real bedroom. Studio lighting kills this format.
- Show the actual label on the bottle during the mechanism beat. The form-of-magnesium argument needs visual proof.
- Talk about your own outcome only. Specific number of nights, your own tracker, your own pattern.

### Don'ts

- Don't read the hook line word-for-word. Say it the way you'd say it to a friend who asked why you look rested.
- Don't claim a specific percentage of users sleep better. We don't have a trial figure for this product — keep it personal.
- Don't say "doctor recommended" or "clinically proven." Neither phrase is documented for Harborline.
- Don't film in a kitchen, gym, or studio. The bedroom is the demonstration.

### Launch Notes

- Both Proof and Objection beats are tagged `[source: original — needs review]`. The creator's outcome must be their actual experience — confirm the creator has used the product for at least 14 nights before filming.
- The "first week sleeping through the night in three years" hook framing must match the creator's lived timeline. Do not put that line in the brief if the creator's actual experience differs.
- Sleep tracker screen must be the creator's own data. Do not use a stock screenshot.
- The 90-day guarantee phrase must be confirmed against current offer terms before the cut goes live.
- Substantiation file is light for this script because it relies on first-person testimonial. Keep the personal-outcome framing consistent on the landing page — no aggregated "X% of users" claim should appear alongside this creative.

## Strategy Notes

**Awareness Stage:** problem-aware — buyer recognizes the night-waking pattern, has tried 1-2 over-the-counter sleep aids. 30s is right because testimonial doesn't need a long mechanism arc; it leans on the personal proof.
**Market Sophistication:** Stage 4 — buyer has tried melatonin and sleep teas. The script opens with a failed-alternative confession to bypass another "sleep better" promise.
**Buyer Belief Shift:** "I've tried everything for sleep, nothing works long-term" -> "I was using the wrong form of magnesium — glycinate is the one that actually absorbs."
**Market Signal:** VOC pulled from sleep-supplement reviews and r/Sleep — recurring "wake up at 3am every night" pattern, melatonin-tolerance complaints across competitor brands.
**Reference Control Pattern:** testimonial-confession for the opening arc, before-after-timeline for the personal-outcome beat. Adapted to creator_brief delivery, not copied.
**Schwartz Lever:** sophistication stage 4 — failed-alternative + form-of-active reframe, not a louder sleep claim.
**Halbert Lever:** A-pile credibility through peer voice. AIDA opening with confession grabber. Risk reversal in the CTA via the 90-day guarantee.
**Kennedy Lever:** reason-why anchored on the glycinate form, plus first-person specificity (nights-in-a-row count) over generic outcome language.
**Cialdini Lever:** social proof via peer testimonial format itself; contrast via the competitor-magnesium cutaway; liking via low-fi bedroom intimacy.
**Wundt Inhibition:** removes the "I already tried magnesium" objection by naming the form distinction inside the Objection beat rather than adding new excitation.
**Copy-Chief Pass:** Removed any "guaranteed to work" phrasing from the CTA direction. Moved aggregate-outcome language to Launch Notes (creator stays first-person only). Tightened mechanism direction from "explain magnesium" to "name the glycinate form and why it absorbs differently." Hook delivered in own words, not read.
