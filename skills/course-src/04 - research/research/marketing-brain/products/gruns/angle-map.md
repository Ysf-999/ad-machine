# Angle Map

Research date: 2026-05-03

## Strategic Direction

Current sale-price UGC likely wins because it talks to product-aware or deal-aware buyers. To capture more TOF traffic, shift the first three seconds from "discounted gummies" to the deeper cold-market pressure:

"I keep failing daily nutrition routines, and I am tired of feeling guilty about it."

The winning cold path should make Gruns feel like a behavior-design solution first and a discounted product second. Sale price still belongs near the close, in retargeting, or after the mechanism has created desire.

## Reference Control Selection

Control-library patterns selected as structural references:

1. `pov-skit`
   - Fit: Cold traffic with private frustration and self-blame.
   - Borrowed movement: Lived micro-moment -> absolution -> new routine.
   - Visual grammar: Cabinet, shaker cup, morning counter, travel bag, desk drawer.
   - Do not copy: Any exact skit premise or unrelated product claim.

2. `comparison-contrast`
   - Fit: Powders/pills versus gummy daily pack.
   - Borrowed movement: Old way looks obviously too high-friction -> new way feels easier.
   - Visual grammar: Powder mess, pills, bottles, then clean pack.
   - Do not copy: Competitor attacks or unsupported superiority claims.

3. `dialogue-skeptic`
   - Fit: "Isn't this just candy?" is the buyer's real objection.
   - Borrowed movement: Skeptic voices the block -> answerer shows proof and mechanism.
   - Visual grammar: Friend/founder/creator conversation, split-screen, comment reply.
   - Do not copy: Fake credentials, fake consumer dialogue, or invented proof.

Use `authority-expert` only when credentials and approved clinical language are supplied.

## Angle 1: Powder Graveyard

- Archetype: Powder Quitter.
- Awareness stage: Solution-aware.
- Core belief shift: "I failed greens powders" -> "The routine failed me; a lower-friction format may be the real upgrade."
- Hook territory: Failed alternative contrast.
- Mechanism: Gummies and daily packs remove the sensory/prep barriers that make powders easy to quit.
- Proof needed: Product demo, no-mix format, ingredient/testing proof, reviews from former powder users.
- Objection handled: "Will I quit this too?"
- Example hooks:
  - "If your greens powder is still sitting in the cabinet, this ad is for you."
  - "The problem with my greens routine was never the greens. It was the routine."
  - "I did not need a new powder. I needed a version I would actually take."
- Recommended format: POV skit, comparison static, creator kitchen demo.
- Risk notes: Do not claim powders are unhealthy or inferior for everyone.

## Angle 2: The Habit You Actually Keep

- Archetype: Minimum Viable Health Buyer.
- Awareness stage: Unaware / problem-aware.
- Core belief shift: "I need more discipline" -> "I need a healthier routine with less friction."
- Hook territory: Identity-safe absolution.
- Mechanism: Daily pack turns nutrition support into a repeatable snack-like cue.
- Proof needed: Reviews, daily pack demo, guarantee, serious proof layer.
- Objection handled: Self-blame and routine fatigue.
- Example hooks:
  - "The healthiest supplement is the one you actually take."
  - "I stopped buying healthier intentions and started buying lower-friction routines."
  - "If you know what to do but never do it daily, start here."
- Recommended format: UGC confession, native notes static, morning-routine demo.
- Risk notes: Keep promise to habit adherence, not guaranteed health outcomes.

## Angle 3: Skeptic's Gummy Audit

- Archetype: Skeptical Supplement Adult.
- Awareness stage: Solution-aware / product-aware.
- Core belief shift: "A gummy is probably candy" -> "This one deserves a closer look because the proof is visible."
- Hook territory: Skeptic-first proof.
- Mechanism: Testing, ingredient panel, retail proof, science page, review footprint.
- Proof needed: Exact claims and screenshots from approved pages.
- Objection handled: "Is this just expensive candy?"
- Example hooks:
  - "Before you call these candy, check the label."
  - "A superfood gummy sounds too easy, so I looked for the proof first."
  - "The 4 things I checked before taking Gruns seriously."
- Recommended format: Dialogue-skeptic, screen recording, checklist static.
- Risk notes: Do not overstate clinical results or imply FDA approval.

## Angle 4: Nutrition Gaps Without Wellness Homework

- Archetype: Minimum Viable Health Buyer.
- Awareness stage: Problem-aware.
- Core belief shift: "I need to overhaul my diet" -> "I can add one backup routine that fits real life."
- Hook territory: No-lifestyle-overhaul.
- Mechanism: Broad daily nutrition support in a low-friction pack.
- Proof needed: Nutrition-gap context, ingredient facts, "not a replacement for food" framing.
- Objection handled: "Why not just eat vegetables?"
- Example hooks:
  - "For the days your diet is more 'busy adult' than wellness influencer."
  - "You do not need a perfect morning routine to cover more basics."
  - "This is not a replacement for eating well. It is backup for real life."
- Recommended format: Native static, day-in-the-life UGC, editorial advertorial.
- Risk notes: Avoid scolding, body-shame, or replacement-for-food claims.

## Angle 5: Supplement Cabinet Cleanup

- Archetype: Stack Simplifier.
- Awareness stage: Solution-aware.
- Core belief shift: "I need more products" -> "I need fewer decisions and a clearer daily pack."
- Hook territory: Clutter relief and value.
- Mechanism: Broad ingredient pack simplifies the routine.

## Angle 6: Why Eight Gummies?

- Archetype: Skeptical Supplement Adult.
- Awareness stage: Product-aware.
- Core belief shift: "Eight gummies is too many" -> "The serving size is part of the product's breadth and ritual."
- Hook territory: Objection reversal.
- Mechanism: Serving-size explanation plus sensory compliance.
- Proof needed: Product label, serving facts, low-sugar/sugar-free options.
- Objection handled: Serving size, sugar, dose skepticism.
- Example hooks:
  - "Yes, it is eight gummies. Here is why that actually made me trust it more."
  - "The serving size was the first thing I questioned."
  - "If one tiny gummy promises everything, I get suspicious too."
- Recommended format: Hand demo, label close-up, dialogue-skeptic.
- Risk notes: Make sure nutrition label and serving size are current.

## Angle 7: GLP-1 Nutrition Backup

- Archetype: GLP-1 Nutrition Support Buyer.
- Awareness stage: Problem-aware / solution-aware.
- Core belief shift: "Eating less means my routine is simpler" -> "Eating less makes the nutrition routine more important and it should get easier."
- Hook territory: New life context.
- Mechanism: Low-friction nutrition/digestion support pack.
- Proof needed: GLP-1-specific page language, nutrition panel, compliance review.
- Objection handled: "I am already doing enough" and "is this medical?"
- Example hooks:
  - "When your appetite changes, your nutrition routine has to get easier, not harder."
  - "Eating less does not mean your body needs less support."
  - "The GLP-1 routine nobody thinks about until meals get smaller."
- Recommended format: Educational static, creator desk/kitchen UGC, advertorial bridge.
- Risk notes: Do not claim weight loss, medication results, constipation treatment, or provider recommendation without proof.

## Angle 8: Retail Trial Proof

- Archetype: Skeptical Supplement Adult / Minimum Viable Health Buyer.
- Awareness stage: Product-aware.
- Core belief shift: "This is a random DTC product" -> "This is visible enough to inspect and try."
- Hook territory: Seen-in-retail trust.
- Mechanism: Retail availability lowers risk and creates social proof.
- Proof needed: Current Target/Ulta listing screenshots and permissions if used visually.
- Objection handled: Scam/random-brand doubt.
- Example hooks:
  - "I saw Gruns at Target, then finally looked up why people were buying them."
  - "The retail pack is how I tested the habit before committing."
  - "Not every wellness gummy makes it onto a Target shelf."
- Recommended format: Retail haul UGC, unboxing, review overlay.
- Risk notes: Follow retailer trademark and ad policy rules.

## Angle 9: Family No-Fight Daily Pack

- Archetype: Family Gatekeeper.
- Awareness stage: Problem-aware.

- Hook territory: Parent guilt relief.

## Angle 10: Sale Price With Belief Bridge

- Archetype: Product-aware / deal-aware.
- Awareness stage: Product-aware / most-aware.
- Core belief shift: "I might buy because it is on sale" -> "I should buy now because this solves a routine I keep failing."
- Hook territory: Price close after mechanism.
- Mechanism: Sale lowers trial risk after the ad builds belief.
- Proof needed: Current active sale, terms, guarantee.
- Objection handled: Price and procrastination.
- Example hooks:
  - "The sale got my attention. The routine is why I kept using it."
  - "Before you buy Gruns for the discount, ask if this is the routine you will actually keep."
  - "The first-order deal only matters if the habit survives week two."
- Recommended format: Retargeting UGC, testimonial, offer static.
- Risk notes: Do not make TOF dependent on discount if scaling goal is colder demand.

## 2026 Format Recommendations

### Format 1: Powder Graveyard POV

Looks like:

- Camera opens on a half-used tub, dirty shaker, and ignored vitamin bottle.
- Creator admits the routine failed.
- Cut to Gruns pack as lower-friction mechanism.

Why it can win:

- Visual, relatable, not price-led.
- Turns failed alternatives into demand.

### Format 2: Skeptic Checklist Static

Looks like:

- iPhone Notes or clean checklist:
  - Is it tested?
  - What's the serving size?
  - What is the sugar option?
  - Is there a guarantee?
  - Can I try before a full commitment?

Why it can win:

- Makes the buyer feel smart, not sold.

### Format 3: Old Routine vs New Routine

Looks like:

- Left: powder, pills, blender, clutter.
- Right: daily pack in bag/desk.

Why it can win:

- Mechanism is visible without over-claiming.

### Format 4: "Actually Take It" UGC

Looks like:

- Creator avoids big health claims.
- Focuses on habit: "I kept this one."

Why it can win:

- Strong belief shift with lower compliance risk.

### Format 5: GLP-1 Support Advertorial Static

Looks like:

- Educational, not hype:
  - "Eating less changes the daily nutrition problem."
  - "Ask your provider what support routine fits you."
  - "Here is why people look at a simpler daily pack."

Why it can win:

- High-intent lane with distinct pressure.

## Strategy Notes

**Schwartz Lever:** Stage 4-5 sophistication. Do not escalate generic greens claims. Lead with mechanism and identification: the buyer who quits healthy routines needs a different format, not another lecture.

**Halbert Lever:** Starving crowd is not "people who like gummies." It is people with recent failed health-routine behavior: abandoned powders, messy shaker cups, supplement clutter, GLP-1 appetite changes, or parent routine friction.

**Kennedy Lever:** Apart-Above-Alone through category refusal. Gruns should look apart from powders and ordinary gummy supplements by owning routine compliance and proof-aware skepticism.

**Cialdini Lever:** Contrast and social proof. Contrast old routine vs new pack first, then use retail/review/science proof near the decision point.

**Wundt Lever:** Excitation comes from guilt relief and "I might finally keep this." Inhibition comes from "expensive candy," subscription fear, and overclaims. Ads must raise desire and remove doubt in the same asset.

## DR Copy-Chief Pass

- Make the failed alternative concrete in the first frame: tub, shaker, bottle, travel bag, desk drawer.
- Do not let the product appear before the buyer's private frustration is named.
- Use the sale as risk reduction after the mechanism, not as the cold-market idea.
- Replace generic benefit stacks with one belief shift per asset.
- Keep "gummy" tension alive. The ad should not hide the objection; it should beat it with proof.
- Keep GLP-1 creative in a separate lane and avoid weight-loss or treatment implication.

## Launch Risks

- If sale price remains the first-frame hook, the account may scale only in product-aware pockets.
- If ads overclaim digestion, energy, immunity, GLP-1, or clinical outcomes, compliance risk rises.
- If subscription terms stay vague, reviews/objections may depress CVR.
- If the product is framed as a vegetable replacement, the ad invites criticism.
- If the creative looks too polished, it may trigger wellness-gummy skepticism.
