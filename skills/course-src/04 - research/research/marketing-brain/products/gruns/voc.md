# Voice Of Customer

Research date: 2026-05-03

## Top Market Pains

1. I keep buying healthy routines and abandoning them.
2. Greens powders taste, smell, mix, or feel bad enough that I stop using them.
3. I know my diet has gaps, but I do not want another wellness lecture.
4. My supplement cabinet is too complicated.
5. A gummy supplement sounds convenient but suspicious.
6. Subscription supplements can feel like a trap.
7. Price feels high if the buyer compares Gruns to a basic multivitamin instead of a full routine.
8. GLP-1 users or reduced-appetite buyers worry about nutrition, fiber, digestion, and staying regular.
9. Parents want healthier routines that do not create daily fights.

## Top Desired Transformations

- "I finally do the healthy thing daily."
- Fewer bottles, scoops, powders, and decisions.
- A morning routine that feels easy instead of aspirational.
- A travel/workday pack that does not require prep.
- Feeling nutritionally covered without becoming a wellness maximalist.
- A greens routine that does not taste like greens powder.
- More confidence that a gummy can still be serious.
- A family wellness habit people actually accept.

## Pain Ladder

### Theme: "I keep quitting the routines I buy."

- Surface pain: I forget supplements or stop mixing powders.
- Practical cost: Wasted money, expired tubs, clutter.
- Emotional/social cost: Guilt and annoyance every time I see the unused product.
- Identity/status fear: "Maybe I am just not disciplined enough to be healthy."

### Theme: "Greens powders feel like health punishment."

- Surface pain: Taste, texture, smell, shaker cleanup.
- Practical cost: Extra prep and mess in the morning.
- Emotional/social cost: Resentment that health has to feel unpleasant.
- Identity/status fear: "I cannot keep up with the wellness-person version of myself."

### Theme: "A gummy feels too easy to trust."

- Surface pain: Gummies look like candy.
- Practical cost: Fear of paying premium prices for low-dose ingredients.
- Emotional/social cost: Feeling foolish for being sold wellness candy.
- Identity/status fear: "I am a smart buyer, not someone fooled by influencer supplements."

### Theme: "My health basics are scattered everywhere."

- Surface pain: Too many bottles, powders, reminders, and apps.
- Practical cost: Reorder fatigue and skipped doses.
- Emotional/social cost: The routine feels like one more job.
- Identity/status fear: "I do not have my life together."

### Theme: "GLP-1 changed my appetite, but not my body's needs."

- Surface pain: Eating less, constipation/fiber concerns, smaller meals.
- Practical cost: Harder to get enough nutrients or routine foods.
- Emotional/social cost: Anxiety about doing the medication lifestyle wrong.
- Identity/status fear: "I am improving one thing but neglecting another."

## Quote - Evidence - Implication

1. Theme: Routine failure beats nutrient education.
   - Evidence: Reviews and category comments repeatedly frame value around ease, taste, and daily use.
   - Implication: Lead TOF with the moment of abandoning powders or bottles, then introduce Gruns as the routine design.

2. Theme: Gummy format is both desire and doubt.
   - Evidence: Buyers like convenience but question seriousness, dose, sugar, and price.
   - Implication: The best ads should voice skepticism first instead of pretending it does not exist.

3. Theme: Price-led UGC works because the buyer is already close.
   - Evidence: User reports strong ROAS from sale-price UGC.
   - Implication: Keep sale as a closer, but make cold ads about failed routines and proof.

4. Theme: Retail validation matters.
   - Evidence: Target and Ulta listings create public purchase context.
   - Implication: Use retail proof as a trust layer after the hook, especially in skeptic ads.

5. Theme: GLP-1 is a strong but sensitive expansion lane.
   - Evidence: GLP-1 communities discuss eating less, fiber, constipation, and nutrition support.
   - Implication: Build a separate compliant lane around nutrition support, not weight loss or medication results.
