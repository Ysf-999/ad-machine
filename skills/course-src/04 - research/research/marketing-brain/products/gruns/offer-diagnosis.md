# Offer Diagnosis

Research date: 2026-05-03

## Scale Thesis

Gruns can plausibly scale beyond sale-led UGC because the product sits on a large, durable mass desire: feel healthier and more in control without changing one's whole lifestyle. The stronger TOF thesis is not "superfood gummies are discounted." It is "this is the daily nutrition habit you might finally keep."

The offer's main scale advantage is friction removal. The offer's main scale risk is believability. Gummies are easy to want but also easy to dismiss as candy marketing. TOF creative should therefore run a two-step move:

1. Make the buyer feel understood in their failed routine.
2. Immediately show why this gummy format is more serious than the buyer expected.

## Main Desire To Channel

"I want to be the kind of person who covers the basics daily, but I need it to fit the life I actually live."

Do not lead with "you need more nutrients" in a preachy way. Lead with the buyer's lived admission:

- I bought the greens powder.
- I meant to use it.
- I hated the taste.
- I got tired of cleaning the shaker.
- I forgot it on trips.
- I still want the health upside.

## Functional Product Statement

Gruns is a daily nutrition adherence product disguised as a gummy pack. It lowers the friction between intention and action.

## Biggest Conversion Blocker

The buyer's skepticism is rational:

- Gummies are associated with candy.
- Wellness gummies are often overhyped.
- Ingredient lists can look impressive without proving meaningful doses.
- Subscription supplements can feel hard to cancel.
- The buyer has probably abandoned other routines already.

The copy must not bulldoze this skepticism. It should borrow it:

"If a greens gummy sounds too easy, that is exactly the point to inspect."

## Mechanism And Proof Gaps

Strong mechanisms:

- Format: gummies beat powder friction.
- Routine: daily packs beat bottle clutter.
- Sensory: taste makes repetition easier.
- Proof: testing/science/retail/reviews reduce gummy doubt.

Proof gaps to fill before launch:

- Active serving size, sugar/sugar alcohol, and ingredient fact panel screenshots.
- Current subscription terms and cancellation flow.
- Current first-order price and whether discounts are evergreen.
- Current clinical/science page wording that legal approves for ads.
- Current retail review count/rating screenshots if used.

## Offer Upgrades Ranked By Expected Impact

1. TOF pre-sell page: "The Daily Nutrition Habit Audit"
   - Why: Captures cold buyers before they care about Gruns or price.
   - Sections: powder graveyard, nutrition-gap guilt, gummy skepticism, proof, offer.

2. Subscription transparency block
   - Why: Trustpilot and review friction can turn into a buying objection.
   - Include: first-order price, renewal price, how to pause/cancel, guarantee basics, shipping cadence.

3. "Why eight gummies?" proof visual
   - Why: Serving size can become either an objection or a proof mechanism.
   - Show: ingredient breadth, pack ritual, taste/compliance reason, low/sugar-free option.

4. Powder quitter landing path
   - Why: Powders are the cleanest old-way/new-way contrast.
   - Avoid: claiming powders are bad. The enemy is friction, not powder users.

5. Skeptic's gummy audit
   - Why: Converts gummy doubt into engagement.
   - Needs: testing, clinical, retail, review, ingredient panel proof.

6. GLP-1 support path
   - Why: High-intent expanding market.
   - Guardrail: Avoid implying treatment, weight loss, medication outcomes, or medical necessity.

7. Family variant split
   - Why: Kids/family buyers need different proof and objections.
   - Guardrail: Do not mix adult and kids claims without separate compliance review.

## Claims To Avoid Unless Supplied And Approved

- "Treats bloating," "fixes IBS," "cures constipation," "heals gut," "resets microbiome."
- "Replaces vegetables" or "you do not need fruits and vegetables."
- "Clinically proven to improve energy/digestion/immunity" unless using the exact approved study claim.
- "Guaranteed results" beyond the refund policy.
- "Doctors recommend" unless specific approved source exists.
- "No side effects."
- "Weight loss" or GLP-1 outcome claims.
- "Cancels any time in seconds" unless proven in the active subscription flow.

## DR Copy-Chief Direction

The sharpest TOF line of attack:

"The supplement industry keeps selling discipline. Gruns sells a routine people can actually repeat."

That gives the campaign a bigger idea than price:

- Enemy: wellness routines that ask too much of normal people.
- Pressure: the private shame of failed health intentions.
- Mechanism: snackable daily pack.
- Proof: serious testing, ingredient breadth, retail/reviews.
- CTA: try the routine, not just buy discounted gummies.
