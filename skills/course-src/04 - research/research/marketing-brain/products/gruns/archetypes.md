# Archetypes

Research date: 2026-05-03

## Archetype 1: The Powder Quitter

Confidence: High

- Core desire: Get greens/superfood benefits without drinking something they dislike.
- Current belief: "Greens are probably good for me, but powders are annoying and I never stick with them."
- Hidden fear: "I keep wasting money because I cannot make myself do the healthy thing."
- Failed solutions: Greens powders, smoothies, multivitamins, health challenges.
- Main inhibition: "Will I quit this too?"
- Awareness stage: Solution-aware.
- Sophistication stage: Stage 4, mechanism elaboration. The market knows greens; the fresh mechanism is compliance through gummy packs.
- Proof needed: Taste, no prep, daily pack, ingredient/testing proof, reviews from powder quitters.
- Mechanism they will believe: Format/routine mechanism.
- Language markers: "chalky," "shaker," "forgot," "half-used tub," "tastes like grass."
- Behavioral evidence: Category behavior around abandoned powders and Gruns' own powder-alternative positioning.
- Trigger event: Sees unused powder or dreads mixing it again.
- Best first ad angle: Powder Graveyard / Habit You Keep.
- Worst angle to avoid: Ingredient-count lecture before naming the routine failure.
- Best channels/sources: Meta Reels, TikTok-style UGC, comparison statics, retail retargeting.
- Retargeting implication: Show reviews from former powder users, price/guarantee, and "first week routine" walkthrough.

## Archetype 2: The Minimum Viable Health Buyer

Confidence: High

- Core desire: Cover the basics without becoming a wellness maximalist.
- Current belief: "I know I should eat better and take care of myself, but my life is busy."
- Hidden fear: "I am neglecting my health in small ways every day."
- Failed solutions: Meal prep, perfect morning routines, grocery intentions, supplement reminders.
- Main inhibition: "Is this too simple to be real?"
- Awareness stage: Problem-aware / unaware.
- Sophistication stage: Stage 5 identification. Claims are less important than feeling seen.
- Proof needed: Practical daily-use visuals, no-dramatic-claims positioning, retail/review proof.
- Mechanism they will believe: Behavioral compliance mechanism.
- Language markers: "busy," "real life," "I know I should," "something easy," "cover the basics."
- Behavioral evidence: Broad wellness routines fail when they demand too much friction.
- Trigger event: A busy week, travel day, skipped meals, or renewed health intention.
- Best first ad angle: Nutrition Gaps Without Wellness Homework.
- Worst angle to avoid: Shame-heavy "you are unhealthy" framing.
- Best channels/sources: Broad Meta, native checklist statics, lifestyle UGC, family/parent variants.
- Retargeting implication: Use guarantee, first-order offer, and review proof after the identification hook.

## Archetype 3: The Skeptical Supplement Adult

Confidence: High

- Core desire: Find a convenient product without being fooled by supplement hype.
- Current belief: "A gummy supplement with big promises is probably candy marketing."
- Hidden fear: "I will look stupid for paying premium prices for weak gummies."
- Failed solutions: Influencer wellness products, vague gummies, underdosed supplements, overhyped powders.
- Main inhibition: Believability.
- Awareness stage: Solution-aware / product-aware.
- Sophistication stage: Stage 4-5. Mechanisms and claims are overworked; proof and skepticism must lead.
- Proof needed: Ingredient panel, third-party testing, science page, retail distribution, real review volume.
- Mechanism they will believe: Proof mechanism plus transparency.
- Language markers: "just candy," "worth it," "does it actually work," "expensive," "show me the label."
- Behavioral evidence: Public review and Reddit skepticism around gummies and subscriptions.
- Trigger event: Sees an ad and pauses because the format seems too convenient.
- Best first ad angle: Skeptic's Gummy Audit.
- Worst angle to avoid: Big benefit stack without proof.
- Best channels/sources: Screen-recording, notes/checklist statics, expert-coded explainers with no fake credentials.
- Retargeting implication: FAQ, testing proof, "why eight gummies," subscription clarity.

## Archetype 4: The Stack Simplifier

Confidence: Medium-high

- Core desire: Replace supplement clutter with one easier daily action.
- Current belief: "My health routine has become too complicated."
- Hidden fear: "I am spending money without really knowing what is helping."
- Failed solutions: Vitamin stack, probiotic, fiber, greens, collagen/beauty gummies, separate reminders.
- Main inhibition: "Will this really replace enough to justify the price?"
- Awareness stage: Solution-aware.
- Sophistication stage: Stage 3-4, mechanism and bundle comparison.
- Proof needed: Clear what Gruns includes and does not include, ingredient transparency, bundle/value comparison.
- Mechanism they will believe: Stack simplification and daily pack mechanism.
- Language markers: "too many bottles," "cabinet," "one pack," "simplify," "subscription."
- Behavioral evidence: Reviews and category behavior around replacement/simplification.
- Trigger event: Reordering or looking at supplement clutter.
- Best first ad angle: Supplement Cabinet Cleanup.
- Worst angle to avoid: Saying it replaces everything without proof.
- Best channels/sources: Long-form static, carousel, desk/countertop UGC.
- Retargeting implication: Comparison table and offer bundle clarity.

## Archetype 5: The GLP-1 Nutrition Support Buyer

Confidence: Medium

- Core desire: Make reduced-appetite life feel nutritionally safer and easier.
- Current belief: "I am eating less, so I need a simple support routine."
- Hidden fear: "I am solving weight/appetite but creating another health problem."
- Failed solutions: Fiber powder, protein-only focus, forced meals, complicated meal prep.
- Main inhibition: Medical/supplement claim skepticism.
- Awareness stage: Problem-aware / solution-aware.
- Sophistication stage: Stage 3-4, mechanism and safety/proof matter.
- Proof needed: Nutrition label, fiber/prebiotic/probiotic language, GLP-1 page claims, disclaimers, no medical guarantees.
- Mechanism they will believe: Nutrition support routine.
- Language markers: "eating less," "fiber," "constipation," "small meals," "GLP-1," "protein."
- Behavioral evidence: Public GLP-1 communities discuss nutrition, fiber, digestion, and reduced food intake.
- Trigger event: Starting medication, constipation, appetite change, smaller meals.
- Best first ad angle: GLP-1 Nutrition Backup.
- Worst angle to avoid: Weight loss, medication result, or disease-treatment claims.
- Best channels/sources: Separate campaign, compliant educational static/video, search/retargeting.
- Retargeting implication: FAQ and "support, not treatment" framing.

## Archetype 6: The Family Gatekeeper

Confidence: Medium

- Core desire: Create a healthier family routine without resistance.
- Current belief: "Healthy routines fail when everyone hates them."
- Hidden fear: "I am not doing enough for my kids/family."
- Failed solutions: Vegetables battles, vitamins kids forget, powders nobody wants, snack policing.
- Main inhibition: Sugar, safety, kid-specific proof, price.
- Awareness stage: Problem-aware.
- Sophistication stage: Stage 5 identification.
- Proof needed: Kids product facts, parent reviews, sugar/nutrition label, pediatric/legal approval if making kid-targeted claims.
- Mechanism they will believe: Taste-led family compliance.
- Language markers: "kids," "lunchbox," "they actually take it," "no fight."
- Behavioral evidence: Kids SKU and family review themes.
- Trigger event: Kid refuses healthy foods or parent resets household routines.
- Best first ad angle: The No-Fight Daily Pack.
- Worst angle to avoid: Adult claims mapped onto kids.
- Best channels/sources: Separate family creative, retail/trial pack, parent UGC.
- Retargeting implication: Kids-specific proof and subscription/price clarity.

## Channel Implications

- Broad Meta TOF: Powder Quitter, Minimum Viable Health Buyer, Skeptical Supplement Adult.
- Retargeting: Skeptic proof, subscription terms, guarantee, retail proof.
- Search/native advertorial: Stack Simplifier and GLP-1 Support Buyer.
- Family campaigns: Separate creative and claims for Kids Gruns.

## Decision-Maker / User / Payer Notes

- Adult Gruns: buyer, user, and payer are usually the same person.
- Kids Gruns: parent is buyer/payer; child is user and taste gatekeeper.
- GLP-1 support: buyer/user/payer are usually the same, but medical decision context can involve provider advice.
