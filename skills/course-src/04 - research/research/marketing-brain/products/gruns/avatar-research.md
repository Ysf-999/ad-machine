# Avatar Research

Research date: 2026-05-03

## Decision-First Research Brief

Decision to make:

Which buyer psychology should Gruns TOF ads target first to expand beyond sale-price UGC?

Options compared:

1. Powder Quitter: wants greens benefits but hates powders.
2. Minimum Viable Health Buyer: wants to cover basics without a full lifestyle overhaul.
3. Skeptical Supplement Adult: doubts gummies and wellness marketing.
4. Stack Simplifier: wants to replace bottles/powders with one routine.
5. GLP-1 Nutrition Support Buyer: eating less and worried about fiber/micronutrients/digestion.
6. Family Gatekeeper: wants a household routine that people actually accept.

Evidence that changes the decision:

- If price objections dominate high-intent sources, build price/value and subscription transparency first.
- If gummy skepticism is high-intensity, lead with proof-first skeptic formats.
- If powder failure language is vivid and repeatable, make powder quitters the first TOF lane.
- If GLP-1 language shows strong purchase intent, split into a separate compliant campaign.
- If family language requires different proof and creative, separate adult and kids campaigns.

Recommendation:

Lead broad TOF with Powder Quitter plus Minimum Viable Health Buyer. These are emotionally specific, visually obvious, and less claim-sensitive than disease or outcome-led health promises. Use Skeptical Supplement Adult as the proof layer inside those ads. Launch GLP-1 as a separate high-intent angle with careful constraints.

## Source Quality Notes

High credibility:

- Official Gruns pages for claims, product facts, guarantee, and offer details.
- FDA/FTC/NIH sources for claim limits.
- Retail listings for price and retail proof.

Medium credibility:

- Trustpilot and independent review sites for service friction and objection themes.
- Public Reddit threads for raw language.

Weak but useful:

- Influencer/affiliate reviews and isolated comments. Use for language only.

## Trigger Event Map Ranked By Creative Value

1. The buyer sees the half-used tub or supplement cabinet.
   - Awareness: Problem-aware.
   - Buyer pressure: guilt, waste, self-judgment.
   - Hook territory: "The healthiest supplement is not the one with the longest label. It is the one you actually take."
   - Why valuable: Visually obvious and emotionally true.

2. The buyer quits greens powder again.
   - Awareness: Solution-aware.
   - Buyer pressure: frustration and sensory rejection.
   - Hook territory: "If your greens powder is still sitting in the cabinet, the problem might be the routine."
   - Why valuable: Direct competitor contrast without needing aggressive claims.

3. The buyer wants a quick sale but is not sure a gummy is serious.
   - Awareness: Product-aware / solution-aware.
   - Buyer pressure: deal interest plus skepticism.
   - Hook territory: "I thought these were just expensive gummies until I checked the label."
   - Why valuable: Bridges current winning price ads into colder proof ads.

4. The buyer starts GLP-1 or eats much less.
   - Awareness: Problem/solution-aware.
   - Buyer pressure: nutrition and digestion concern.
   - Hook territory: "When your appetite changes, your nutrition routine has to get easier, not harder."
   - Why valuable: High buyer intent, but needs claim control.

5. The buyer travels, has a busy week, or restarts a health routine.
   - Awareness: Unaware/problem-aware.
   - Buyer pressure: convenience and identity repair.
   - Hook territory: "For the days your healthy routine leaves the house before you do."
   - Why valuable: Broad and native to UGC.

6. The buyer sees Gruns in Target or Ulta.
   - Awareness: Product-aware.
   - Buyer pressure: trial confidence.
   - Hook territory: "I saw these at Target and finally checked why they were everywhere."
   - Why valuable: Retail proof lowers risk.

## Pain Ladder By Archetype

### Powder Quitter

- Surface: Hates taste, smell, texture, and cleanup.
- Practical: Wastes money on tubs and misses days.
- Emotional: Feels annoyed that health is so unpleasant.
- Identity/status: "I am not a wellness person. I cannot keep this up."

### Minimum Viable Health Buyer

- Surface: Does not eat enough fruits/vegetables or remember supplements.
- Practical: Busy life creates inconsistent nutrition.
- Emotional: Feels behind on basic self-care.
- Identity/status: "I should have this figured out by now."

### Skeptical Supplement Adult

- Surface: Gummies sound unserious.
- Practical: Fear of wasting money on weak doses.
- Emotional: Resents being sold wellness hype.
- Identity/status: "I am not gullible."

### Stack Simplifier

- Surface: Too many bottles/powders.
- Practical: Decision fatigue and reordering.
- Emotional: Health feels cluttered and expensive.
- Identity/status: "My routine is out of control."

### GLP-1 Nutrition Support Buyer

- Surface: Eating less, constipation/fiber concern, smaller meals.
- Practical: Harder to cover protein, fiber, micronutrients, and regular meals.
- Emotional: Anxiety about doing the lifestyle wrong.
- Identity/status: "I am taking the medication seriously, but I need a simpler routine."

### Family Gatekeeper

- Surface: Kids/partner resist healthy routines.
- Practical: Daily battles and food waste.
- Emotional: Parent guilt.
- Identity/status: "I want to be the person who keeps the family healthy."

## Recommended Creative Decisions

### Hooks

- "If your greens powder is still sitting in the cabinet, this ad is for you."
- "The healthiest supplement is the one you actually take."
- "I did not need another wellness routine. I needed one with less friction."
- "A gummy that sounds too easy should be inspected, not ignored."
- "Before you call these candy, check the part most gummies do not show you."
- "If you are on GLP-1 and eating less, make the nutrition routine easier, not harder."
- "My supplement cabinet got simpler when I stopped buying routines I hated."

### Angles

- Powder graveyard.
- Habit you keep.
- Skeptic's gummy audit.
- Cabinet cleanup.
- Nutrition gaps without lifestyle overhaul.
- GLP-1 support routine.
- Retail trial proof.
- Family daily routine.

### Landing Page / Pre-Sell Sections

- The real reason greens powders fail.
- Why gummies can be a compliance mechanism, not just a flavor choice.
- What's inside the daily pack.
- Why eight gummies.
- Low sugar vs sugar-free.
- Subscription and guarantee transparency.
- Retail proof and third-party testing.
- GLP-1 support explanation with careful language.

### Retargeting

- Sale price and guarantee.
- Review/social proof.
- "Still wondering if gummies are serious?"
- "Powder vs gummies: the routine difference."
- "What happens after the first order?"
- "Subscription terms in plain English."

## Research Gaps And Weak-Evidence Warnings

- Need ad comments from the winning sale-price UGC.
- Need current customer review export.
- Need sample counts if the team wants quantitative claims about themes.
- Reddit is useful for language, not market share.
- Official science claims must be used verbatim or re-approved.
- GLP-1 angles require extra compliance review.
