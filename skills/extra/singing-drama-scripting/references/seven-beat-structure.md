# The 7-Beat Story Structure

Every proven singing drama runs the same emotional engine. The details change; the beats don't.
Source: the Singing Ads Breakdown deck (slide "The same 7-beat story") and seven proven scripts in
`examples/` — five True Nutra ads (Ancient Roots / Lymphoria, Resilia ×4) and two Penrose ads
(Cologne Billionaire, Sniper Song, Out of Milk).

| # | Beat | Job | Share of the song |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Shock line | Stop the scroll with a betrayal, humiliation or tragedy | first 1–4 lines |
| 2 | Sacrifice | Show what the hero gave, so the betrayal hurts | 10–25% |
| 3 | Rock bottom | The lowest point — and everything they tried that failed | 10–15% |
| 4 | Messenger | Someone (or something) reveals the hidden cause | 10–20% |
| 5 | The new ritual | The product as one simple daily habit | 5–15% |
| 6 | Glow-up | Visible wins, counted out day by day or week by week | 10–15% |
| 7 | Revenge payoff | The person who hurt them sees it — then the offer | 10–20% |

The offer lives at the end of beat 7: the singer turns to the viewer, restates the cause and the
ritual, gives price / bundle / guarantee and the link.

---

## 1 · Shock line

**Job:** the first sung line does all the stopping. First person, past tense, a specific person
and a specific wrong. No product, no claim, no question.

**Proven:**
- "He dumped me for gaining 10 pounds." (Resilia)
- "They found my mama on the ground." (Ancient Roots / Lymphoria — score 100)
- "I walked in on my stepmom sending nudes to my uncle on Christmas morning." (Resilia)
- "One night, I put on lingerie for my husband." (Resilia)
- "Best man at my wedding, he's in bed with my wife." (Penrose Sniper Song)
- "I cleaned an Arab cologne billionaire's pool and then I slept with his wife." (Penrose)

**Rules:** a relationship word (husband, mama, best man, stepmom) + a betrayal + one concrete
detail (10 pounds, Christmas morning, the front yard). Under ~15 words.
**Mistakes:** opening on the problem ("Tired of bloating?"), opening on the product, being vague
("my life fell apart").

## 2 · Sacrifice

**Job:** before the fall, show how much the hero gave. The bigger the sacrifice, the harder the
betrayal lands and the more the viewer wants the payoff.

**Proven:** "I was there for every appointment, every chemo session." · "Twenty years he was my
brother… three of us since we were nine." · "Best friends since high school, twenty-two years." ·
"Four years of driving to her house before work and after work."

**Rules:** a number of years, a duty, a moment of loyalty. Optional — Resilia "lingerie" skips it
and goes straight to rock bottom — but scripts with it run longer and hold better.

## 3 · Rock bottom

**Job:** the lowest point, always visual, plus a list of everything they tried. An authority
fails them — that's what makes the product "the thing the system missed".

**Proven:** "The doctor didn't even look up from her chart. Said it's just her age." · "I sat in my
car, crying for forty minutes." · "That night I looked in the mirror and I scared myself." · "I
had Baccarat, I had Creed, four hundred a bottle and I still came second place." · "The classes,
the Pilates, walking every morning, eating clean all week."

**Rules:** one physical place (mirror, parked car, motel bed), one failed authority or failed
expensive alternative, a list of 3 things they tried. The list kills objections before the product
appears.

## 4 · Messenger

**Job:** a trusted person — or a discovered object — reveals the real, hidden cause. It's never
your fault. The claim comes from a character, not the brand.

**Proven:** the sister on the phone from the motel · brother Marcus flying in from Atlanta ·
Keisha at church whose mother walked in alone · a gold pouch in the stepmom's cabinet · a box on
the rival's doorstep · the billionaire's wife in bed.

**The hidden villain** is named here: cortisol, parasites behind a biofilm wall, lymphatic waste,
alcohol-based cologne that fades in two hours. **It must be true for the product.** Use the
product's real mechanism; never borrow a villain because it worked for another brand.

## 5 · The new ritual

**Job:** the product as one simple, sensory daily habit — not a feature list.

**Proven:** "Two softgels every morning, that's it. Tastes like honey, a little warmth under your
tongue." · "Two drops under the tongue every morning." · "Took it. Rubbed it in that night."

**Rules:** how much, when, how it feels or tastes. One or two ingredients by name at most.
In the Penrose scripts the beat drops here — the musical lift lands on the ritual.

## 6 · Glow-up

**Job:** proof as a countdown of small visible wins, each a little bigger.

**Proven:** "Week one, my ring spun loose. Week two, my belt went tighter. Week three, my jawline
came back." · "Day one, the barista held my hand a second when she gave me my change. Day five…
Day nine… Day twelve…" · "Week two, mama got up out of her chair without grabbing the armrest."

**Rules:** 4–5 steps, each a different kind of win, each something you can see. Other people
notice (the barista, the coworker, the doctor). Results must be things the product can honestly
contribute to — see the compliance notes in `copy-chief.md`.

## 7 · Revenge payoff

**Job:** the person who hurt them sees the change. The hero stays calm and walks away. A stranger
asks the CTA question; the brand name is the answer. Then the offer.

**Proven:** "He said maybe he was too hasty." (left on read) · "Stop, what are you on? You're
glowing." "I said, Resilia." · "Babe, what is that?" "Ask him." · "He can't believe he smelled his
own secret on the pool boy's skin." · "Without that jar, he's just a man."

**Offer line pattern:** cause + ritual restated in one breath → product name → price / bundle →
guarantee → "link below" + a scarcity or secrecy kicker ("They sell out constantly", "Be careful
who you tell").

---

## Length

Proven runtimes 2:18 to 13:42. At ~2.5 sung words per second: a 3-minute song ≈ 450 words, a
5-minute song ≈ 750 words. Default draft target: **3–4 minutes (450–600 words)**.

**Short form works too.** Penrose "Out of Milk" runs all 7 beats in **1:15** (~190 words) and
scored 100. In short form keep shock, rock bottom, messenger, ritual and payoff full; give
sacrifice and glow-up one line each.

## Not every song is a drama

True Nutra Health "Blood Sugar" (score 100, 58 days) is an **ingredient countdown song**: one
person + one symptom, then "And if she took [ingredient] for 28 days, [visible change]" × each
ingredient, then the offer. See `examples/true-nutra-health-blood-sugar.md`. Offer it in Step 3
only when the product has several named ingredients and no natural betrayal story — label it as
the alternative format.

## Three hooks per script

Every script ships with three hooks that enter the same story from different doors (see
`hooks.md`). The body after the hook is shared.
