# Copy chief pass

The draft is a starting point. This pass shows the writer **what to fix and how**, so they learn
to do it themselves. Never rewrite the whole script for them in this pass — point, explain, give
one example, and let them decide.

## The scorecard (one row per beat)

| Beat | Score | What's working | Fix this | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Shock line | 🟢 / 🟡 / 🔴 | … | … | Before → After |

🟢 = ready · 🟡 = works, can be sharper · 🔴 = rewrite before recording.

## What to check, beat by beat

1. **Shock line** — Relationship word? Concrete detail? Under 15 words? Would it stop *you*?
2. **Sacrifice** — Is there a number of years and a duty? Do we love the hero before they fall?
3. **Rock bottom** — Is there a place you can see? A failed authority or expensive alternative?
   A list of three things they tried?
4. **Messenger** — Is the reveal coming from a character, not the brand? Is the hidden villain
   true for this product?
5. **The new ritual** — Is it one habit with a sensory detail, not a feature list?
6. **Glow-up** — 4–5 steps, each different, each visible, each noticed by someone?
7. **Revenge payoff** — Does the person who hurt them see it? Does a stranger ask the question the
   brand name answers? Is the offer one clean breath?

## Line-level checks

- **Sing it out loud.** Lines over ~14 syllables or with tongue-twisters will be mangled by the
  song tool. Mark them.
- **Specifics over adjectives.** "Ten pounds" beats "a little weight". "$400 a bottle" beats
  "expensive".
- **Cut re-explaining.** If a line says what the last line already said, cut it.
- **One idea per line.** Two ideas in one line = two lines.
- **Rhyme serves the story, never the other way round.** No line exists just to rhyme.

## Compliance — flag, don't fix silently

The proven ads in the swipe file make aggressive claims (parasites, cortisol, "lost 19 pounds").
That is what they ran, not what you should copy. Flag every line that:
- makes a health, weight-loss or medical claim the product can't support;
- promises a specific result or timeline as fact;
- implies sexual results or explicit content (keep intimacy non-explicit — ad platforms reject it);
- names a real brand in a comparison that could be challenged (e.g. "same oils as Creed").
Tell the writer to check these with the product's own approved claims before recording.

## The homework (end every pass with this)

> **Before you use this script:**
> 1. Read the proven scripts in `examples/` — at least two, start to finish.
> 2. Fix every 🔴 line yourself. Then the 🟡 ones.
> 3. Sing the whole thing out loud once. Mark anything you trip over.
> 4. Check every claim against what the product can actually say.
> This draft is a starting point. The script that works will be the one you rewrite.
