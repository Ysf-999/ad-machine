# Research — from a product link to a story brief

The story only works if the hidden villain and the ritual are **true for this product**. Research
first, invent second.

## What to collect

| Field | Where | Example (Penrose) |
|---|---|---|
| Product, format, how it's used | Product page | Pheromone body butter, rub into neck and wrists |
| Real mechanism / ingredients | Product page, ingredient list, FAQ | Same fragrance oils as Baccarat / Creed, in a butter, no alcohol |
| The cheaper/failed alternative | Category knowledge, reviews | Alcohol-based cologne that fades in ~2 hours |
| Buyer and their pain | Research doc, reviews, Reddit, forums | Men who never get compliments on their scent |
| Core desire | Research doc, reviews | To be noticed by women |
| Words buyers use | Reviews, comments, forums — quote them | "She asked what I was wearing" |
| Proof you can state | Product page, reviews | Review count, guarantee, ratings |
| Offer | Product page | $35, buy two get one free, 30-day guarantee |

## How to search

1. **Fetch the product link.** Read the product page, ingredients, FAQ, reviews on the page.
2. **Read the research doc** if given. It overrides your guesses.
3. **Search the web** for reviews, Reddit threads and forums about the category and the pain
   ("cologne doesn't last", "never get compliments on my scent"). Pull 5–10 real phrases.
4. **Competitor ads** — if an ad library tool is connected (GetHookd, Meta Ad Library, Maxfusion
   research), look for singing or story ads in the niche. Note their hooks and villains; never copy
   their lines.
5. **Write the brief** (Step 2 of the skill). Mark anything you could not verify as **unverified**.

## Truth rules

- Never invent an ingredient, a study, a statistic, a result or a price.
- The hidden villain must follow from the product's real mechanism. "Parasites behind a biofilm
  wall" belongs to an oregano-oil product making that claim — not to yours.
- Fiction is allowed in the **story** (characters, events). It is not allowed in **claims**.
