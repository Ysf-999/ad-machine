## What is taught
This is a breakdown of an organic, text-only ad for Dog Friendly Co. The ad has no voiceover. The instructor first plays the finished ad, then walks through the brief. His key point is that the layer order changes for text-only ads: music goes first instead of A-roll/VO. Music guides the viewer's "internal monologue" while they read the on-screen words.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Brief structure: 3 hooks and 2 holds, 3 total ads.
  - Hook 1 leads into Hold 1. This is the text-only version, with no VO.
  - Hooks 2 and 3 lead into Hold 2. These are the usual voiceover versions (Hook 3 uses an AI VO in an American female voice).
- The brief was built by combining an older ad that had potential but low spend with a UGC ad that was performing well. Both had similar concepts.
- Normal layer order: A-roll/VO, then music, then b-roll, and so on.
- Text-only layer order: **music first**, then A-roll/VO, then b-roll. This matches the board shown at 02:15.
- Why music comes first in text-only ads:
  - No one is talking, so the viewer reads the words in their own head.
  - The right music steers that internal monologue and lets you emphasize certain words.
  - It makes the audience emotionally invested, so they "self-validate" what they read.
- Hook 1 copy from the brief: "THIS IS CHOKING YOUR DOG…" followed by "AND THIS GETS YOUR DOG TO STOP PULLING."
- Brief notes for Hook 1:
  - Same visuals as Hook 3 in the example batch, but without the split screen.
  - Use only the top part of the split screen as the first hook (the owner falling), followed by the Ivarcs hook for the second clip.
- Brief notes for Hold 1: keep the same copy and visuals as the example batch, with no VO.
- Brief notes for Hold 2: start at the 0:14 point in Hook 1 of the example batch, on "the moment your dog…". The AI VO needs to be regenerated in an American accent to match the hooks, and the captions stay the same.
- Hook 2 note: no new copy, since the copy is on the UGC hook.
