## What is taught
The instructor sources b-roll for a dog-pulling-owner hook. He browses TikTok and his own clips bank, and rejects footage that looks wrong for the brand or is hard to read. He then picks a POV, motion-blur clip, brings it into Premiere and trims it to the voiceover line "…drag you down the street, but here's what happens when you…".

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Reject big, bulky or "bulldoggish" dogs. Also reject dogs with a prong collar. The dog shouldn't look unpremium, and the instructor "does not really pet this type of dogs". The footage then doesn't fit the brand (his personal opinion).
- Reject a clip even if it carries a message when you can't see what is happening. Handheld footage that isn't stabilized on the dog and the owner's action is not usable.
- The preferred clip is stabilized on the dog, with the owner's action tracked. He would use that in almost every project.
- Judge a clip by its first frame or thumbnail and understand it immediately. Don't hunt for a moment inside it.
- Motion blur in the first frame signals motion. That suits a dragging or pulling hook.
- An over-the-shoulder POV shot is "really what we need".
- The clip should run long enough before any burned-in text appears. Burned-in text on the source can be removed later with other tools.
- Trim the source to only the needed section ("we don't need the whole footage"). Place it on V1 under the matching VO phrase.
- After the drag clip, he cuts to a clip of the creator (Livamos) clipping the harness on from the front. This follows the VO "but here's what happens when you move that clip to the front".
