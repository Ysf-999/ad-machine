## What is taught
A tour of the ElevenLabs tools for ad voiceover. The instructor covers Text to Speech, Voice Changer and Sound Effects, then says the team actually generates voiceovers in **Studio**, using the Multilingual v2 model at Standard quality. At the end he starts a new Studio project and names it after the concept being worked on.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- **Text to Speech:** pick a voice (example "Jon Paul"), paste the script, hit Generate speech. Limit shown is 5,000 characters per generation (the example script was 790 characters).
- **Voice Changer:** record the script yourself (phone or mic), upload it, and pick a target voice. Pronunciation, tone and delivery stay the same, but the voice becomes the chosen one. Use it when text-to-speech struggles to read a script the way you want. Upload limit is 50MB for audio or video, or you can record in the tool.
- **Sound Effects:** generate SFX from a text prompt. The example prompt was "ticking". It returns 4 generations, and quality varies ("that one's not that good"). The instructor expects a more specific prompt to give better results. Use it when you need a specific sound effect you can't find elsewhere.
- **Studio** (the tool the team uses for voiceovers):
  - Click Start from scratch, then choose a model.
  - Recommended model: **Eleven Multilingual v2**. It is the most human-like and is labelled "High Quality".
  - Quality preset: **Standard** (128kbps, 44.1kHz, MP3). He says it is "perfectly fine".
  - Higher presets need the Pro plan:
    - High: 192kbps, +20% character cost.
    - Ultra: 192kbps, +50% character cost.
    - Ultra Lossless: 705.6kbps WAV, +100% character cost.
  - Rename the untitled project to the concept being worked on. Chapters can be named by script part.
- Script formatting seen in the Text to Speech frame: a `<break time="1s" />` tag inserts a 1-second pause. In the Milanote board the same pause is written as "(short pause in VO)".
