## What is taught
The instructor introduces a VSL ad breakdown. The brief is a story-based ad for a pouch supplement (cordyceps, the 1993 Chinese women's track team). For long scripts he recommends planning b-roll before opening the editor, using a two-column Google Doc with script lines on one side and the specific b-roll needed on the other. The aim is to avoid endlessly scrolling for clips and to work from a targeted list.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Brief context (the product is an oral pouch, not a pet product):
  - The story is based on real events: the Chinese women's track team broke world records in 1993, was suspected of doping, tested clean, and the coach credited cordyceps mushroom.
  - The product's main ingredient is cordyceps.
  - Differentiator: "90% of supplements get destroyed in the digestive system", whereas the pouch is absorbed through the buccal cavity (gums) into the bloodstream.
  - The claim is that this works better than what was available in 1993 ("30 years ago").
- Ad structure: 3 hooks plus 1 hold (the hold is the longer VO body).
- B-roll planning workflow:
  - Do it before opening the editing software.
  - Make a Google Doc with a 2-column table, as long as needed.
  - Left column is the script, put in line by line. Right column is the b-roll for that line.
  - Be as specific as possible about the b-roll. This stops your brain wandering and gives you "a targeted mission".
  - When you find and download a clip, highlight it or cross it off the list.
  - If you get a new b-roll idea while editing, edit the table. Ideas come while you work.
- The instructor frames this as especially useful for beginners.
