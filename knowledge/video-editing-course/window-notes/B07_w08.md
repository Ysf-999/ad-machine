## What is taught
How to build a top/bottom split-screen in Premiere Pro. The top half shows the bad example (the dog pulling the woman around) and the bottom half shows the good example (her walking easily with the harness). The setup is a guide at the exact vertical midpoint of a 1080x1920 frame. Both clips get the same Position, and each is cropped at the guide so the two halves meet cleanly. All action stays inside the safe zones.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Two clip sets are laid on two video tracks for one voiceover segment. Top: bad example, dog pulling her. Bottom: good example, calm walk in the harness.
- Frame is 1080x1920 (9:16). Midpoint is 1920 / 2 = 960.
- Show rulers: View > Show Rulers, or click the Program Monitor and press Ctrl+R.
- Add a guide by dragging down from the ruler. For a horizontal split, drag from the horizontal ruler.
- Guides must be unlocked to drag or edit them. Toggle Lock Guides in the View menu or with Ctrl+Alt+Shift+R (may differ on Mac).
- Right-click the guide > Edit Guide and enter 960, the frame height divided by 2. This puts the guide in the middle so both halves are equal.
- Position the top clip so the action stays above the midpoint and inside the safe zones. The bottom clip should show only what is below the midpoint.
- Copy the Motion settings from one clip and paste them onto the other so both share the same Position and Scale.
- The top clip's Position Y reads 606 in Effect Controls. Scale is 50 and X is 540.
- The bottom clip (Raw.mp4) shows Position 540/960, Scale 50, Anchor 1080/1920.
- Crop with the Crop Bottom setting in Effect Controls, on the top clip so that only the part above the midpoint remains.
- Hold Ctrl (Cmd on Mac) while dragging the value to change it in fine increments. Without Ctrl it moves too fast to measure accurately.
- Crop Bottom was about 27.3% mid-drag and 31.6% on screen at the end (about 31–32%). Zoom in on the Program Monitor to check the cut lands at the center.
