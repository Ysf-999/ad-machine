## What is taught
This is the module intro, not an editing tutorial. The instructor says strategy and fundamentals (buyer psychology, perfect-customer identification, what stops the scroll) must come before ad formats. He previews four ad types: organic-style, UGC, high-production and VSL. His main claim is that the right style depends on product and audience, and that a raw, low-fidelity ad can beat an expensive one.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Four ad types covered in the module:
  1. Organic-style ads that feel native to the platform.
  2. UGC that looks authentic.
  3. High-production ads, for when a premium feel is needed.
  4. VSLs that keep people glued to the screen.
- Pick the style by product, niche, brand and client. No single style wins everywhere.
- Case study (Wazoo Survival Gear, outdoor brand):
  - The cinematic shoot took days of planning, hours of filming, and professional cameras in the woods. It "did nothing".
  - The winner was a random person with an iPhone doing ASMR-style content in the woods, sharing favourite gear. It "printed money".
  - Takeaway: "sometimes the worst looking ad is actually the better ad". Ad quality is judged by resonance with the audience, not polish.
- Pillar curriculum:
  - Key characteristics of each ad type and when to use them.
  - How to pick the style for the product, brand and client.
  - How to structure hooks that stop scrolling.
  - How to retain viewers longer.
  - His tech stack and workflow.
- No editing numbers (cut rate, zoom, caption specs, dB levels) are given in this window.
