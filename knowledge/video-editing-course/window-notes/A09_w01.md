## What is taught
This window is strategy talk, not editing technique. The instructor explains why high-production ads matter: they reassure buyers who first found the brand through organic content. He then gives three scenarios where the spend is justified: premium positioning, visually driven products, and long-term brand building. He ends by starting to say that editing high-production ads has no one-size-fits-all approach.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Buyer journey (03:06–03:35): a viewer sees organic content or an ad, checks the brand's Instagram and website, may see another ad a few days later, reads reviews, then buys. Premium assets are what convince them the brand is "the real deal" at the checking stage.
- Go high production when:
  1. You need to establish a premium position. His example is skincare priced about 3x the market. The ad must "look as premium as your price tag". Basic Android footage won't carry a premium price.
  2. The product is bought with the eyes: fashion, designer pieces, beauty, lifestyle products, glasses. Here the production quality of the ad demonstrates the quality of the product.
  3. You are playing the long game, building a brand that runs for years. The footage is an asset that can be reused.
- Reuse of premium footage (05:02–05:24):
  - as B-roll mixed into UGC footage
  - in VSLs
  - as clips on the website
  - as a "premium toolkit" to pull from whenever something needs to feel more expensive
- He recommends brands invest in at least some high-production content.
- Editing (05:49 onward): there is no one-size-fits-all approach. Some studio footage needs only "subtle color grading and clean cuts" (the sentence is cut off at the end of the window).
- No numeric edit settings are given in this window: no durations, zoom %, caption specs, or dB levels.
