## What is taught
This window covers the voiceover stage of a long-form VSL-style ad built on a real-incident story (Chinese runners, cordyceps, Flow Pouch). The instructor generates an authoritative AI narrator voice in ElevenLabs Studio, then cuts and colour-codes the VO in Premiere. He leaves deliberate pauses for emphasis and applies an audio chain (multiband compressor, hard limiter, DeEsser) to the VO.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Voice choice: pick a voice that matches the ad's tone. Here he wanted "authority", a proper narrator, because the story is based on a real incident. The voice shown in ElevenLabs is "James T". He contrasts it with a more casual, relaxed voice that he says is also great for AI.
- ElevenLabs script prep:
  - Separate the hooks into their own lines/blocks.
  - Capitalise words to emphasise, e.g. "didn't JUST win", "EVERY single test", "FAR more ancient", "POWERFUL", "TRULY", "GONE".
  - Put dashes and ellipses in place for pacing, e.g. "…" and " - ".
  - Listen to the generations and check the delivery suits the ad before exporting and downloading.
- Three hook variants are written at the top of the script:
  - "How China's track team 'LEGALLY Cheated' in 1993"
  - "How China Shattered 3 World Records EXPLAINED"
  - "The Truth Behind China's 1993 'Doping' Scandal"
- Cutting the VO in Premiere:
  - Cut out silences and breaths, as with a human recording.
  - Fillers are unlikely with an AI voiceover.
  - Leave subtle pauses on the parts you want to emphasise. He demonstrates with "Records that had stood for years… gone." A small gap before "gone" makes it land; without the gap it hits much weaker.
  - Cutting does not have to be word by word every time.
- Colour-coding VO clips on the timeline (helps with long scripts):
  - Blue = hook.
  - Green = hold.
  - Orange = small bits marking the words he wants to emphasise (he says "four words"; the transcript is unclear on exactly what they are).
  - A colour change (back to blue/teal in the later part) = the point where the product is introduced. Everything after is product-related.
- VO audio effect chain on the track (Premiere Audio Track Mixer, effects slots on A1/Output 1):
  - Multiband Compressor, preset "Broadcast".
  - Hard Limiter at -6 (dB).
  - DeEsser, which removes hiss/sibilance. He uses it "kind of often, depending on the voices". He starts to play an A/B example without it.
- DeEsser panel values (from screen): preset "Low Voice DeEsser", Mode set to Multiband, threshold -30 dB, center frequency 4700 Hz, bandwidth 3000 Hz, "Output Sibilance Only" unchecked.
