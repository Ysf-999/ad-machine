## What is taught
The instructor sets the voiceover (A-roll) loudness in Premiere Pro using the sequence's audio meter. Phones played without headphones need loud VO, and the hook is the part most likely to be too quiet. He measures the hook, adds gain in the Properties panel, then re-checks the meter. He finds that too much gain pushes peaks to 0 dB.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- The raw hook VO ("This one change stops pulling forever…") peaks at about −15 to −21 dB. He calls this too quiet.
- Reason: viewers are on Facebook/Instagram on phones, often without headphones, and cannot hear quiet audio right away.
- Rule of thumb: keep A-roll (the voiceover) peaks between −3 and −6 dB on the meter.
- Music, SFX and everything else can sit lower than the VO.
- The VO can occasionally drop to about −9 dB, but aim for −3 to −6.
- Method: take the gap between the current level and the target and add that much gain. From −15/−21, he adds +11 dB and the VO then reads around −10 on the meter.
- He then raises the whole VO track to +12 dB, which he says is "just right."
- The hook still fluctuates around −10. Raising it to +15 dB makes it far too loud and the meter goes straight to 0, which is bad (clipping).
- Gain is applied per clip. The long VO clip ("hold dogs.mp3") and the hook clip ("h1 hook dogs.mp3") are adjusted separately. The hook is boosted more than the body.
