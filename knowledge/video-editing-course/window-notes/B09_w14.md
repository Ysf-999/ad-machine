## What is taught
The instructor finishes a subject/background separation in Photoshop (select subject, expand the selection, remove the subject from the background, export both as PNGs). He then builds a fake-3D parallax push-in in After Effects by scaling the two layers at different rates. He then moves to the captions/text layer of the VSL ad. In Premiere he generates captions from the transcript with short, single-line settings.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Photoshop separation workflow:
  - Ctrl+click the layer mask to reload the subject selection.
  - Select > Modify > Expand by about 3–5 px, then OK.
  - Run the Remove tool (it sits in the Generative Fill contextual bar) to erase the subject from the background plate.
  - The leftover artifact (partial "TCK" sign text) is ignored because the subject will cover it.
  - Export the subject layer as PNG (right-click > Export As / Quick Export as PNG).
  - Select the background layers, press Ctrl+G to group them, then export the group as PNG.
- Parallax principle: closer objects move faster and farther objects move slower, so scale the subject and the background at different speeds.
- Parallax values:
  - At 0:00, both layers' Scale = 100%.
  - At about 10 s, subject Scale = 115% and background Scale = 106%.
  - Animate Scale only, with two keyframes per layer.
  - The screen shows the subject at about 115.2% and the background at 106.0% at 0:00:10:00.
- The instructor says the effect doesn't really matter: "I just did it for the fun of it."
- Captions in Premiere:
  - Open the Text panel (Window > Text) and click Create captions from transcript.
  - Open Captioning Preferences and drag "Maximum length in characters" all the way down to the minimum.
  - Set Lines to Single.
  - Click Transcribe & create captions.
  - The result is very short caption cards of one to three words, e.g. "September 1993,", "Beijing, China", "The World Track", "and Field", "Championships", "in just", "15 days,", "a group".
