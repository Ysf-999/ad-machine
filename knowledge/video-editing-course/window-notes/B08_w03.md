## What is taught
How to source b-roll for an organic-style UGC ad: search TikTok by keyword (e.g. "dog leash pull"), download the clip with SnapAny, and strip burned-in captions or watermarks with Vmake. From about 10:54 the instructor starts a clip-by-clip breakdown of the finished ad. Each b-roll clip is matched literally to the line of on-screen text, starting with the pain point and then the solution.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Sourcing: search TikTok for keywords that match the ad concept (example: "dog leash pull") and pick a clip that suits the script.
- SnapAny downloads images and videos and works on almost every site **except Facebook**.
- Avoid clips from very famous or well-established creators, to reduce the chance of copyright claims. The instructor says there are workarounds but he won't recommend any.
- If a good clip has burned-in captions, run it through Vmake's "Watermark & Text Remover".
- Vmake free tier: limited to **5 seconds per clip** (previews are labelled "5s Preview"). Pro is **$70 per year**, which he recommends. He says he uses it "pretty much all the time" when sourcing clips.
- Vmake also offers a noise reducer and video and image enhancers.
- Breakdown logic: each b-roll clip should be coherent with the line shown on screen.
  - Line 1, "Traditional harnesses and collars give dogs exactly what they want": show the pain point right away, with a dog in a collar pulling on the leash and the owner struggling.
  - Line 2, "Forward momentum": a goofy clip of a woman being pulled or overpowered by the dog. It is there for shock factor and attention, and it illustrates the word "momentum".
  - Line 3, "But watch what happens with this front clip attachment": introduce the solution with b-roll of the front D-ring attachment being clipped onto the harness.
- Timing on the timeline (read from the playhead, not stated by the instructor):
  - Hook line 1 runs around 0:04–0:06.
  - "FORWARD MOMENTUM" shows at about 0:07.
  - The front-clip line shows at about 0:08.
  - So each clip lasts roughly 1.5–3 s.
