## Concrete rules and numbers
- Music choice follows the intended audience emotion: happy music for happy, sad and dull music for sad.
- No music is a valid choice. Skip it when the ad should feel like a person talking to the audience, authentic and raw, especially on a sensitive topic. Music would distract from the message. He adds that it is his call and others can choose differently.
- B-roll emphasizes the A-roll, so what is shown should match what the speaker is saying.
  - Speaker talks about birds → show a bird.
  - Speaker talks about shoulder pain → show a person suffering with shoulder pain, or a shoulder-joint animation showing the cause.
  - The B-roll just has to get the point across.
- Reason for B-roll: nobody wants a talking head the whole video, especially a random person. B-roll "breaks the monotone" and adds engagement.
- Example in this ad:
  - A-roll line: "This is my body. It grew two sets of humans. It's not broken. It's just operating on a different set of rules now."
  - B-roll: a woman in a fitted beige/tan set, shown holding her stomach and body, used as pain points the audience can relate to, "a person suffering with the same problem."
- With UGC ads you are limited to the footage the creator supplies. He starts to expand on this, but the window ends mid-sentence.
