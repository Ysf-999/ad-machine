## What is taught
The instructor explains why music under a voiceover needs a "pocket" cut for the voice. He applies Premiere's Simple Parametric EQ to the music track so the music's midrange dips where human speech sits. He then A/B tests with and without the EQ. His rule is that lowering volume alone makes the mix flat and still leaves the speech unintelligible, so EQ first and volume second if still needed.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Human speech sits in the midrange of the spectrum, so carve the music's midrange to make space for the voice.
- Simple Parametric EQ settings on the music clip (visible in Effect Controls, named "Music eq"):
  - Center: 1500.0 Hz
  - Q: 4.0
  - Boost: -15.0 dB
- Order of operations: apply the EQ first, then adjust music volume only "if needed".
- Volume only: the music is "too loud" and speech is unintelligible. Lowering the volume alone "kind of works" but sounds flat.
- Music is "chaotic" and uncontrollable even when instrumental, so the EQ is applied to make it sit with the voice.
- A/B demo: without the EQ the speech cannot be recognised; with it the speech is clear. He repeats the test on the line "and that back clip harness is making it worse".
- Voice clip level boosted: the hook voice clip shows Level 13.0 dB and "hold dogs" shows 12.0 dB, with keyframes on the level.
- Music clip Level was briefly -10.0 dB on screen (20:00), then 0.0 dB (20:15 onward). The final volume is unclear.
