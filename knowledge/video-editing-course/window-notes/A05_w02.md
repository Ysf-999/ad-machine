## Concrete rules and numbers
- The CTA should tell the viewer exactly what to do next. It should be simple and clear, and usually carries urgency or an incentive. The sample CTA is "Click the link below to get limited time 20% off."
- The sample UGC script runs in this order, with section labels shown on screen:
  1. Hook: "Posture check. If your back is killing you right now from sitting in that chair, give me 60 seconds."
  2. PROBLEM: 12 hours a day at a desk, destroyed back, couldn't focus, "22 and already walking around like an old man", back crackling "like a bowl of Rice Krispies".
  3. PRODUCT INTRO: "that's when I discovered this ergonomic chair", plus a pre-emptive objection handle: "before you scroll past thinking, oh great, another overpriced office chair, this is different."
  4. BENEFITS / FEATURES: dynamic lumbar technology that moves with you; adjustable arms, seat depth and back tension. Unlike the "basic" alternative with flat back support.
  5. Bad alternatives: "three different ergonomic chairs from Amazon" that felt good for about a week, then the support gave out. Then the product: used for six months, never felt better.
  6. RESULTS: "Look at this. This isn't me forcing good posture. This is how I naturally sit now." Then: no back pain, no fidgeting, can focus.
  7. CTA: limited-time 20% off, plus "Your back and your productivity will thank you."
- The structure follows the customer's thought sequence: realise they have a problem, see why other solutions haven't worked, then seek proof the product is different.
- The same structure works for any product (skincare, fitness equipment, tech gadgets). Keep the core structure and swap the details.
- Alternative structures the instructor lists:
  - Lead with results immediately if the product is very results-oriented. For the chair, this could be a split screen of bad posture before and good posture after.
  - Drop the "bad alternatives" section if the product is unique and has no competitors.
  - Show the product in action for about 30 seconds with minimal talking. His example is a nonstick pan: cook several different foods and nothing sticks, with no explanation of how it works.
- "There's no one size fits all template." Understand the product, know the audience, and structure the content so it feels natural.
