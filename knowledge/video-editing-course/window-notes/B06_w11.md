## Concrete rules and numbers
- Hiding a cut: a zoom was used to remove the visible cut between two clips. Use tricks like this where they fit, but "don't overdo it. It can get funny."
- You don't have to rely only on b-roll. Use a graph, image or meme as a visual insert when the context and audience suit it.
- Authority graph: when the speaker makes a factual or health claim, show a Google-sourced graph related to it.
  - The line was "Prolactin levels stay elevated for 6 to 12 months after nursing. Programming your body to hold on to fat stores."
  - The graph was dropped in and cropped a bit.
  - Purpose: the audience thinks "this person did her research."
- Auto-captions in Premiere Pro (Text panel → Captions tab → Create captions):
  - He says he doesn't go into the details for UGC and just clicks Create captions.
  - The dialog shows preset "Subtitle default", Format "Subtitle", Style "None", and Lines set to Double.
  - The slider labels are max length in characters, min duration in seconds, and gap between captions in frames. The values on screen look like about 42 characters, min duration about 3 s and gap 0, but they are small and not clearly readable.
- Default caption placement is not in the safe zone, so fix it in Properties.
- Caption style:
  - Font: Proxima Nova, Semibold. He says to download it, because it is used in TikTok and organic text and is one of the most used.

- Position: use the Align and Transform "Zone" 3x3 grid to snap the captions to a screen zone. He usually keeps them at the bottom, bottom-center on screen. Keep them inside the safe zone.
