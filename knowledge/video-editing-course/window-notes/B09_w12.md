## What is taught
The instructor shows how he builds b-roll frames in After Effects for a VSL ad. He makes a rounded-rectangle shape layer and uses it as a track matte, so each b-roll clip sits inside the frame, and he parents or pick-whips the clips to it. He then adds small animations, such as a position and scale zoom-in to reveal the coach's face. For news articles, he adds a highlighter effect with a yellow shape in Multiply blend mode.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Rounded rectangle shape: set Roundness to a higher value. He mentions 16, 32 or 8 as examples, and he used **16**.
- To put a clip inside the frame, use the Track Matte dropdown and select the shape layer (here "Shape Layer 1"). Alternatively, drag the pick-whip (the "swirl") to the shape layer in the top row. The clip then sets itself to that layer.
- Repeat for every clip that needs the frame. Then resize or reposition each clip to match the framing.
- Do this in After Effects rather than Premiere. He says it can be done in Premiere but is annoying, and he doesn't recommend it.
- Add b-roll animation where it helps the viewer. Example: when the coach is introduced, the shot moves left to right across many people, so you can't tell which one is the coach. He animated position and scale so it zooms in on his face first.
- Highlighter effect for news articles:
  - Put the article screenshot in the track matte.
  - Create a shape with a bright yellow (or other highlighter) fill.
  - Set the shape's blend mode to **Multiply**.
  - Move or resize the shape over the text you want highlighted.
- If the Track Matte or Blending Mode columns aren't visible, enable them with the toggles at the bottom-left of the timeline. He keeps both on.
