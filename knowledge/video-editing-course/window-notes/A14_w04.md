## What is taught
Music should carry emotion and back up the story the script tells. The CarePod humidifier ad is the first example. Hectic, fast music runs under the problem section. At the solution/product switch it changes to chill lo-fi that matches the brand's high-end, cozy feel. The second example is a jewelry ad that won using a trending TikTok sound, picked because the target audience (about 20–25-year-olds) already listens to that kind of music.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Use music to make people feel something and to support the visuals and the script's story.
- Problem section: fast, hectic music with a lot happening on screen.
- Solution/product section: switch to very chill, aesthetic lo-fi music. The switch lands on the "designed by a doctor" moment.
- Match the music to the brand's vibe. CarePod is high-end, almost luxury, cozy and warm, "elevates your home".
- Match the music to what the target audience consumes. The jewelry ad targeted roughly 20, 22 and 25-year-olds.
- A trending or viral TikTok sound (many edits already using it) can work for a young audience. The instructor says this is a legal gray zone and that you shouldn't legally use it. The sentence is cut off at the end of the window.
- The jewelry ad with the viral sound was a winning ad that "crushed" for the client.
- No dB levels, durations or SFX settings are given in this window.
