## What is taught
The instructor shows an AI voiceover workflow in ElevenLabs Studio. He pastes the VO script from a brief (kept in a Milanote board) into the "Hold" chapter, picks the voice "Carepod Mama", generates the whole read, and listens. The first full generation sounds disjointed, so the lesson is not to paste a brief and use the output as-is. He regenerates and edits line by line until it sounds right, and he starts commenting on each edit.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Never copy-paste a brief into the TTS tool and ship the first generation. Instructor: it "would be a disaster."
- Generate the whole script first, listen, then decide what to change.
- Regenerate "a couple of times until you really get it sounding perfect." He calls this the key to generating voice sources.
- Problem found: the lines sound "disjointed and disconnected," so the connecting parts between sentences need fixing.
- Fix example: add an exclamation mark at the end of "That's when I found CarePod, a humidifier designed by a doctor for his own family!" to give the sentence more energy.
- Script structure: the board has Hook 1, 2 and 3 cards, then a HOLD card, then a CTA card, each with its own "AI VO" text. Each section is its own chapter in ElevenLabs (Hook 1, Hook 2, Hook 3, Hold).
- The HOLD VO runs about 5 seconds in the player in the early takes (timeline shows 0:04–0:05). The CTA runs about 2–3 seconds.
- Stage directions like "(short pause in VO)" are written in the script text. The model read that text aloud ("Short pause in VO.") in the first generation, so such directions should not be left in the text sent to TTS.
- The ElevenLabs player speed is 1.0.
- A voice can switch unexpectedly to another one. The instructor had to reselect "Carepod Mama" because the tool kept choosing "Honey Packs Female," then applied it to all the selected lines.
