## What is taught
Organic ads are the easiest ad type to make, sometimes just text slapped on a video. There are three types: customer testimonials, social media style ads, and lifestyle content. The window covers the first two and stops just before the lifestyle type. The choice of type depends on what content the customer actually consumes and finds natural in their feed, not on what advertisers think looks cool. The editing format should make the ad feel like organic content rather than an ad.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Three organic types: customer testimonials, social media style ads, lifestyle content.
- Organic ads need no fancy transitions, complicated effects or animations.
- Choose the type by asking:
  - What content does the customer consume day to day?
  - What makes them stop scrolling?
  - What are their interests?
  - What feels natural in their feed?
- Testimonials: real customers talking about their experience. Keep them raw, authentic and minimally edited.
- Imperfect footage is fine and even desirable. A video that isn't level, has bad lighting or looks "ugly" signals "raw, not scripted".
- Don't try to make testimonials pretty.
- Minimum bar for a testimonial: the audio is clear and a face is visible.
- Social media style ads: shoot on an iPhone, in a home setting, with a simple POV-style text overlay that the creator could have written herself on TikTok.
- Example: a "burned slideboard" ad of a fit mom working out at home. It beat all the brand's professional content because it matched what the target audience (busy moms who want to get fit) watches daily.
- The team replicated that ad in the exact same style, with the same angles and casual feel as the fitness influencers the audience follows.
- The goal is that these ads "don't feel like ads at all". The editing format matters a lot for this (the explanation is cut off at 03:00).
- No numbers were given for durations, font sizes, zoom, dB or similar in this window.
