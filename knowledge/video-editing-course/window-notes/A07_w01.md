## What is taught
This window is VSL (video sales letter) scripting structure, not editing technique. It covers the rest of the hook rules, then elements 2–4 of a four-part VSL: problem amplification, the hope spot, and the start of solution buildup. The examples use a sleep supplement as a stand-in product.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- A hook must do two things: grab attention and qualify the audience. A clickbaity hook that doesn't relate to the viewer's problem is "useless".
- Hook formula: a personal struggle the target audience has, plus a shocking twist.
  - Controversy example: "The real reason you're still awake at 3am isn't what the doctor told you."
  - Belief-questioning example: "What if everything you know about sleep is actually making you more tired."
- Element 2, problem amplification:
  - Make the problem feel worse than the viewer thought.
  - Find new ways to scare them beyond what they already know.
  - Show why everything they've tried before is doomed to fail.
  - Build tension until they are desperate for a solution.
  - Examples: "that afternoon crash isn't just annoying, it's your brain eating itself"; "those sleeping pills are making your natural sleep patterns worse."
- Element 3, the hope spot:
  - Don't jump straight to the product. Build credibility first with a discovery story.
  - The story should present something unique that others missed and that isn't common knowledge.
  - Target emotion: "I've never heard of this, this sounds promising."
  - Examples: "Everything changed when I met this strange sleep researcher in Japan"; "I discovered this ancient herb that monks have been using for centuries."
- Element 4, solution buildup:
  - Introduce the solution gradually.
  - Don't dump everything at once, such as the full ingredient list. The window ends before he gives the fix.
- Order of elements: 1 Hook, 2 Problem Amplification, 3 Hope Spot, 4 Solution Buildup.

No timings, zoom, caption, audio or other editing numbers are given in this window.
