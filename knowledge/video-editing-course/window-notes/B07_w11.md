## What is taught
The instructor explains why the hooks in this batch pair a negative voiceover with a positive visual. Hook 1 pairs a "regret" line with a selling point (the dog putting on the harness, doable in about 3 seconds). This creates cognitive dissonance and an open loop that keeps people watching. He then moves to the "Animation and Transitions" layer. His one small but important fix is keeping direction consistent between similar clips by applying a Horizontal Flip so the action doesn't jump sides at a cut.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Hook 1 visual: a clip of the dog putting on the harness, chosen because the harness goes on "within three seconds". The selling point is shown right away.
- Hook 1 voiceover: "I regret getting this from my dog." Showing a selling point while the voice says she regrets the purchase creates cognitive dissonance (two things that don't go together) and an open loop. Viewers are curious about the answer and are more likely to keep watching.
- The batch's main idea is negative framing. Because the voiceover is negative, the visuals in hooks 2 and 3 are positive. Example: on "Don't make the same mistake as me" he shows the dog chilling, then a clip of her putting on the harness and a happy dog.
- Consistency rule: keep screen direction consistent between similar clips.
  - Example: a front-attachment clip is followed by another close-up of the same action filmed from the opposite side.
  - Our eyes get drawn to the action on one side, and a flipped next clip feels jarring, as if something is off.
  - Fix: apply the Horizontal Flip effect to the mismatched clip. The difference between clips then feels much more fluid.
- Train yourself as an editor to spot these small continuity issues.
- Other editing software may need a different approach to flipping. In ffmpeg this is the `hflip` filter (my note, not the instructor's).
