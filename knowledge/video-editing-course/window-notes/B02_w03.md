## What is taught
The instructor opens Final Cut Pro with a raw talking-head UGC clip (about 1:04, a woman speaking to camera) and starts the editing workflow. Step 1 is audio: set the voiceover level so it peaks at -6 dB. Step 2 is cutting: the raw take contains three spoken hooks in a row, so for each variation you keep only one hook and cut the others out. He says he won't teach the editing software itself.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Check audio levels first and make the audio crisp before anything else. He calls this "super important".
- Voiceover baseline: levels between -6 and -12 dB, with -6 dB as the maximum ("minus six tops").
- Worked example: the level sat a little under -6, so he raised it by "maybe just one decimal more" (about 0.1 dB steps) until the meter peaked at -6. He calls that "exactly where we want it to be".
- Second step after levels: cut the footage.
- The raw clip has three hooks back to back, with a pause of about a second between them:
  - Hook 1: "...and it's ice cold" (the hook text itself is only partly audible).
  - Hook 2: "I used to hate taking the last sip of my morning coffee..."
  - Hook 3: a "was spending money every..." line, which then leads into the rest of the script.
- Variation logic: each variation uses one hook. Variation 1 uses only the first hook, so he cuts after hook 1 (Cmd+B in Final Cut) and removes hooks 2 and 3.
- The body of the script stays the same across variations. Only the hook changes.
