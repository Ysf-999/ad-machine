## What is taught
The instructor lays out the five-step process behind a UGC ad: research, ideation, script and shot list, production, editing. He explains that the editor's job is to piece the creator's script and dedicated B-roll together so the ad feels natural and keeps people watching. He assigns a practical exercise: edit a real UGC brief, creator script and B-roll into an ad. He also shares a board of reference UGC ads to study before starting, and teases VSLs as the next lesson.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- UGC production pipeline, in order:
  1. Research: product, audience, what's working in the market.
  2. Ideation: choose the angle and the script structure.
  3. Script and shot list: write the copy and build a shot list for the creator.
  4. Production: the creator records the script and shoots B-roll.
  5. Editing: piece it all together.
- Every part of the script structure has dedicated shots and dedicated B-roll that supports it. Most UGC briefs the editor receives will look like this.
- The editor's job is to assemble the footage "in a way that feels natural and keeps people watching."
- When studying the reference ads, look at three things:
  - how they paste (place) the footage
  - how they match B-roll to the script
  - how they transition between shots
- The editing exercise package contains a real UGC brief, a creator script and B-roll from the same creator. Submit the result to the instructor for personal feedback.
- No numeric values (durations, zoom %, caption specs, dB levels) are given in this window.
