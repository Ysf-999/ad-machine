## What is taught
The end of the progress-bar/timer retention technique, then a wrap-up of the three MrBeast-derived retention principles: open loops, visual variety matched to audience pace, and progress psychology. Progress bars and countdown timers show viewers how much longer they have to pay attention, so they are more likely to stay to the end. The instructor says a scroll-stopping hook only gets a pause; retention principles keep people watching.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Testimonials: don't run them back to back with nothing between them. Add a small progress bar that fills with each testimonial.
- VSL-style ads:
  - Spend the first part of the ad on the problem, pain points and struggles.
  - Mention the "breakthrough solution" now and then, but don't reveal what it is or show the product until later.
  - Just before the product reveal, show a title like "revolutionary solution revealed in 20 seconds" and start a countdown timer.
  - Keep reinforcing the earlier points while the timer runs, so viewers are on the edge of their seat when it hits zero.
- Why it works: people stay longer when they know exactly how much longer they must pay attention. They see progress, and that rewards them as they watch.
- Summary of the three principles:
  1. Open loops: stack reveal after reveal, each more intriguing than the last, so people need to know what happens next.
  2. Visual variety, which he calls the most important of the three: never let the viewer's eyes get comfortable, and always match editing pace to the audience.
  3. Progress psychology: use visual cues, timers and progress bars to keep people invested, with some reward for completing parts or all of the video.
- A hook may make people pause, but retention principles keep them watching.
