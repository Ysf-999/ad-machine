## What is taught
The window finishes the first MrBeast technique (visual variety: every visual change has a purpose) and adapts it to ads by compressing it: something must change every 1–3 seconds, and the pace should match the audience's age. It then introduces technique two, "progress bar psychology": a progress bar or timer that starts fast and then slows, so the viewer commits and keeps watching. The instructor suggests using a visible timer in ads.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Each visual switch should have a purpose:
  - Close-ups of people for emotional moments.
  - Wide shots (e.g. a big zoom-out showing the whole area) for scale and impact.
  - Reaction shots for authenticity: raw camera footage, behind-the-scenes feel.
  - Speed up or slow down deliberately.
- For ads, compress this principle: something must happen every 2–3 seconds. The instructor would "even say every one second".
- A "change" means a B-roll change, a new animation, or new text appearing. Other examples: a new angle, a new product shot, text popping up.
- Reason: people scroll "at light speed", and new visual information every couple of seconds stops them leaving.
- Match pacing to the audience:
  - Gen Z: something every single second, quick "brain rot" edits.
  - People in their sixties: fast, hectic editing is probably the worst choice because they can't keep track.
  - Style should resemble the content that audience already consumes daily.
- Progress bar psychology (MrBeast sponsor segments):
  - The bar moves very fast at first: about 50% within the first 10 seconds.
  - The viewer thinks "this will be quick", then the bar slows and the second half takes much longer.
  - By then the viewer has sunk in effort and finishes.
- Ad application: don't jump straight to before/after. Talk about something else first and show a small timer, e.g. "see results in 15 seconds", and start it. This gives the viewer a clear expectation of how long to pay attention.
