## What is taught
The instructor shows how to pick a b-roll clip for a stretch of voiceover. The clip's length should roughly match the length of the spoken line, so the viewer can take in what it shows while hearing the explanation. A clip that is too short flashes by, carries no information and just distracts. The same applies to composition: if the dog is out of frame for half the clip, the clip needs fixing.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Choose each b-roll clip by its length relative to the speech segment it covers. The goal is for the clip to cover that part of the speech from start to end.
- Do not stack a couple of short clips over one sentence, even though that might help retention. For a line like "It connects right to their strongest muscles", use one clip that fits that line.
- A clip that is too short ends before the line does. The viewer starts to understand it and then it disappears, which does not help the message.
- Test: put the short clip in as-is and play it against the line. The instructor demonstrates that it "does not carry any sort of information".
- Goal: the viewer gets the information "clearly and vividly… without any flaws or distractions."
- Check the usable part of the clip. If the dog is not visible for half of it, that clip is a problem and has to be fixed (the fix is cut off at 15:00).
- Clip length matters as much as clip content.
