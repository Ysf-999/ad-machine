## What is taught
The instructor shows a "dual-track green screen" workflow in Final Cut Pro. A green-screen-keyed copy of the talking-head video sits directly above an unkeyed copy, and both are cut identically so they stay perfectly synced. Per scene, he can toggle between the keyed version (speaker shrunk over background videos) and the full-frame version. At the end he adds music, copying a track from an earlier version, and explains how he found it.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Green screen key: apply the Green Screen Keyer effect to the top clip (the effect is searched as "green" in the effects browser). Then transform it: scale it down and reposition it to get the green screen look.
- Position seen in the inspector at about 21:15: X 322 px, Y -224 px. This puts the small speaker in the lower-right of the 1080x1920 frame.
- Keyed copy and unkeyed copy must be cut the same way, with the same cut points. Make every cut on both layers, so both versions are "perfectly aligned, perfectly synced".
- Per-scene switching:
  - Hook: turn the unkeyed clip off and the keyed clip on. Make the speaker smaller and add videos in the background.
  - Next scene: show the speaker full frame by turning the keyed layer off.
  - Following scene: turn the keyed layer back on while she talks about something.
- Toggling layers per cut makes switching between green screen and no green screen easy, because both versions stay on the timeline at once.
- Music: add it after the cuts and green screen are done. The instructor copies the track from an earlier version of the project.
- Music sourcing: search TikTok for "coffee". Look at "day in the life" and aesthetic videos of girls grabbing a coffee or walking, and take the track from those.
- Project: 1080x1920, 30 fps, 9:16. The final timeline is 47:16 long, with roughly 14 cuts in about 47 s (about 3 s per segment, some shorter at about 1 s).
- The music clip's volume is shown as -18.0 dB in the inspector at 23:47.
