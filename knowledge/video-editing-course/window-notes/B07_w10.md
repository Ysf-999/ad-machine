## What is taught
The instructor walks through how he chose b-roll for the "hold" (body) of a UGC-style organic ad. Each clip is matched to the line being spoken, so the visual never disconnects from the voiceover. He keeps the edit simple, with no fancy effects. At the end he moves on to hooks and argues they deserve far more edit time than the body.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Check the start and end points of any AI-assisted result (an automated tool, unnamed in this window). It can miss, and the error carries through the rest of the video. He says he "got lucky" on a high-movement clip.
- Match b-roll to the literal meaning of the spoken line:
  - "Everyone at the dog park keeps asking where I got it… I have to stop every two minutes to show them" → happy dog clips in a park setting.
  - "Lifetime warranty" → close-ups of the harness showing material quality (stitching, the NOVA-labelled strap).
  - "These are the kind of problems I can live with" → shows both the person and the dog happy with the harness. This is the turning point of the video. It signals she doesn't regret the purchase, and the clip is chosen to emphasize that.
  - "Shock absorption leash is great for dogs with sudden bursts of energy / if your dog's a puller" → show the leash, then the product in action.
- After the turning point, the b-roll shows the solution and how it works, still matched to what's said.
- Keep the edit simple. He did nothing fancy or complicated in the body.
- Hooks: most editors spend hours on the hold and about five minutes on the hook. He calls this backwards. His line is that a bad hook wastes the hold, however good the hold is.
