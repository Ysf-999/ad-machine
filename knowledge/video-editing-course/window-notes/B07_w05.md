## What is taught
How to source background music for an organic-style ad, in three options. (1) Search TikTok for the product niche, find a video with decent views and engagement, identify the song (Shazam or Google), then find an instrumental version. (2) Use stock music sites, mainly Artlist, filtered by mood and genre. (3) Use the TikTok Creative Center trends tab to find songs trending in the target country. The instructor's own ad used a track called "Aesthetic" found there.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Option 1, TikTok search: search niche keywords (here "best dog harnesses" / "dog harnesses"). Pick a video with a decent number of views and engagement. Identify the song with Shazam or Google, then search for the **instrumental version**.
- Reason for instrumentals: vocals clash with the voiceover. If the song has vocals, look for an instrumental.
- Option 2, stock sites: Artlist is his pick for its wide variety of good music, plus stock footage and AI tools. Epidemic Sound is also good, but Artlist wins on footage and AI. Free sites like Pixabay "aren't the best". Paying for a stock site is "worth your buck".
- Artlist workflow: Music tab on the left. The most-used filters are **Genre** and **Mood**. For a happy ad, include the mood "Happy". Click the minus on a mood (e.g. "Serious") to exclude it. Genre works the same way. Then browse the list for what suits the ad.
- Option 3, TikTok Creative Center (bookmark it): Trends tab, then Songs. Select the country you sell in (United States here). The default list is songs trending over the last 7 days. The dropdown also offers last 30 days and last 120 days.
- His ad's choice: last 120 days (about 4 months), song "Aesthetic" (Tollan Kim, rank 7 in that list). Clicking a song shows related videos using it. Warning: audio can be very loud when previewing.
