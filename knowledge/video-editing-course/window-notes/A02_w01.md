## What is taught
Ads should look like organic, non-professional content, as if someone who just posts on Instagram made them, not like polished editing. The instructor gives Peekaboo Pillow as the example. When an ad looks like an ad, viewers put their guard up and scroll past. When it looks like normal content, they stop and watch. He then starts the two critical elements of organic ads. The first is showing a target-audience person who looks "achievable". The second, copy, is introduced at the end of the window.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Goal: "not trying to show off our editing skills". Make it look like a non-professional editor or ordinary Instagram poster made it.
- Peekaboo Pillow ads are made to look like they were edited by moms and posted as Instagram Reels.
- Use trendy music.
- Use Instagram-style captions that look like someone just typed them on their phone.
- Sometimes make the ads slightly imperfect when editing, because that makes them feel real.
- Why it works: an ad-looking video raises guards. Content-looking video doesn't trigger "ignore it mode". A badly made ad with a "sponsored" label gets zapped through.
- Element 1: show your target audience in the most organic way possible, with someone who looks achievable.
  - Burn (fitness) ad: a lady in her 40s working out at home, not a very young, very fit, sexy model.
  - Not someone unrealistic for the audience. Find the "perfect middle", a person the target audience sees as achievable.
  - Peekaboo: real moms with their babies, shot raw and authentic at home, like a mom would post.
- Element 2: the copy on the ad. The instructor says it's not really the editor's job. The sentence is cut off at the end of the window.
- No numeric specs (durations, font sizes, dB, zoom %) are given in this window.
