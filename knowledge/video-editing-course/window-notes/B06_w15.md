## What is taught
The editor finishes the hook-variation workflow. He duplicates the sequence, then Alt-drags each new hook image onto the timeline to replace the previous one. After that he covers the final layer, sound effects. SFX pull attention to a specific action and make it feel impactful. In this ad he used only one SFX, a pop on the prolactin graph reveal, and he made it sound natural with room reverb.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Hook swap in Premiere (shown by the editor):
  - Duplicate the sequence (BREAKDOWN V2 → renamed BREAKDOWN V3).
  - Drag the new hook PNG (HOOK 2 or HOOK 3) onto the timeline over the existing hook image.
  - Hold Alt (Option on Mac) while hovering until the clip highlights blue, then release. The new hook replaces the old one instead of stacking on top.
  - Repeat for each version (hook 1 → 2 → 3).
- Safe zones: the new hook text sat too close to the edges of the safe-zone overlay, so he scaled it down slightly until it was readable. Scale on the hook image is 67.8 in the Effect Controls (frame at 45:00).
- Sound effects:
  - Their purpose is to draw attention to a specific action. His example is a WWE ladder jump with a bone-crushing sound, which makes the impact felt.
  - He used only one SFX in the whole ad: a "pop" on the graph appearing.
  - Source: Premiere Composer (free version), Starter Pack → Sounds → Pop → "Hollow Pop 06" (the other option is "Small Pop 01").
  - Make the SFX natural: open Essential Sounds, tag the clip as SFX, then open Creative → Reverb and choose the "Room Reverb" preset, because the speaker is indoors.
  - The other presets listed are Heavy Reverb, Light Reverb and Outside Reverb.
  - He found the reverb too strong, so he lowered the clip level and reduced the reverb Amount. The slider reads about 5.0 in the frame at 47:45, but the value is hard to read.
  - He recommends studying sound design separately: "it's an art of its own".
