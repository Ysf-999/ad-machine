## What is taught
The editor fixes a voiceover (VO) track in Premiere Pro by listening to the AI-generated lines. He trims awkward pauses and inhale sounds, and replaces a line that sounds wrong because it has no pause. To regenerate the line he goes to ElevenLabs, generates it again, drops the new file into the timeline, and pastes the old clip's audio attributes onto it. He then smooths the leftover inhale with a fade instead of cutting it out.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Don't delete an inhale breath outright if it creates a needed pause. Keep the pause and add a smooth fade-out so the inhale is no longer audible.
- Remove pauses only where they sound unnecessary. He keeps pauses that were "actually necessary" or "on point."
- If a section sounds good as a whole but one phrase sounds off, usually because it has no pause, regenerate that line rather than patching it. He says regenerating is "not a must-have, but this is the way how I like to go."
- Regeneration workflow:
  1. Edit the script text in ElevenLabs. He splits the line to force a pause: "But here's what happens when you move that clip to the front." and "their own pulling force works against them." as separate lines. The second try was one line: "Every time they try to lunge forward, the front attachment redirects that energy back toward you automatically."
  2. Generate and listen.
  3. Download and name the file by its text ("Just name it as it is").
  4. Remove the previous clip from the timeline.
  5. Insert the new one.
  6. Copy the old clip, then Paste Attributes onto the new clip.
  7. Fix the inhale: add a fade and leave a bit more pause.
- Paste Attributes dialog checkboxes: Scale Attribute Times, Volume, Channel Volume, Panner, and Essential Sound Sections (Dialogue: Enhance Speech, Loudness, Repair). Audio Effects was unticked. The Video Attributes section was greyed out.
