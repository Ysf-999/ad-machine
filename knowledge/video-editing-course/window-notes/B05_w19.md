## What is taught
The instructor styles burned-in captions on an organic/UGC-style dog-leash ad in Premiere Pro. He copies a simple, UGC-looking reference: white text with a black stroke, no shadow, and a larger font size. He then explains that captions should not run as one long line, because viewers skim them without sound and a wide line forces their eyes to travel across the whole screen. The goal is to shorten the line length.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Caption rule #1 is readability. Style the text to match the reference ad: "as UGC as possible, as simple as possible".
- No drop shadow. The instructor says shadows make the look more complicated and cheap, and that no shadow looks "way more premium".
- Add about 5 px of stroke (the screen shows 5.0 px, Outer, black stroke, white fill).
- Stroke line join: choose Round join or Bevel join instead of the default Miter join, which gives sharp corners. Round makes the corners rounder. Bevel is only slightly different, and he sometimes prefers it.
- Font size: 45 looked too small, so he raised it to 50. The font is HeadingNow, 64 Norm Regular.
- Captions should not run in one long line. Viewers' eyes have to travel from the left edge to the right edge, which is "a struggle and a chore".
- About 80% of viewers watch without headphones, so they skim the text. Reduce the caption line length so it does not span the screen.
- Positioning (opening remark): a small offset toward the right side wouldn't be noticeable, but if the text goes almost to the right edge it becomes very noticeable and dramatic. Place it properly first, then style it.
