## What is taught
The instructor walks through the middle of an organic-style dog-harness ad. Each on-screen text line gets b-roll that literally shows the claim ("show, don't tell"), using before/after pairs. For the key claim, "Within minutes most dogs learn to walk beside you", he gives the b-roll a "fake dolly" effect so it stands out, and begins to explain how to build it.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Pair each text claim with b-roll that visually proves it. The clip "speaks for itself", so no extra explanation is needed.
- "Dog pulls" + 👇 (finger-pointing-down emoji) + "Gets redirected back toward YOU":
  - The before clip is a first-person POV of a small dog pulling the owner.
  - The after clip shows the owner unaffected by a big dog.
- "It's not about fighting your dog's friend / It's about REDIRECTING it": a dog pulling on the leash, then an owner walking calmly with the dog.
- "No pain. No discomfort": a smiling, happy, relaxed dog wearing the harness, so the viewer sees "no negative emotions only positive vibes".
- "Dogs simply discover: Pulling doesn't work anymore":
  - Cut from two dogs pulling the owner to a woman casually walking two dogs with the front-clip harness.
  - Choose the two clips so they look like the same park on the same day. It reads as a learning story: swap the harness and the change is immediate.
- "Within MINUTES most dogs learn to walk beside you":
  - He treats this as a really important claim, so the b-roll gets the fake dolly effect to stand out.
  - Dolly definition: the physical camera moves toward the subject while the focal length decreases (zooms out).
  - In his clip the effect is much less intense than the film example. In his shot the subject walks away from the camera.
  - The build starts in Effect Controls. The detailed settings fall after this window.
