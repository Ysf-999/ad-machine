## What is taught
The instructor, working in After Effects, shows how to split a flattened clip into per-cut layers with Scene Edit Detection. He then sets up a safe-zone overlay and builds a white 4:5 rectangle frame inside the 9:16 canvas. He says this "little frame" raises production quality and gives the ad "more class", compared with plain full-bleed cuts.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Scene Edit Detection: right-click the clip layer and choose Scene Edit Detection. In the dialog, choose "Split Layer at Scene Edit" and leave "Create Pre-comps" unchecked. After "Analyzing Clips…", the one layer becomes many layers, one per cut (the layer list reaches 18+ HOLD.mov layers).
- Safe-zone overlay: place the safe-zone PNG on the top layer. Press T for opacity and set it to 20%. The PNG comes in larger than the frame, so fit it to the comp with Ctrl+Alt+Shift+G. Then lock the layer.
- Frame shape: use the Rectangle tool (shortcut Q) and fill it white so it is clearly visible.
- In Shape Properties > Size, click the chain icon (Constrain Proportions) to disable it so the X and Y scale can be set independently.
- Type 1080 (horizontal) and 1350 (vertical). He says this is the 4:5 resolution and roughly where the safe zone sits.
- Center the shape with Align Horizontally and Align Vertically, with Align Layers to: Composition.
- Re-enable the chain, then scale the shape down so everything sits inside the safe zone.
- Leave a small gap ("breathing space") so the frame does not touch or stick to the safe-zone edge.
- The final video uses this frame instead of straight-line cuts to the full 9:16 edge.
