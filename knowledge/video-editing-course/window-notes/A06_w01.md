## What is taught
This window walks through the three UGC ad briefs in a Milanote board used as an editing assessment. It covers how variations are built for each type. UGC #1 varies only the hook and keeps the hold identical. UGC #2 has one spoken line, so the variations are different hook visuals. UGC #3 is a voiceover-driven (non-creator) ad modelled on Dr. Squatch's heavy-b-roll editing style.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Students are told to research UGC ads (e.g. on Atria). The instructor deliberately gave no example ads, so he can judge what students come up with and give feedback.
- **UGC Ad #1 (Salon Quality Hair):** 3 different hooks, then a single shared hold. The only variation is the hook; the rest of the video is identical across variations.
- **UGC Ad #2 (Kids Getting Sick):** a different product and a different creator, with only one hook line delivered. Variation comes from 3 different hook visuals:
  - Hook 1: the creator talking.
  - Hook 2: the creator picking up the package or unboxing.
  - Hook 3: the product missing, or something else.
  - So three hook visuals instead of three hook texts and copy.
- Each ad has a hold laid out, with the script and a lot of custom footage to use as b-roll.
- **UGC Ad #3 (Shady Shilajit):** not true UGC, because no user generated content is involved. It is still a very popular ad format that falls loosely under UGC. It uses an AI voiceover instead of a creator on camera, chosen because that voice was popular.
- The brief for #3 says to go as crazy as possible with the editing, "a bunch of b-roll, make it quick and punchy", and to replicate Dr. Squatch's ads. The instructor praises how they add b-roll so that something changes constantly.
- The on-screen brief for #3 gives 3 hooks + 1 hold = 3 total ads.
