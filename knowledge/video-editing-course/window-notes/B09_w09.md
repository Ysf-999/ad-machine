## What is taught
The instructor finishes the b-roll layer on the three hook variants of a runners/athletes ad for a pet-style "Flow Pouch" product. He then moves to the next layer of his workflow, Animations/Transitions. He demonstrates how to add a zoom-in across several consecutive still images with a single adjustment layer holding a Transform effect, instead of keyframing each clip's scale separately.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Hook b-roll matching:
  - Hook 1: Chinese runner before the event starts, then the athlete in action.
  - Hook 2 ("How China shattered three world records"): clips of Chinese athletes running.
  - Hook 3 ("The truth behind China's 1993 doping scandal"): a clip of the Chinese runner overtaking almost everyone.
- After the b-roll is set up, review it again for weak points where viewers might click off. Swap clips or animate something there. Fixing weaknesses at the b-roll layer makes later layers easier.
- Animations/Transitions layer: add zoom effects and animate motion to keep the video engaging.
- Two ways to zoom a still:
  1. Animate Scale in Effect Controls on the clip itself.
  2. Use an adjustment layer. This is needed when several images should zoom together, because per-clip scaling can't be matched in speed across three images.
- Adjustment layer behavior: it applies its effects to every track below it. In the example it sits on video track 5, so it affects V4, V3, V2 and V1. It does not affect V6, V7, V8 and above.
- Zoom recipe: add the Transform effect to the adjustment layer (drag it on, or select and double-click). Animate Scale from 100 to about 112.
- Create the adjustment layer from the Project panel's New Item button → Adjustment Layer, then drag it onto the timeline.
