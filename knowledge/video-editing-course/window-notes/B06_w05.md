## What is taught
How to tighten a raw UGC talking-head clip by removing dead air. The instructor cuts the silent gaps between sentences, breaths and filler words ("uh", "um"), and drops weaker alternate takes of the hook. The aim is a fast, dense delivery for people scrolling a phone. The clip is about a minute long and the work is done in Premiere Pro using the waveform.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Gaps visible in the waveform between phrases should be removed. "They're not helping anyone." Scrollers "don't have time for that."
- Main task: take out silences and filler words ("uh", "um"). The instructor calls these "your main task."
- Breathing pauses count as silence to trim.
- Creators often record the hook two or more times. Listen to each take and keep the best-sounding one.
- On long videos, cut from the back: start at the end and work toward the start. This keeps earlier timeline positions from shifting as you ripple-delete.
- Don't trim too tight. The instructor's first attempt ("Can we stop with the… getting my body back narrative") was "a bit too much," so he backed off to a slightly looser cut, which he then called "a lot better." Leave a small natural breath so it doesn't sound clipped.
- Premiere shortcuts he uses (his custom mapping):
  - Q: ripple-trims everything to the left of the playhead.
  - Shift-T: cuts at the playhead. He also mentions "Shift-C" when describing the same step, and the transcript is unclear on which is meant.
  - Workflow: put a cut at the end of the silence, move the playhead to the start of the next waveform burst, and press Q to remove the gap.
- Sample sentence used: "Can we stop with the getting my body back narrative for a second? Because that's not what this is about. I'm 18 months postpartum with my second baby and everybody keeps on asking me when."
