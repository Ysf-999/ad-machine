## What is taught
The instructor explains UGC ad structure as intentional persuasion, not casual content. He breaks a common, simple UGC script into eight beats: hook, problem, twist the knife, product intro, feature/benefit, bad alternative, results, CTA. He explains the purpose of each beat. The CTA explanation is cut off at the end of this window.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Full structure, in order: Hook → Problem → Twist the knife → Product intro → Feature/Benefit → Bad alternative → Results → CTA.
- **Hook**: it is the scroll stopper. It must grab attention and qualify the viewer, either by calling out the target audience or by saying something that makes them think "this video is made for me."
- **Problem**: state the main issue the product solves. Describe it exactly as the customer would, in their language and with their frustrations, so it feels real and authentic.
- **Twist the knife**: dig into the emotional impact of the problem and show you understand how it affects their life. Make them realize how big the problem is and make them a bit fearful.
- **Product intro**: the product is the solution and must feel like the natural next step, an "aha moment" in a story, never forced.
- **Feature/Benefit**: don't just list features. Tie every feature to a specific benefit by answering "why should they care and how does it make their life better?"
- **Bad alternative**: address what they have already tried, the failed past solutions. Show why other solutions fall short without necessarily bashing competitors directly, since sometimes you can't. Acknowledging their experience builds credibility and trust.
- **Results**: show proof. This can be personal results, before/afters or customer results. It must be specific and believable.
- **CTA**: the explanation is cut off at the window end.
- No durations, zoom values, fonts or dB levels are given in this window.
