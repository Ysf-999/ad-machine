## What is taught
The instructor wraps up the hooks lesson. A winning hook becomes the new baseline, and you test new variations against it. A killer hook is a scroll-stopping visual that is instantly clear to the right audience, supported by short, readable text that gives context. Decide with test data, not gut feeling or competitors.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Once a hook wins, keep testing. Use it as the baseline and test new variations against it.
  - Example: if before/after wins, make three other before/after hooks. A later "hook six" might beat the original.
- Treat every ad launch as a controlled experiment that teaches something about the audience.
- The visual has to stop the scroll, but with the right attention, not clickbait. It must be instantly clear to the target customer: "no confusion, no chaos."
- Support the visual with strategic text:
  - Not a novel.
  - Not fancy animated text.
  - Clear, readable text that gives context and says why to keep watching.
- Visual = stops the scroll. Text = context that keeps them watching. This gives "two hooks in one hook."
- Text is not always required in a hook, but it has worked well for them.
- Testing method: change the visual and keep the text the same, or change the message and keep the visual the same. Change one variable at a time.
- Don't rely on gut feeling or competitors. Let performance data decide.
- Ego warning: gut feeling is wrong more often than right. After spending over $20M on ads, they found that some of their dumbest ideas crushed it.
