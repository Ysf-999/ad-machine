## Concrete rules and numbers
- Before editing, work out what the brief requires of you. Don't read all of it; look at the colour-coded comments (pink, green, orange) that carry the copywriter's visual notes.
- Reference ad: 0:42 long. Voiceover is an AI-style female voice. The script ends with the CTA "Get yours at dogfriendlyco.com".
- The reference ad's pacing is "artificially boosted" by the music. A rock-ish track pushes the viewer to absorb information faster, which makes the ad feel dynamic and engaging. Choose music style deliberately for energy.
- The brief maps each script line to a visual, and each line gets one clear visual idea:
  - Pink: "back clip closeup / red arrow to it – to highlight it", attached to "and that back clip harness is making it worse".
  - Green: "dramatic drag clip", attached to "lets them drag you down the street".
  - Orange: "demo this mechanism", attached to "Every time they try to lunge forward, the front attachment redirects that energy back toward you automatically".
- The brand's single mechanism is the front-bearing attachment that redirects the dog's momentum when it pulls. Ads should force-show this mechanism.
- Folder workflow:
  - Create a new folder for the project.
  - Name it exactly as the brief/batch is named.
  - Right-click and pin it to Quick Access so it sits in the taskbar/sidebar.
- The brief's structure is Hook 1/2/3 (each with copy and visuals) feeding into a single "HOLD 1" (the body script, AI VO + copy: female, generic), then a CTA (2 cards).
- Brief hooks:
  - Hook 1: "This 1 change stops pulling forever", visuals "similar to OG ad".
  - Hook 2: "Watch me outsmart my dog", "same idea as V1 but diff clip".
  - Hook 3: "Watch my dog train himself", "same idea as V2 – if the dog is wearing pink/has feminine name visible – please swap to 'herself'".
