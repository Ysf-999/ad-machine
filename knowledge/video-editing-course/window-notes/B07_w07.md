## What is taught
The instructor explains why the organic ad uses clips from a single creator only: it should look as if she filmed and edited it herself, and mixing creators could make viewers skeptical or confused and cause them to scroll away. He then shows how he organizes b-roll with "pancake" editing, where the b-roll sequence is stacked above the main edit in Premiere Pro. He covers two ways to pull b-roll into the edit, then starts breaking down the opening split-screen b-roll.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- For an organic ad, use one creator's clips only, so it feels self-shot and self-edited. Reason given: another creator "might get skeptical or even confused", which can cause doom-scrolling away. He chose this because he knew the client's content library well enough to trust one creator would cover it.
- Keep b-roll in a separate sequence, named "pancake". Pancake editing means two timelines stacked on top of each other: the b-roll sequence on top, the main edit below.
- Method 1, drag and drop from the pancake timeline:
  - Skim the b-roll timeline, find a clip, and drag it into the main sequence.
  - A clip with audio brings its audio along. Toggle off Linked Selection first, so selecting picks video only and only video is dragged in.
- Method 2, source monitor with in/out points:
  - Put the playhead over the b-roll clip and press F (match frame) to load it into the source monitor.
  - Press I to set the in point and O to set the out point.
  - Use the drag-video-only button in the source monitor, which gives a clip that is already cut, and drop it on the timeline.
- He says choosing between the two methods is a matter of preference.
- The hook opens with a split screen "to show the difference right at the start". The split must be coherent with the line spoken. The spoken hook line is "First off. My dog suddenly stopped yanking on the lead."
