## What is taught
The hook decides whether the rest of the ad gets seen, so the first ~3 seconds (maybe less) must stop the scroll. The instructor starts on visual hooks with two parts: composition (one obvious focal point, readable text) and context (the viewer understands the video within half a second). Viewers scan rather than watch, so the hook can't make them think.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Hook window: about 3 seconds, "maybe even less nowadays", to stop the scroll.
- A strong demo at second 10 or a strong testimonial at second 30 is worthless if the hook fails, because nobody reaches them.
- Composition in the first 3 seconds: the viewer must immediately understand what they are looking at.
- Assume the phone is held about half a meter from the face. So:
  - the hook must be obvious,
  - text must be readable at that distance,
  - there must be one clear focal point.
- Don't confuse the viewer. Confusion causes a scroll-away because people don't want to think.
- Context: a viewer scanning for about 0.5 seconds should understand what is going on.
- People scan, they don't watch or read. Large chunks of text get skipped, so the hook must be scannable.
- Context can come from big, bold, easy-to-read text at the top of the screen, called "hook text".
- Most of the time the best context is a super clear visual that tells the story immediately. His example is someone throwing a phone against a wall, which needs no text.
- Never make people work to understand what they're looking at. The moment they stop to think, they scroll.
- Common mistake: spending hours on product shots, script and structure, then adding a basic hook.
