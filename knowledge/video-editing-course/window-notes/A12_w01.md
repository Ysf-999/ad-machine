## What is taught
This window finishes the open-loop idea. Stack impossible-seeming results and hold back the product reveal, so the viewer is invested and hopeful by the time the solution appears. Showing the product too early triggers skeptic mode. It then moves into "how to edit like Mr. Beast". Energy and pacing must be sustained through the whole ad, not only the hook. Editing principle #1 is visual variety and pacing, and the lesson starts listing the cadence of changes.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Do not reveal the product early in the ad. Early product exposure puts the viewer in "skeptic mode" ("just another supplement / scammy weight loss program").
- Open loops first: stack results that seem impossible. The example is losing a lot of weight "while eating whatever they want in just 90 days, and it works for everyone".
- The loop makes the viewer move from "no way this works" to "what if this is the one thing I've been missing?" That builds hope, so they are "waiting for the answer" when the product is revealed.
- Don't front-load effort: a dynamic, fast-paced hook with a bunch of clips, followed by energy drops and weird pacing, loses trust. Viewers notice if the first 3 seconds got all the effort and the rest was "half-assed", and then they leave.
- Be "obsessive about every frame" of the ad, as Mr. Beast's team is about every second.
- Principle #1, visual variety and pacing: "never let your eyes get comfortable". Mr. Beast's cadence:
  - a new camera angle every 3–5 seconds
  - a new scene or development every 10 seconds
  - a new mini story or similar beat every 20–30 seconds (the sentence is cut off at the window end)
