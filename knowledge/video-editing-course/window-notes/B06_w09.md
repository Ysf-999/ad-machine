## What is taught
In UGC and organic ads, keep b-roll to the same creator instead of cutting to a random stranger. This reads as one person's personal journey and is more authentic, and the instructor's agency has seen better results with it. B-roll should match what the audience is thinking at that moment in the script. When a creator's supplied b-roll is thin, pull clips from her other content, such as an older batch. Show a transformation as before and after. Keep the look raw, with at most a few stock clips. The window ends by introducing "animations and transitions" as the next topic.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Use the same person's footage as b-roll throughout a UGC or organic ad. Avoid cutting to another random woman.
- Match b-roll to the spoken line. Example: when she says "calories in, calories out," cut to b-roll of her eating. When she talks about the product, cut to the product b-roll provided.
- If the supplied b-roll is limited, reuse the same creator's clips from other ads. In this ad, the calories line used an older clip, probably from the "gummies" batch (the same person, a different ad batch).
- Transformation structure:
  - Show the problem state early (the postpartum, pregnant-looking body).
  - Pay it off later with the "after" clip over the line "Four months later. And I don't want my old body. I want this body to feel strong, energetic, and comfortable."
  - The "before" appears early in the ad and the "after" appears near the end, so the product is shown to produce the change.
- Choose b-roll by asking what the audience is thinking at each point in the video.
- Stock clips are acceptable in small numbers ("a few"). Don't overdo it. Keep the ad raw and authentic.
- The instructor puts b-roll throughout the clips, not just in one spot.
- No durations, zoom values or dB values are given in this window.
