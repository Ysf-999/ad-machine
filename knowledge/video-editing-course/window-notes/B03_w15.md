## What is taught
The instructor cleans up the voiceover (VO) audio of an organic ad inside Premiere Pro. He cuts out dead air and over-long pauses, then hides every join with short audio crossfades, so the VO sounds like one smooth continuous take. He also trims and fades out breath (inhale) sounds that land just before the CTA, and leaves a slightly longer pause there.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Add fade-ins and fade-outs at the edges of VO clips so the joins are smooth.
- If a pause is "a bit too long", cut roughly half of it. This is judged by ear and is not a fixed duration.
- Build the crossfade by dragging the right clip's edge left so it overlaps the previous clip. Premiere then creates the transition automatically (described as "holding the right side of the audio and shifting it to the left").
- The visible audio transition tooltip reads "Duration: 00:00:00:01, Fade Cosine Value: 8". That is about 1 frame at the sequence frame rate, and the type is a constant/cosine-style fade (the exact transition name is unclear).
- Later the crossfade is stretched wider, to roughly 10–15 frames judging by the timeline ruler, until it sounds natural. This is an estimate from the frames, not a stated number.
- Check the join by listening. The instructor says: "it sounds really good and I don't need to touch it anymore."
- Before the CTA, add a small extra pause. A breath/inhale sound sits there, so use a fade-out transition that covers the inhale so it does not appear in the final audio.
- Insert a little more silence before the CTA phrase ("Shop the sale before redirecting it"). The instructor says "just a bit more of a pause won't make a big deal."
- Trim by edge-dragging. One trim readout shows "+00:00:00:01 Duration 00:00:03:16".
- The VO is assembled from separate generated or recorded mp3 clips, placed on one track (A1): hook clips (h1/h2/h3 hook dogs), "hold dogs", "cta", "Every time they try to lunge forward…", and "So you're stopping their pulling without pain".
- The instructor says these audio-transition features need Premiere Pro 2025. He believes they are not in 2023 and is unsure about 2024.
