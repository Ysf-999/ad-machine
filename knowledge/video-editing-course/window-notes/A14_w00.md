## What is taught
The lesson introduces music and sound effects in ads: which SFX to use, which music to use, when, and how music triggers emotional and psychological responses. The instructor plays reference ads and comments on them. The first example is a Dr. Squatch "build your own bundle" ad that has no music and relies entirely on sound effects synced to the on-screen action and voiceover.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Dr. Squatch ad: runtime is 00:41. It has no music, only sound effects. The instructor calls it a "very classic example" of SFX use in ads.
- Sync SFX to the visual action. Example: a key enters a lock and a lock/key sound plays.
- Use a cash sound effect when the voiceover mentions money or savings ("up to 15% off", the money-bag graphic).
- When the voiceover lists bundle combinations, layer SFX that rise in pitch ("going higher and higher") with each added item. This emphasizes "you can choose so many things".
- Each new item in the list gets its own SFX hit, and the hits escalate as more items are added.
- Humor: the instructor cites research his team found, "90% of people are more likely to remember funny ads" than other ads. His advice is to make more funny ads.
- The Dr. Squatch ad library is recommended as an inspiration source when out of ideas.
