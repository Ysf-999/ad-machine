## What is taught
This is the assignment brief for a VSL (long-form video sales letter) ad. The student edits a real client VSL (Warroad hockey neck guard, product "TILO") from a script, an AI voiceover and a footage folder. The instructor deliberately gives little guidance so the student has to invent the visuals. After the student submits, the instructor sends back the agency editor's version to compare against. No editing technique is demonstrated in this window.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Deliverable: 3 total ads exported (3 hooks, 1 hold, 2 CTAs; the board's "Variations needed" card reads "3# hooks / 1# holds / 2# CTA / 3# total ads").
- Hook 1 uses CTA for Hook 1. Hooks 2 and 3 share the "CTA for Hook 2 & 3", because the context differs.
- Hook copy on the board:
  - Hook 1: "Top 3 Neck Guard Mistakes Hockey Parents Make"
  - Hook 2: "3 big mistakes hockey players make when choosing a neck guard"
  - Hook 3: "Avoid these 3 big mistakes when choosing a neck guard"
- Provided assets:
  - Google Drive with footage (not yet uploaded at recording time).
  - Brand assets: logo, brand guidelines.
  - Brand name and product page link.
  - Full script.
  - AI voiceover files: separate hook VOs, plus one VO for the whole script, titled "3 Big Neck Guards Fails".
- The script includes some visual suggestions, but they are sparse by design.
- Instructor's view: VSLs are tedious and a lot of work, but being able to make good ones is a separate skill and valuable for a portfolio.
- The agency's edited version was loved by the client but the ad did not perform well. The instructor says the editing itself is how a good VSL should be edited.
