## Concrete rules and numbers
- Emphasized moments (visuals, animations, "pop") must still be simple enough that "a fifth grader could understand it".
- Do not animate every word or every part of the ad. Editors who spend hours doing this "think it'll help retention", but "it's just working backwards".
- Animate only: key points, statistics, main benefits. Baseline captions carry everything else.
- Match the editing style to the audience. His example contrasts a joint-health supplement for a 60-year-old with a pre-workout for a 25-year-old gym bro. He implies they should not be edited the same way but gives no specific parameters.
- Assignment brief: a real VSL project for a hockey protective-gear client. Provided assets are the actual VSL script, a professional voiceover track, product shots, and custom hockey footage.
- An inspiration board of already-edited VSLs is also provided, similar to the UGC one. He wrote notes on what he likes and dislikes about each.
- After submission he shows how an agency editor handled the same VSL, for comparison.
- Time estimate: the project may take "seven, eight, nine, 10 hours" or a few days, especially for a first VSL. He tells students not to get discouraged.
