## What is taught
This is the end of a breakdown of an organic-style (UGC look) dog-harness ad built in Premiere Pro. It covers the b-roll choices for the three hooks, then the animation/transition, caption/text and SFX layers, which are kept very simple on purpose. The voiceover versions reuse the same b-roll, with only a conversational voiceover added. The instructor ends with export advice.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Hook 1 b-roll: shows the attachment mechanism but not how it works, so viewers wonder "how can this stop my dog from pulling?" (curiosity gap).
- Voiceover versions: b-roll stays the same. The only change is a conversational-tone voiceover. The ad idea is otherwise identical.
- Hook 2 b-roll order:
  1. Owner attaches the front D-ring.
  2. She shows the bungee leash.
  3. The product is shown in action.
  - The hook was reused because a UGC ad that was performing really well used it ("it kind of ripped").
- Hook 3 is the same idea, with the on-screen line "If you have a big dog, I need to show you this…". Its b-roll order:
  1. Owner puts the harness on the dog.
  2. Back to attaching the D-ring.
  3. Bungee leash.
  4. Product in action.
- Animation/transition: only the dolly effect discussed earlier. The reason given is to stay simple and digestible, with nothing distracting from reading.
- Captions/text: essentially none beyond the text-on-screen a-roll. The a-roll is itself text.
- SFX: used only on the hook clip, to grab attention and emphasise the action. The sound came embedded in the original clip, so the editor dragged its audio down with it and added nothing else.
- Workflow end: make variations, export, and double-check that the safe-zone overlay is turned off before export.
