## What is taught
The instructor gives the build order for a VSL (long-form video sales letter). First, fix the audio of the main content (talking head or voiceover), because everything else sits on top of it. Second, cut silences, bad words and bad takes. Third, add B-roll that illustrates what is being said. Fourth, add text and graphics, starting with consistent baseline captions and then emphasis graphics for key moments. The window ends mid-sentence on the key-moment text.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Main content (A-roll or voiceover) is the backbone. Fix its audio before anything else. Bad audio ("recorded in a washing machine") ruins the whole piece no matter how good the B-roll is.
- Then remove all silences, bad words and bad takes until the delivery is clear and flows smoothly.
- Only after that, add B-roll. B-roll must amplify what is being said, so match the footage to each script beat. His examples use a sleep supplement:
  - Sleep deprivation: someone missing their alarm, or looking exhausted at work.
  - Japanese sleep researcher: footage that feels authentic to Japan.
  - Ancient herb: exotic locations, traditional methods, herb processing.
  - Customer results: real people and real transformations. These can be ugly and unedited, because that is what makes them authentic.
- Text and graphics give context to anything in the script. Most people either use none or turn the VSL into "a PowerPoint presentation".
- Baseline captions:
  - Consistent throughout the whole video, in the same position.
  - Position is usually bottom center or slightly above.
  - Clear and readable, with no fancy fonts.
  - Use a solid background or a drop shadow so they are visible on any footage.
  - Size must be readable on a phone. If it is not readable on a phone, redo it.
- Key moments (shocking statistics, crucial points) get extra emphasis on top of the baseline captions. The detail comes after this window.
- No numeric values (font size, stroke, dB, durations) are given in this window.
