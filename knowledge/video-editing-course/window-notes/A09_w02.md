## What is taught
The instructor explains that high-production ads should match what the brand needs. A tech product (his example is an electric toothbrush) wants more dynamic editing, motion graphics and energy. A luxury watch wants perfect, cinematic, slow-motion, moody cuts. He says editing high-production footage is the same as clipping UGC or organic ads together, only with more premium footage. He then introduces a practice assignment and recaps the four ad types in the module.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- No numbers (durations, zoom %, dB, fonts) are given in this window.
- Tech or startup brand: use more dynamic editing, more motion graphics and more energy. He points to Apple and Samsung ads on YouTube as references ("type Apple ads").
- Luxury brand (watch): cuts and edits must be perfect and cinematic, with luxurious slow motion and a moody vibe.
- Match the editing style to what the brand needs. Do not over-complicate high-production footage. The workflow is the same as clipping footage together for UGC or organic, just with premium footage.
- Use high-production content strategically, for impact and to position the brand where you want it.
- Assignment: the student gets a creative brief, raw studio footage and the ad copy. The goal is a premium ad that positions the brand as an authority in the market.
- After the student submits, the instructor walks through how he edited the original. That ad "did okay" and got some performance, but it was not a scaling winner.
- Module recap of ad types:
  - Organic ads blend in and convert.
  - UGC builds trust and drives purchases.
  - VSLs tell complete stories and convince people to buy.
  - High production is the last type covered.
