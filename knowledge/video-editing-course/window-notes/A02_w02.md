## What is taught
The instructor finishes the three organic ad types with lifestyle content, then explains which type to choose. The choice depends on the customer: testimonials build trust, social-media style fits platform-native niches, and lifestyle sells an aesthetic. Across all three, the ad should look made by a person, not by a brand or a professional editor. Copy should also sound like a person speaking.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- No numeric editing specs (durations, zoom, dB, fonts) are given in this window.
- Copy voice: write as the person, not the brand.
  - Bad: "try our revolutionary peekaboo pillow today"
  - Good: "I finally found something that helps my baby with tummy time"
- Lifestyle content sells a vibe or aesthetic, not just the product.
  - Example: a jewelry brand that was really selling "main character energy".
  - Place the product in beautiful, Instagram-worthy moments and in an environment the customer would love to be in.
- To edit lifestyle ads, find lifestyle or luxury influencers in the niche, study how they edit, and replicate it.
- Choosing the type:
  - **Customer testimonials:** use when you need to build trust, the product solves a specific problem, and real customers have genuine success stories. The testimonials should be raw, natural and unscripted. Take them and run them as the ad.
  - **Social media style:** use when the audience is active on social media (example: women 25 to 45) and the niche has clear content trends (example: fitness, with many similar-format "get fit at home" tip videos). Make ads that look exactly like those trends and feel native, organic and not like ads.
  - **Lifestyle:** use when the lifestyle around the product matters more than the product itself. The sentence is cut off at 09:00.
- Key principle: the ads should look edited by regular people, not by professional advertisers or editors.
