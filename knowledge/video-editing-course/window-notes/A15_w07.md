## What is taught
How the instructor sources music for ads. For trending or popular tracks, he finds a TikTok, Instagram or YouTube version, grabs the link, and rips the audio with a YouTube downloader. For stock music and sound effects he uses Artlist. He also covers how to identify unknown "original sound" tracks and how to avoid copyright flags on Meta.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Workflow for trending music:
  - Search YouTube for the song plus "tiktok version" (example on screen: "aces by dkj tiktok version"; the transcript spells it "aces").
  - Open the video and copy the link.
  - Paste it into y2mate (the YouTube downloader shown earlier), go to the Audio tab, and download.
  - Screen shows the output as MP3 – 128kbps, 1.9 MB.
- If a TikTok or Reel just says "original sound", use Shazam on a phone: play the sound, hit Shazam, get the song name, then repeat the same YouTube search and download.
- Avoid downloading original versions of popular artists' songs (example: The Weeknd) because they get flagged more easily.
  - Look for the TikTok version, slowed version, reverb version, or other slightly edited versions.
  - These are "not as easily flagged by Facebook's algorithm". He calls this a hack that "has been working well so far".
- Stock music and SFX come from Artlist.
  - The Music section filters by genre, instrument, mood and video theme. Preview tracks, pick one that fits the ad, and download it.
  - There is a separate Sound Effects section, which you search.
  - He may share a pack of top stock music used for ads below the video.
