## What is taught
The instructor tightens the voiceover audio on the hook of an organic ad, working in Premiere Pro on an audio-only timeline. He trims the pauses between phrases to roughly half their length and keeps some pause so the line still breathes. He cuts out an inhale breath and uses short audio fades at the cut edges so the join sounds smooth.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Tool: use Track Select Forward to grab everything after a point and drag it earlier. This closes the gap and ripples all later clips.
- Hook pacing: if the pause after the hook line ("This one change stops pulling forever") is too long, viewers can't absorb the hook well enough to understand what the video is about. Tighten it.
- Method for pauses: listen first, then remove about half of the pause ("just half"). He does this after the hook, again after "your dog's body is built for pulling", and again around the "harness is making it worse" line.
- Keep some pause. He likes a pause existing at "built for pulling", but cuts it to about half. After re-listening he says "maybe a bit more of the pause here", so judge by ear and don't remove it entirely.
- Breath removal: to remove an inhale but keep the pause, cut at the breath, delete the unwanted part, and leave the silence.
- Smoothing the edit: drag the audio fade handles on both sides of the cut. This gives a smooth fade-in on the incoming clip and a smooth fade-out on the outgoing clip. At 32:45 the timeline is zoomed to about frame level (about 06:10 to 06:25) and a short fade curve is visible on the incoming clip.
- Always re-listen after each edit and check that the line "connects right to" the next one.
- Voice clip volume in Properties is about 12.0 dB for the "hold dogs.mp3" clips and 13.0 dB for the "h1 hook dogs.mp3" clip.
