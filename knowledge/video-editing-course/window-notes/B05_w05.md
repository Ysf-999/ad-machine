## What is taught
The editor builds the "hold" section of an organic ad in Premiere Pro. The voiceover line "creating a perfect lever system that lets them drag you down the street" needs more visuals. He splits the long clip so a new b-roll shot appears about every 3 seconds, which keeps retention up. He then sources a "dramatic drag clip" from TikTok by searching, and rejects one clip because the dog is too big and doesn't fit the brand's premium look.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Keep the dog visible from the start to the end of the hold section.
- If one visual runs about 3+ seconds, it is too long for retention. Divide the segment by two and insert two different clips.
- Track which stock clips are already used (e.g. "we use this clip as well") and pick unused ones. He searched the stock folder for "pulling" and chose an unused clip ("if your dog pulls like a freight train").
- Time new clips to the voiceover: the new clip starts right after the word "system" and covers "that lets them drag you down the street."
- Scale clips up to fill the 9:16 frame. A clip is set to a 1080×1920 sequence; the Position is 540/960 on screen.
- Trimmed clip durations on screen are about 1:22 to 1:24 (source timecode), and the tooltip showed a trim of +2 frames.
- Search by natural terms, not by the script note. Don't type "dramatic drag clip" literally; type "dog drag" or "dog drags owner".
- Reject clips when:
  - the dog is too big, too massive and too strong, so few viewers relate to it ("what percentage of people have this big dog?");
  - the clip clashes with the brand's premium style (Dog Friendly Co).
- The clip should still show the owner clearly being dragged and the dog clearly pulling.
