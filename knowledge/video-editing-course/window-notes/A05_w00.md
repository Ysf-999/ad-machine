## What is taught
This is the conceptual intro to UGC (user generated content) ads. The instructor says that once you can make good UGC ads you can make any other ad type, and that UGC is the most common and most in-demand type. He explains why it took over from polished branded ads. Customers trust other people more than brands, so brands began paying creators to make strategic product videos. He separates organic content (casual, no agenda) from UGC (authentic but with a strategic persuasion layer).

## Concrete rules and numbers
- No numbers, durations, zoom values, caption specs or dB levels are given in this window.
- Branded ads, the pre-UGC style: product shots, basic background music, and text showing benefits or features. He calls them boring and cookie-cutter, with no personality.
- UGC definition: brands reach out to content creators, send them product, and pay them to record a video talking about it.
- Organic vs UGC:
  - Organic is a casual mention with no agenda and no attempt to convince.
  - UGC is genuinely loved but strategic. It lays out "every single reason" the viewer needs the product, like convincing a friend to buy a game by listing graphics, storyline and multiplayer.
- Takeaway for the agent: a UGC ad should feel like a real person's honest recommendation and also be built to persuade, with a deliberate reason-by-reason argument. It should not read as a polished brand spot.
- Social proof is the underlying principle. People check reviews before buying and trust other people over brands.
