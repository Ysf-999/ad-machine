## What is taught
The instructor builds a 2x2 video collage in Premiere Pro, in a nested sequence named "collage". Four vertical clips sit on V1–V4. He scales each clip to 50% and positions it with simple half-width and half-height arithmetic so each fills one quadrant of the 1080x1920 frame. The collage is then used inside the main sequence (h1) as a short cutaway.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- The frame is 1080x1920 (vertical). Source clips are 100% scale with position 540, 960 (the frame centre) and anchor 540, 960.
- To fill one quarter of the frame, set Scale to 50%. Each clip's position is then offset by half the frame dimensions.
- Position values for the quadrants:
  - Top-left: X 270, Y 480.
  - Top-right: X 270 + 540 = 810, Y 480.
  - Bottom-left: X 270, Y 480 + 960 = 1440.
  - Bottom-right: X 810, Y 1440.
- The instructor says X "540 plus 270" and Y "plus 480". He also says "plus plus 480" for the bottom row, which is meant as +960. The numbers above are derived from the frame dimensions.
- The first clip is at 50% scale with position 460→270 X, 960→480 Y.
- Some clips are not at 50%. IMG_1089.MOV has scale 25%, anchor 1080 X / 1920 Y, and position 540→810 X, 960 Y. So its source media is larger (about 2x), and 25% on it gives the same 50% visual size. Position then goes to 810.
- The instructor's rule: "if scale is 50%, I need half of it, 25%" for the larger clip. This is the same mental math.
- Project guides and the Program monitor grid show the quadrant lines. The centre cross-lines are in red/teal.
- Each clip's scale must be matched to its source resolution. The instructor notes "one extra frame" appears with the first quartile, and he fixes it.
