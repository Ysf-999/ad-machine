## What is taught
The instructor breaks down a 25-second Oats Overnight ad to show music and sound effects used for tension and release. Chill, wholesome music plays at the start. Fast, upbeat music carries the production/explanation section. The music is cut out completely on the key lines ("our customers", the customer's "that's delicious") so those lines land harder, then it comes back in. He says the same script and voiceover would feel completely different with different music and SFX.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Build tension in the ad: the first ~2–5 seconds use calm, wholesome, "chill" background music.
- After the hook, switch to faster-paced music as the video moves into the explanation/production section, where a lot is happening visually (cooking, lab, packing).
- Match music energy to visual activity: upbeat music over lab/production footage.
- Pause or drop the music completely right before or on the line you want to emphasize (e.g. "our customers"). The silence puts all the focus on that one line of the script.
- Restart the music after the emphasized line and let the script continue.
- Repeat the pattern: upbeat music over process footage, then stop the music again for the next key moment.
- Final payoff: music stops, there is a build of tension, then the customer says "that's delicious" with no music and no supporting sounds, because that is the line the viewer should focus on.
- Music comes back in for the CTA ("Try it out at oatsovernight.com"). The ad ends at about 25 s.
- Summary: the ad builds tension and releases it throughout. Music and SFX make people feel something.
- The instructor gives no dB levels, fade lengths or SFX names in this window.
