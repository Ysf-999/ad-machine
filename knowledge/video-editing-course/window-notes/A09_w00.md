## What is taught
This is the strategic intro to high-production ads, not an editing walkthrough. The instructor argues that premium, cinematic ads make the brand look like the market leader, because viewers equate better production with a better product at the same price. He adds that mediocre high production is worse than good organic/UGC. Premium ads are useful as extra touchpoints in a multi-step customer journey (the window ends just as he starts describing this).

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Premium production is a positioning tool: it makes the brand look like "the premium choice, the market leader" and makes competitors look "amateur and cheap".
- Example: with two brands at the same price, viewers pick the one with the better-looking ad.
  - Brand A runs typical UGC: a random person, basic product features, a discount code at the end.
  - Brand B runs cinematic, movie-like footage.
- Quality bar: high production only works if it is very good. "Mediocre high production is actually worse than good organic content."
- Organic/UGC can scale and perform without high production. Premium is an extra asset, not a requirement.
- Customer journey: the ideal is see ad → click → buy. In reality there are usually several touchpoints, e.g. a first organic/UGC ad, then more. The list is cut off at 03:00.
- No editing numbers (durations, zoom %, fonts, dB, etc.) are given in this window.
