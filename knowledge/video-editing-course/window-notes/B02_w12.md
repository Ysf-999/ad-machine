## What is taught
The instructor shows how he built a "friend recommends this product" section in a cup ad. He mined the brand's Instagram, which is full of influencer/UGC content, and found a "day in the life" video in which the creator uses the cup from morning to night. He cut that video into short clips and put time-of-day text overlays on them (6am, 9:30am, 2pm, 7pm). Together these visualise the spoken claim that the product lasts the whole day and keeps drinks hot. If the brand already has UGC or influencer footage, use it. If not, look for it on the brand's social accounts.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Source b-roll from the brand's own Instagram, especially influencer and paid-partnership posts. If the brand already has creator/UGC footage, use that instead of scraping.
- Look for footage that shows the product used across a whole day. Here it ran from morning coffee, to the gym, to work, to home in the evening.
- Match the visual to what the voiceover says. Here the creator talks about a friend recommending the cup, so the day-in-the-life footage plays the friend.
- Cut the long source video into short clips. Each clip shows one moment of product use. The source clips had no text on them.
- Add a time-of-day overlay to each clip to tell the story:
  - 6am: coffee in hand, cup shown
  - 9:30am: in the gym, drinking from the cup
  - 2pm: heading out or to work (the 2pm clip shows her standing, full length, holding the cup)
  - 7pm: back home, still drinking from the cup
- Steaming drink footage is a good way to show the cup preserves temperature. The instructor points this out as a selling point.
- Show the product in the first clip ("perfect we show the cup").
