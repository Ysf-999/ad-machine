## What is taught
The instructor continues his editing tech stack. He covers scraping footage from YouTube (documentaries and the like, downloaded with a web tool) for a niche. He gives the Shilajit brand as an example, where harvesting documentaries were reused in ads with no problems. He says finding clips on TikTok, Instagram and YouTube is a separate skill, built by editing more scripts and hitting the "what clips do I use" problem. He then moves to Envato Elements (paid), a stock footage library that also sells video templates, transitions, camera zoom-ins, titles and other plugin-style assets.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Scrape footage from YouTube for your niche and download it with the listed site (y2mate). The instructor says that in the Shilajit ads, using YouTube documentaries on how the substance is harvested never got them into trouble.
- When stuck on which clips to use, go to TikTok, Instagram or YouTube and search for clips. This is "a separate skill" that improves by editing more videos or scripts, hitting the problem, solving it and remembering what worked.
- Envato Elements is paid. It is a "huge stock footage library" and also offers video templates.
- Video templates can be filtered by application: After Effects, Premiere Pro, Apple Motion, Final Cut Pro, DaVinci Resolve. CapCut is not supported.
- Filtering by Final Cut Pro gives effects, animations, transitions, camera zoom-ins and other plugin-like assets. He says these make editing "faster and easier and more efficient".
