## What is taught
The instructor levels a voiceover-only timeline in Premiere Pro by watching the audio meter and raising any weak clips or phrases. He then does a "third check" by playing the whole VO without looking at the timeline and listening and watching only the volume. He flags phrases that are too loud (for example "Shop the sale" and a passage near "automatically") and trims them back.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Target VO loudness on the meter is about -3 to -6 dB.
- He sometimes allows peaks down to -9 dB so the voice isn't monotone and keeps natural intonation.
- Sections sitting at -6 to -12 dB are too low and need a lift.
- If the meter is reaching -12 or -15 dB, that is the signal to increase volume.
- Raise weak clips "as much as possible", then fine-tune individual phrases by ear. Examples were "for my dog", "immediate for my dog", and "So you're stopping their pulling without pain".
- Don't over-correct. Some tweaks are "just a little bit".
- Over-loud phrases get pulled down. "Shop the sale" was too loud relative to its neighbours, and "automatically" and "without pain" were slightly too loud in the third pass.
- Do a final check pass at least three times, the last one without looking at the timeline and focusing only on the volume.
- Level adjustments are made per phrase or clip, using the clip's volume rubber-band with keyframes. The status bar reads "Add a keyframe when near the connector line" and "Drag to vertically adjust the connector line between keyframes".
