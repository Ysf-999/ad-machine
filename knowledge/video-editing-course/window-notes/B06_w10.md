## What is taught
Cutting out silences and filler words from UGC A-roll leaves some jump cuts that feel abrupt. The fix is to alternate the scale of adjacent clips, so the second clip is slightly punched in compared with the first. This reads as a camera change and the cut feels cleaner and more natural. Don't overdo it, because constant zooming in and out looks funny. Cover bad spots with b-roll, and add a subtle zoom on an important sentence for emphasis.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Alternate scale between neighbouring A-roll clips: the first clip stays at 100, the next goes up (example 110).
- A very abrupt cut got 112.
- Any number works, but the instructor says the practical minimum is about 102, 103 or 106. Smaller values "won't make that big of a difference".
- Don't overdo it. Example of overdoing: one clip at 106 and the next at 112 in sequence, which gets "kind of funny" when zooming in and out repeatedly.
- To get over that overdone look, use b-roll over the awkward cuts ("B-rolls to the rescue").
- Emphasis zoom: on an important sentence in a UGC ad or talking head, add a subtle zoom. The example sentence is "But that doesn't mean I have to accept feeling uncomfortable in it forever", which follows the pain-point section. Zoom set to 112.
- Compare each cut with and without the scale change to check it. The instructor plays each version once to confirm the difference.
