## What is taught
This is the intro to the VSL (video sales letter) module. It covers what VSLs are, when to use them (long-form, education-heavy, older audiences), and why they are hard to edit. The instructor then starts the four elements of a VSL, beginning with the hook, using a sleep-supplement VSL as the running example.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- VSL length: typically 1–15 minutes. Some run 30 minutes or even 1 hour on Facebook and "crush it".
- Best-fit niches: health products, anything needing customer education, problem-solving products, anything aimed at older audiences. Examples named: supplements, weight loss programs, skincare. The general test is "anything where you need to explain a problem and convince people your solution works".
- Older audiences are more willing to watch a 5-minute ad because they are less "brain rotted" from scrolling.
- Editing a VSL is like editing a mini movie. You juggle voiceover, b-roll, animations and text overlays. If any one of these is poor, viewers leave within seconds.
- Most editors won't put in the work to master VSLs, which is why they pay well.
- VSLs work mainly through storytelling.
- Four elements make a VSL. Only the first, the hook, is covered in this window.
- Hook: you have about 3 seconds to grab attention on social media. The VSL itself can run 3, 10 or 15 minutes, so the hook must "hit like a truck".
- Hook formula: take a familiar, relatable thing (can't sleep) and add a shocking twist (your brain is trying to kill you).
- Example hook for the sleep supplement: "What if I told you the reason you can't sleep is because your brain is actually trying to kill you."
