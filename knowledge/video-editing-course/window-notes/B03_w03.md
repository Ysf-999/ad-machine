## What is taught
The instructor sets up the project: a per-ad folder with a default subfolder stack, then starts the first editing layer, the voiceover, in ElevenLabs. He reads the copywriter's brief to see which voice is required (here female, generic style). He pastes the hook copy into a favorite voice, listens to several takes, and downloads the best one into the VO folder.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Pin the project folder to File Explorer Quick Access (drag it to the sidebar) so he can return to it instantly.
- Default folder stack in every ad folder:
  - `stock`: all clips found later that could serve as stock footage.
  - `VO` (voiceover).
  - `music`: added only if more than two music tracks are used in the ad. He normally skips it, but says it keeps the project better structured.
- Voiceover is the first layer of the edit. Do it before anything else.
- Choose the voice from the copywriter's brief. Here the brief says "AI VO + COPY: (female, generic)", so he picks a female generic voice.
- He keeps a shortlist of pre-approved voices in ElevenLabs "Recently used". For generic female he likes a specific one that is "emotional, fun" and useful.
- Workflow: copy the script text, paste it into ElevenLabs, generate, listen, and regenerate if it doesn't sound right. He tried the line "This 1 change stops pulling forever" about 4–5 times, then said "It's good" and ran one more variation.
- Download the take and save it into the ad's `VO` folder, then name the file.
- Voice settings visible on screen for the chosen voice (Brittney – Social Media Voice, Fun, Youthful & Informative):
  - Model: Eleven Multilingual v2.
  - Speed: slider about 55%, a bit above the middle.
  - Stability: about 70%.
  - Similarity: about 50%.
  - Style Exaggeration: about 5%, near none.
  - Speaker boost: on.
