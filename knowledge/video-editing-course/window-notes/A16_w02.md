## What is taught
The instructor demos AI upscaling of low-resolution clips in Topaz Video AI. He shows two examples: a b-roll style action clip (a person kicking a punching bag) and a talking-head clip with a face. Each original is exported at 4x and compared side by side in QuickTime. He says the upscale is a big quality jump, with one caveat about faces that have little detail to model.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Example 1 (action clip):
  - Input is 360x640 @ 30 FPS. Output is 1440x2560 (4x upscale), frame rate kept at 30 FPS (Original).
  - Export took about 18 seconds, and the clip is about 2 s long.
  - Result: much crisper. Background text on the wall and the punching bag are visibly sharper.
  - Downside: the model did not recognise or reconstruct the man's face. The instructor says it "didn't really had a lot to model on", so he can't be mad about it.
- Example 2 (talking head with face):
  - Same input, 360x640 @ 30 FPS, output 1440x2560 (4x).
  - Settings: Enhancement filter on, Video Type Progressive, AI Model Iris (Face / LQ-MQ), Input Condition Low Quality, Parameters Auto.
  - Export format is H264 (mov). Export took about 30 seconds, and the clip is about 3 s long.
  - Result: "crisp and smooth".
- Stabilization, Motion Deblur, Frame Interpolation and Grain are all toggled off.
- Use the Iris model for faces and talking-head footage.
- The instructor says he has used this tool for both slowing down footage and enhancing footage.
- He is running a version about two years old and says it still works well.
- Workflow: load clip, set 4x output, pick model, export, then compare original and upscale side by side before using it.
