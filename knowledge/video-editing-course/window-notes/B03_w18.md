## What is taught
The instructor is mixing the voiceover (VO) in Premiere Pro. He finds VO syllables or words whose waveform peaks are louder than the rest, specifically ones that overreach the −3 dB mark on the meter. He tames them with a dip made of three volume keyframes on the clip's rubber-band line. He then smooths the keyframes with Ctrl-click so the dip fades in and out gently.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Target ceiling: VO peaks should not exceed about −3 dB on the audio meter. Loud peaks "mess with the experience of the viewer."
- Method for a too-loud word:
  - Place 3 volume keyframes on the clip's volume line around the loud part.
  - Drag the middle keyframe down so the loud word matches the overall VO volume height.
  - Ctrl-click the keyframes to switch the interpolation to ease in / ease out, which makes a smoother "U shape."
  - The default linear keyframes make a "triangle" dip, which is less smooth.
  - Ctrl-clicking the keyframe again makes the dip "even more smoother."
- He calls the result a "slopped version," meaning a sloped or curved dip.
- He fixes it word by word: "drag you down the street" and "But here's what happens when you move that clip to the front." Then he moves on to "back towards you automatically," where "automatically" was too loud, and repeats the same dip.
- He re-listens after each fix ("Let's hear how it sounds now").
- Some quieter sections are raised slightly (about "13.46" was mentioned, which seems to be a timeline position and is unclear). One clip's volume tooltip reads "12.6 dB" at 56:15, but that may be a keyframe level display and is unclear.
- Workflow: the VO is cut into separate clips per line, and each clip is balanced on its own.
