## What is taught
The instructor builds the hook of an organic-style dog-leash ad in Premiere. He uses Scene Edit Detection to split a pre-joined raw clip into its two original shots. He then slides the cut to line up with the voiceover words ("pulling", then "stop") and the music beat. He leaves the end of the hook empty so b-roll can fill it. Next he moves to the "Hold 1" section ("Your dog's body is built for pulling"), checks the script doc for notes, and plans to source footage of a dog pulling its owner from TikTok, Instagram or YouTube Shorts.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Do not hunt for cuts by eye in a clip that was exported with cuts already inside it. Run Scene Edit Detection on it: right-click clip → Scene Edit Detection → "Apply a Cut at Each Detected Cut Point".
- After detection, move the cut point to a meaningful moment: a beat drop or a change in the sentence.
- He tests several cut positions against the hook line "This one change stops pulling forever". First he tries the cut where "pulling" starts, then decides it works better on the word "stop". He also checks it against the music beat and adjusts.
- Hook structure: the hook VO line is "This one change stops pulling forever." Clip shots cover only part of the hook. He deliberately leaves empty space at the end of the video track, with no clip, so other footage can be added there ("I want to leave like this much space just without any clip").
- Before editing a section, check the script doc for comments or notes on that line (free direction if there are none).
- The Hold 1 first line, "Your dog's body is built for pulling", has no comment. The other half of that line ("and that back clip harness is making it worse") has a note: "back clip closeup / red arrow to it - to highlight it".
- Plan for illustrating "built for pulling": show a dog pulling, and use maybe two footage clips of a dog pulling its owner. Footage may be sourced from TikTok, Instagram and YouTube Shorts.
- Sequence is vertical, Position 540/960, Scale 50%, Opacity 100%. Cuts in the hook are very short, roughly 1–1.5 s per shot.
