## What is taught
The instructor explains his "fastest editing workflow": build the ad in six sequential layers and finish each one completely before starting the next. Working in single-task passes (for example 30 minutes of only b-roll) avoids jumping between tasks and keeps him in a focus "flow zone". At the end of the window he starts to introduce the example brand, "Stoke" (ceramic coffee cups), for the live edit.

## Concrete rules and numbers
- Six layers, in this order (from the on-screen note "The fastest editing workflow"):
  1. A-Roll / VO
  2. Music
  3. B-roll
  4. Animations / Transitions
  5. Captions / Text
  6. Sound Effects
- Finish one layer before moving to the next, e.g. voiceover is done, then music, then b-roll. Once a layer is checked off, don't revisit it.
- Layers 1–3 (VO, music, b-roll) are the core content. Layers 4–6 "enhance the edit".
- B-roll pass: go through the script and add a clip on top for each point. His example is a coffee cup clip when a coffee cup is mentioned. During this pass you only find clips, place them, trim them and pick the best part of each clip.
- Animations/transitions are optional ("if I want to do that"). Sound effects are optional ("if I decide to add sound effects").
- The only time figure is "for 30 minutes you just add b-roll". It is an example of a single-layer pass, not a rule.
- Anti-pattern he names: editing VO, adding b-roll in one spot, then an animation, then going back to fix VO, then adding captions in one place and returning for more. He calls this "very messy" and slower.
- Cutting silences belongs to the VO layer (Layer 1).
