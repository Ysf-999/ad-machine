# B08 — 8 video editing breakdown part 4 organic ad (Video Editing Breakdowns)

**Summary:** A breakdown of a text-only, no-voiceover "organic" UGC-style ad for Dog Friendly Co. (batch #61, an iteration of #45). The layer order puts music first. Text cards are cut on the beat and held long enough to read. Each card gets literal "show, don't tell" b-roll, with a fake dolly on the key claim and almost no other effects.

## Key lessons

1. **Brief structure.** The batch is 3 hooks and 2 holds, making 3 total ads. [00:00]
   - Hook 1 leads into Hold 1. This is the text-only version, with no VO.
   - Hooks 2 and 3 lead into Hold 2. These are the usual VO versions. Hook 3 uses an AI VO in an American female voice.
   - Hold 2 starts at the 0:14 point of Hook 1 in the example batch, on "the moment your dog…". Regenerate the AI VO in an American accent to match the hooks and keep the captions the same.
   - The brief combined an older ad that had potential but low spend with a UGC ad that was performing well. Both had similar concepts.
2. **Text-only ads reverse the layer order.** [02:15]
   - Normal order: A-roll/VO, then music, then b-roll.
   - Text-only order: music, then the text layer (the A-roll is itself text), then b-roll.
   - Reason: nobody is talking, so the viewer reads the words in their own head. The right music steers that "internal monologue", lets you emphasise words, and makes the viewer emotionally invested so they "self-validate" what they read.
3. **Choose music for the emotion you want, and cut text cards on the beat.** [03:00]
   - Cutting on the beat makes card-to-card changes feel natural and seamless, so viewers aren't pulled off the video.
   - Start the first hold at the beat drop. He found the drop by scrubbing and lowering the volume, then made a cut there. The waveform changes density around 0:18–0:20 in the example track.
4. **Leave enough reading time.** [03:00, 06:00]
   - Older viewers need about 3, 4 or even 5 seconds for what takes about 2 seconds to read.
   - The hook needs at least about 3 seconds per card. With two parts, give the hook about 5 seconds and don't rush it.
   - A short two-word card can be shorter. "FORWARD MOMENTUM" is 1 s 25 frames, and he says "for two words I think that'll pass".
5. **Keep text in the same screen area from card to card.** [03:00, 06:00]
   - Hook text goes in the top half so it is easy to read. Moving text around from clip to clip confuses the audience.
   - Text scale was about 50 with Position 540, 960 on the 1080×1920 sequence. He says scale depends on the canvas.
6. **Build text cards as PNGs.** [03:00]
   - He used Figma, partly because Windows emojis look bad (Apple emojis are better) and partly because it keeps all text in one place. The Adobe text tool is also fine.
   - Export 1x PNG, one file per card.
   - Naming: H1, H2, H3… for holds. Variants are H1-1, H1-2, H3-1 and so on. Other cards are HOOK 1-1, HOOK 1-2 and CTA-1.
   - Emphasis is made by capitalising one word (YOU, REDIRECTING, MINUTES, GUARANTEED, YEARS, START USING IT) or by two-tone boxes.
7. **Use a safe-zone PNG while editing, and turn it off before export.** [03:00, 20:30]
   - It sits on the top video track for the full length. The green usable area has a notch at the lower right where platform UI sits.
   - Closing advice: "make sure to double check and turn off your safe zones."
8. **Sourcing b-roll.** [09:00]
   - Search TikTok for keywords that match the concept (example: "dog leash pull").
   - Download with SnapAny, which works on almost every site except Facebook.
   - Avoid very famous or well-established creators, to reduce the chance of copyright claims.
   - If a clip has burned-in captions or a watermark, use Vmake's Watermark & Text Remover. The free tier is limited to 5 s per clip. Pro is $70/year, which he recommends and uses "pretty much all the time".
9. **Match each b-roll clip literally to its line of text, and lead with the pain point.** [10:54]
   - "Traditional harnesses and collars give dogs exactly what they want": a dog in a collar pulling, with the owner struggling. "I wanted to show the pain point right off the bat."
   - "FORWARD MOMENTUM": a goofy clip of a woman pulled or overpowered by a dog. It gives shock and attention and illustrates "momentum".
   - "But watch what happens with this front clip attachment": b-roll of the front D-ring being clipped on, which introduces the solution.
   - Cuts run about one clip every 1.5–3 s, one card per clip.
10. **Use before/after pairs as proof.** [12:00]
    - "Dog pulls 👇 Gets redirected back toward YOU": a first-person POV of a small dog pulling, then the owner unaffected by a big dog.
    - "It's about REDIRECTING it": a dog pulling, then an owner walking calmly.
    - "No pain. No discomfort": a smiling, relaxed dog in the harness, so the viewer sees "no negative emotions only positive vibes".
    - "Dogs simply discover: Pulling doesn't work anymore": two dogs pulling the owner, then a woman casually walking two dogs with the front-clip harness. Pick two clips that look like the same park on the same day, so it reads as a swap-the-harness, instant-change story.
11. **Give the most important claim a fake dolly.** [13:30–16:00]
    - Target line: "Within MINUTES most dogs learn to walk beside you".
    - A dolly means the camera moves toward the subject while the focal length zooms out. His version is much subtler than the Jaws example.
    - For a subject walking away from camera, start at scale about 80 with a position keyframe (about 526, 960) so the subject stays centred. End at scale 50 and position 540, 960.
    - His verdict: "hardly enough that it's not distracting but it's something better than just having them walk away."
12. **Experiment on your own.** You learn more by trying things than by watching tutorials. [16:00]
13. **Match the body b-roll to the feature or outcome in each line.** [16:00]
    - "Front + Back clips": show the back clip, then the front clip.
    - "Perfect fit GUARANTEED": a clean shot of the harness fitting perfectly.
    - "Premium materials. Last YEARS, not months": close-up aesthetic shots of the material.
    - "Stop fighting physics START USING IT": a peaceful, pull-free walk with a relaxed owner.
14. **End the CTA on a positive note.** [17:30]
    - The CTA is "Join 300,000+ happy dog parents / Shop @dogfriendlyco" over a happy, beautiful environment (a beach sunset clip) with owner and dog. It shows where the buyer hopes the product leads.
15. **Hook b-roll should grab attention and show the pain point at once.** [17:00, 18:00]
    - Hook 1 line 1, "THIS IS CHOKING YOUR DOG...": a dog pulling its owner (owner in all white, on mud).
    - Hook 1 line 2, "AND THIS GETS YOUR DOG TO STOP PULLING.": the owner attaching the front D-ring. It shows the mechanism but not how it works, which opens a curiosity gap ("how can this stop my dog from pulling?").
    - Hook 1 brief notes: use only the top part of the split screen (the owner falling) as the first clip, followed by the second hook clip. A sticky note suggests a Doberman or husky b-roll.
16. **VO versions reuse the same b-roll.** [18:00]
    - The only change is a conversational-tone voiceover. The idea is otherwise identical.
    - Hook 2 b-roll order: owner attaches the front D-ring, shows the bungee leash, then the product in action. It was reused because a UGC ad using it was performing really well ("it kind of ripped"). No new copy was needed, since the copy is on the UGC hook.
    - Hook 3 line: "If you have a big dog, I need to show you this…". Its b-roll order is harness on dog, D-ring attach, bungee leash, product in action.
17. **Keep animation, captions and SFX minimal.** [18:30]
    - The only animation is the dolly. Captions and text are "pretty much non-existent because the a-roll is a text". Nothing should distract from reading.
    - SFX appear only on the hook clip, to emphasise the action. The sound was embedded in the original clip, so he dragged the audio down with the clip and added nothing else.

## Editing recipe

1. Read the brief (Milanote board). Note the hooks, holds, copy, sizes (1080x1080, 1080x1350, 1080x1920, 1920x1080) and notes per hook.
2. Pick the music for the target emotion. Drop it on the timeline first. Find the beat drop and cut there.
3. Place the safe-zone PNG on the top track.
4. Write each text card in Figma and export 1x PNGs (H1-1, H1-2…). Two-tone box cards are used for hooks, and plain white text with a black outline for body lines.
5. Put cards on the timeline on V4, with CTA cards on V5. Cut each card on the beat, position it in the top half and set scale to about 50. Alt-drag to duplicate and swap graphics.
6. Check each card's duration: about 3 s per card for hooks and long lines, shorter for two-word cards. Use mark clip to read the length.
7. The text layer is now the foundation. Source b-roll (TikTok, SnapAny, Vmake as needed) and lay one clip per card on V1.
8. Add the fake dolly to the key claim clip: scale 80→50 with a position keyframe.
9. Add SFX only on the hook, if the clip already carries one.
10. Build the hook variants. Hooks 2 and 3 use the same b-roll with a conversational VO and bottom-centre white captions.
11. Add a fade-out at the end. Turn off the safe-zone overlay and export the needed sizes.

## How an ffmpeg/Python agent applies this

1. **Layer order (music first, text as the A-roll).**
   - AUTOMATE: build the timeline from music duration outward. Take the music first, then place the text cards, then the b-roll.
   - JUDGE: check that the music mood fits the emotion of the copy.
2. **Cut on the beat and start the first hold at the drop.**
   - AUTOMATE: use librosa or `aubio` for beat and onset detection. Find the drop as the largest jump in RMS energy or spectral flux. Snap each card's start time to the nearest beat.
   - JUDGE: listen to or inspect the result, since automatic drop detection can pick the wrong moment.
3. **Reading time per card.**
   - AUTOMATE: set duration = max(about 3 s for hook or long cards, shorter for ≤2 words, for example 1 s 25 frames ≈ 1.8 s). Use a word-count-based minimum and extend to 4–5 s for older audiences. Apply it as a rule over the card list.
   - JUDGE: check that the cards still land on beats after the durations are adjusted.
4. **Consistent text position, top half.**
   - AUTOMATE: render every card at the same anchor, for example centred at x=540 within the upper half of a 1080×1920 canvas, avoiding the lower-right UI notch. Overlay with `overlay=x:y:enable='between(t,a,b)'`.
5. **Cards as PNGs.**
   - AUTOMATE: render with Pillow or Cairo using Proxima Nova Bold 92 (or a fallback font), white fill, 4 px black outside stroke, and optional rounded rectangle boxes (E85048 red, radius 22; blue for the second line). Use a colour emoji font for 👇. Name the files H1-1, H1-2… Alternatively burn an ASS caption with a BorderStyle=3 box. PNG overlays keep the look closest to the original.
   - JUDGE: view a frame to confirm emoji rendering and no overflow outside the safe area.
6. **Safe-zone overlay.**
   - AUTOMATE: use it only as a layout guide. Compute the safe-area margins in code and keep text inside. Never include the overlay in the final render, and add an assertion that the overlay input is not in the filtergraph.
7. **B-roll sourcing (TikTok, SnapAny, Vmake).**
   - SKIP: GUI and web tools that the agent cannot drive. If clips are supplied, go straight to editing.
   - JUDGE: check clips for burned-in captions or watermarks and for famous-creator copyright risk. Cropping or `delogo` is a partial workaround only.
8. **Literal clip-to-line matching and before/after pairs.**
   - JUDGE: a vision model should check that each clip shows what its line claims, that before/after pairs share the same setting, and that the pain point appears first.
   - AUTOMATE: once matched, assemble with `concat` or per-clip `trim` to the card durations, with one clip per card at roughly 1.5–3 s.
9. **Fake dolly on the key claim.**
   - AUTOMATE: do a progressive zoom-out from 1.6× to 1.0×. This is the equivalent of scale 80→50 in Premiere, where 50 is the fit value. Use `scale` with `eval=frame` and `w='iw*(1.6-0.6*t/D)'` followed by a `crop` to 1080×1920 on a subject-centred position, or `zoompan` with a decreasing `z`. Keep it subtle.
   - JUDGE: confirm that the subject stays centred and the motion isn't distracting.
10. **Positive CTA ending.**
    - JUDGE: confirm the final clip is happy and aspirational.
    - AUTOMATE: add a fade-out with `afade` and `fade` at the end.
11. **Hook b-roll (curiosity gap) and hook variants.**
    - JUDGE: check that the hook clip grabs attention and shows the pain point, and that the second line's clip shows the mechanism without explaining it.
    - AUTOMATE: for the VO versions, generate the AI VO (American female voice) with a TTS tool and mix it over the same b-roll. Burn white two-line captions at bottom-centre with ASS.
12. **Minimal effects, SFX only on the hook.**
    - AUTOMATE: use no extra transitions or captions. Add one SFX at the hook start with `adelay` and `amix`, or keep the clip's embedded audio.
    - JUDGE: confirm the SFX doesn't clash with the music.
13. **Export checks.**
    - AUTOMATE: export each size (1080x1080, 1080x1350, 1080x1920, 1920x1080) with `scale` + `pad` or `crop`. Verify there is no safe-zone overlay in the output, for example by checking the filtergraph and sampling a frame.
14. **Experiment and learn.**
    - SKIP: this is a mindset tip, not an operation.
