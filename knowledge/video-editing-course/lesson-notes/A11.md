# A11 — 11 video hooks (Video Ads Mastery)

**Summary:** The hook (about the first 3 seconds, maybe less) decides whether anyone sees the rest of the ad. A strong hook pairs a scroll-stopping, instantly clear visual with short, specific hook text that is on screen from frame one. Hooks are then tested one variable at a time, and the data decides, not gut feeling.

## Key lessons

1. **The hook makes or breaks the ad.** You have about 3 seconds, "maybe even less nowadays". A great demo at second 10 or testimonial at second 30 is worthless if nobody gets past the hook. Don't spend hours on product shots, script and structure and then bolt on a basic hook. [00:00]
2. **Composition: one obvious focal point, readable text.** Assume the phone is about half a meter from the face. The hook must be obvious, the text readable at that distance, and there must be one clear focal point. [00:00]
3. **Context: a viewer scanning for about 0.5 seconds must understand what's going on.** People scan, they don't watch or read, and big chunks of text get skipped. Never make people work to understand what they're looking at, because the moment they think, they scroll. [00:00]
4. **Best context is a super-clear visual that tells the story with no text.** The example is someone throwing a phone against a wall. Big, bold, easy-to-read "hook text" at the top of the screen is the other way to give context. [00:00]
5. **Aim for intrigue, not confusion.** Confusion is "I don't understand what I'm looking at", and it loses the viewer. Intrigue is "I need to know how this works", and it challenges a belief and keeps them. Example of intrigue: "lost five kilograms while still eating pizza every other day." [03:00]
6. **Hook text is not the lip-sync caption.** It is a separate element whose job is to tell people why they should care. [03:00]
7. **Be specific and relatable, not generic.** "This is the best eye protection ever" is bad because nobody believes it. Better: "I stopped getting headaches and still grind Minecraft every night for five hours". It targets a specific audience, gamers with eye strain, and gives the video context. [03:00]
8. **Hook text must be on screen from frame one, with no animation.** No slide-in after two seconds and no pop-in. By the time the animation finishes the viewer is "three videos down their feed". [03:00, 06:00]
9. **Make hook text clean, readable and slightly bigger than the regular captions.** Place it where it doesn't compete with the visual, at the top of the screen in the UGC exercises. Viewers should know what the video is about within the first 2 seconds. [06:00]
10. **Condense rambling creator intros into one line.** Keep the contrast or the impressive number. Cut the skepticism and backstory, and focus on the result and who it's for: "no fluff, just context". [06:00]
    - "I used to spend hours at the gym doing cardio, but I never saw results until I discovered this 15-minute workout routine" becomes "I replaced two hours of cardio with 15 minutes of THIS". It keeps the long-vs-short contrast, and "THIS" in caps creates curiosity.
    - "I was skeptical… every sleep supplement… helped me sleep through the night for the first time in years" becomes "This supplement finally helped me sleep 8 hours as an insomniac". It drops the skepticism and keeps the result plus the audience.
    - "As a professional gamer who spends 12 hours a day in front of screens, I was getting terrible headaches until I found these glasses" becomes "How I game 12 hours a day without headaches as an eSports pro". It keeps the hours figure and upgrades "gamer" to "eSports pro" for authority.
11. **Pattern for a hook line:** a concrete number or result, plus the target person or identity, plus an authority-boosting label. A curiosity word such as "THIS" in caps can replace the reveal. [06:00]
12. **Half-second test.** If someone can understand the hook in half a second, it is done right. Keep what makes people interested, but make it quick and easy to read. [09:00]
13. **Never run just one version of an ad. Test messaging and visuals separately, almost never both at once.** [09:00]
    - **Messaging test:** the same video with 3 variations that differ only in hook text, each targeting a different desire. Weight-loss examples: "I lost 30 pounds without giving up carbs" (food freedom), "Finally hit into my old jeans" (specific goals), "No more hiding in photos" (emotional transformation).
    - **Visual test:** identical messaging with 3 different visual hooks. V1 is a before/after transformation. V2 is someone struggling, e.g. buttoning jeans. V3 is someone confident and happy, "rocking their results".
    - **Reason:** if both change and V2 wins, you can't tell whether message or visual did it. "If you're testing both at once, you're just guessing."
14. **Apply learnings to future ads.** If before/after wins, open future ads with results. [09:00]
15. **A winner becomes the new baseline.** Keep testing against it. If before/after wins, make three other before/after hooks, because a later "hook six" might beat the original. Treat every launch as a controlled experiment that teaches something about the audience. [12:00]
16. **Visual stops the scroll, text keeps them watching.** The visual must stop the scroll with the right attention (not clickbait) and be instantly clear to the target customer: "no confusion, no chaos." Text should be strategic: not a novel, not fancy animated text, just clear, readable context on why to keep watching. This gives "two hooks in one hook". Text isn't always required, but it has worked well for them. Change the visual and keep the text, or change the message and keep the visual. [12:00]
17. **Let data decide, not ego, gut or competitors.** After over $20M in ad spend, some of their dumbest ideas crushed it, and heavily researched ads have flopped. Let anyone on the team pitch ideas (editor, strategist, designer, copywriter, intern) and test even ridiculous ones. [12:00, 15:00]

## Editing recipe

The lesson does not walk through a single edit. Its hook-building steps, in order:

1. Pick the visual for the first 3 seconds. It needs one clear focal point and should explain the story without text if possible, like the phone thrown at a wall.
2. Write the hook text. Take the creator's rambling opener and keep the number or contrast and the result. Add who it's for and an authority label, and drop the skepticism and backstory. Use caps on a curiosity word such as "THIS" if it helps.
3. Place the hook text at the top of the screen, bigger than the lip-sync captions, clean and readable. It must be visible from frame one with no entrance animation.
4. Check that a scanning viewer gets it in 0.5 seconds, and that the video's subject is clear within 2 seconds.
5. Build test variants, changing one variable at a time:
   - 3 hook-text variants over the same video, each targeting a different desire; or
   - 3 visual hooks with identical text.
6. Run them, then take the winner as the baseline. Make new variations of the winning concept and repeat.

## How an ffmpeg/Python agent applies this

1. **3-second window and focal point.**
   - JUDGE: sample frames at 0, 0.5, 1 and 3 s and ask a vision model whether there is one obvious focal point and whether the story is clear without sound.
   - AUTOMATE: check that the first cut shows the strongest visual, not a logo or slow intro.
2. **Readable at half a meter / 0.5-second scan.**
   - AUTOMATE: render hook text large, high-contrast and short (a word-count cap). The lesson gives no font size, so pick one and check it on a downscaled 9:16 frame.
   - JUDGE: confirm the text is legible at phone size.
3. **Context from visual or top text.**
   - AUTOMATE: burn the hook text as an ASS event at the top of the frame.
   - JUDGE: check that the visual plus text tell the story in 0.5 s.
4. **Intrigue vs confusion.** JUDGE: have a model or human rate whether the hook reads as "I need to know how" or "what is this?". This can't be computed.
5. **Hook text is separate from captions.**
   - AUTOMATE: use a separate ASS style and layer for the hook text, apart from the lip-sync captions.
6. **Specific, relatable text.** JUDGE: reject generic superlatives ("best ever"). Check for a concrete number or result, a named audience, and an authority label. An LLM can draft and filter these.
7. **On screen from frame one, no animation.**
   - AUTOMATE: set the hook-text Dialogue start to `0:00:00.00` with no `\fad`, `\move` or `\t` entrance tags.
   - AUTOMATE: verify by extracting frame 0 and checking the text pixels are present.
8. **Bigger than captions, top placement.**
   - AUTOMATE: set the hook style's font size above the caption style's, with top alignment (`\an8`) and a margin.
   - AUTOMATE: keep text clear of the edges where platform UI (right-side icons, bottom bar) overlaps. The lesson gives no margins.
9. **Condense a creator intro.** AUTOMATE: transcribe the opener (Whisper), then use an LLM with the pattern "number/result + audience + authority label, curiosity word in caps, no backstory" to produce candidates. JUDGE: pick the best.
10. **Half-second test.** JUDGE: have a model read only a 0.5 s flash of the first frame and report what the ad is about.
11. **Test messaging and visuals separately.**
    - AUTOMATE: for a messaging test, loop over 3 hook strings with the same source video, producing 3 renders that differ only in the ASS hook text and are named by variant.
    - AUTOMATE: for a visual test, concat 3 different opening clips (about 3–5 s) with the same body and same hook text.
    - AUTOMATE: never vary both in one batch, and log which variable changed per file in a manifest.
12. **Use the results.**
    - AUTOMATE: store winner metadata (concept, hook text, visual type) and generate new variations of the winner.
    - JUDGE: performance data has to come from the ad platform, outside the agent's tooling.
13. **Data over gut.** SKIP as an editing step. The agent should simply produce all variants, including odd ideas, rather than pre-filter them.
14. **Editor UI layer-group names and structure order (Hook, Problem, Demo, CTA).** AUTOMATE as a timeline order for concatenating labeled segments. The GUI itself is SKIP.
