# A17 — 17 AI Voiceovers (Video Ads Mastery)

**Make AI voiceovers that pass as a real person by choosing a human-sounding voice in ElevenLabs, tuning its settings, and rewriting the script's punctuation, emphasis and filler words over several regenerations. Never ship the first generation of a pasted script.**

## Key lessons

1. **Target and proof.** The aim is a voiceover people (clients included) don't recognise as AI, and not the stock "TikTok AI voice". The demo ad was on pace to become a top-spending ad in the client account and was built from only AI voiceover plus product B-roll. [00:00–03:00]
2. **Why use AI VO.** There is no waiting for creators to record, no mispronounced words, and it is fast and effective "if you know how to do it right". [00:00–03:00]
3. **Find voices in the ElevenLabs Voice Library.** Voices → Library, then filter by language, accent, gender (male, female, neutral) and age (young, middle-aged, older). Use the Advertisement, Social Media and Entertainment & TV tabs. Preview a voice (about 7–9 s), then click Add to put it in "My voices". The goal is voices that "don't sound like AI at all". [03:00–06:00]
4. **Know the account limits.** The paid plan shows 30/30 custom voice slots, and adding past that needs deleting voices or upgrading. The free tier is "not that good". [03:00–06:00]
5. **Pick the right ElevenLabs tool.** [06:00–09:00]
   - Text to Speech takes up to 5,000 characters per generation.
   - Voice Changer: record the script yourself, upload it (up to 50 MB), and swap in a target voice. Delivery stays yours. Use it when TTS can't read a line the way you want.
   - Sound Effects generates SFX from a text prompt and returns 4 generations. Quality varies, so use more specific prompts.
   - Studio is what the team actually uses for VO.
6. **Studio setup.** Start from scratch, choose Eleven Multilingual v2 (the most human-like), and use the Standard quality preset (128 kbps, 44.1 kHz MP3), which is "perfectly fine". [06:00–09:00]
   - Higher presets need the Pro plan: High (192 kbps, +20% character cost), Ultra (192 kbps, +50%), Ultra Lossless (705.6 kbps WAV, +100%).
7. **Organise per hook.**
   - Name the project brand – concept number – angle.
   - Make one chapter per script part (Hook 1, Hook 2, Hook 3, Hold).
   - Set the voice for each new chapter, because a new chapter can default to another voice (e.g. "Honey Packs Female"). [09:00–12:00, 18:00–21:00, 21:00–24:00]
8. **Voice settings.** The usual starting point is Stability about 35, Similarity about 40 and Style Exaggeration about 15. Every voice differs and needs its own tweaking, so generate several times and listen. [09:00–12:00]
   - Changing settings forces regeneration of every paragraph that uses that voice.
   - Speaker boost was on in the demos.
9. **Don't paste and ship.** Pasting a Claude/ChatGPT script or a brief into ElevenLabs and using the first generation "would be a disaster". It usually sounds robotic because nobody spent time on delivery ("usually a skill issue"). About five minutes of attention per script fixes it. [21:00–24:00, 30:00–33:00]
10. **Text formatting is the most important lever.** [12:00–15:00]
    - Generate, listen several times, adjust the text and regenerate.
    - Separate lines or paragraphs create awkward pauses between them. Merge lines that belong to one thought into one paragraph. Example: "…one thing... when it comes to the air your child breathes, you shouldn't compromise."
11. **Regenerate selectively.**
    - Use "Regenerate selection" on the one weak sentence. Only changed paragraphs re-render and good takes stay as they were. [12:00–15:00, 18:00–21:00]
    - Cost is small: about 85–170 credits per short line, from a balance of about 197,000. [24:00–27:00]
    - He did 4–5 takes on one line before accepting a "good enough" one. [24:00–27:00]
12. **Emphasis via text.** Read the line aloud first to hear where the stress falls, then encode it. [15:00–18:00, 18:00–21:00]
    - Remove commas that create unwanted pauses ("diagnosis, taught me" → "diagnosis taught me").
    - Capitalise stressed words: "taught me ONE thing", "you CAN'T afford to compromise".
    - If the read is still flat, raise Style Exaggeration to 25%.
13. **Punctuation steers delivery.** [24:00–30:00]
    - An exclamation mark adds energy ("designed by a doctor for his own family!", "sterilizable!", "start breathing easier!").
    - Periods between list items make each phrase drop and sound disconnected. Join them with commas and a dash before the payoff: "No endless filter replacements, no hidden mold - EXACTLY what I've been searching for!"
    - Replace "..." with " - " where you want a connected beat.
    - Emotionless reads sound like AI.
14. **Add spoken connectors.** Put natural filler at sentence starts or ends ("And clearly I'm not the only one who thinks so", "So get yours today…", also "and so", "maybe", "because", "basically", "stuff like that"). Imperfect, spoken text beats polished text. "Our goal is not perfect… organic, authentic, real." [27:00–33:00]
15. **Remove stage directions from the TTS text.** "(short pause in VO)" was read aloud as "Short pause in VO." Delete it, or replace it with ElevenLabs "insert break". Break options are 1, 2 or 3 s (3 s is the maximum, default 1s), and a break can go mid-sentence. The tag form `<break time="1s" />` also works in Text to Speech. [21:00–30:00, 06:00–09:00]
16. **Listen to the joined result.** After converting each chapter, play the pieces back to back to hear how they sound together. [18:00–21:00]
17. **Export a chapter.** Convert → Convert chapter → wait for it to assemble → Download. Attach the mp3 back to the script card (Hook 1 ≈ 244 KB, Hook 2 ≈ 185 KB, Hold ≈ 725 KB / about 0:45). [12:00–15:00, 30:00–33:00]
18. **Script structure.** Write multiple interchangeable hooks that all feed one shared HOLD body, then a CTA, with one VO file per section. [03:00–06:00, 33:00–36:00]
    - The COPY line ("Shop honeypacks.com") should appear only with the last line of VO.
19. **Clone the creator voice of a winning ad.** Instant Voice Clone lets you generate any text in that voice. [33:00–36:00]
    - Download the source ad (e.g. from the Facebook Ad Library link).
    - Go to ElevenLabs → Voices → My Voices → Add a new voice → Instant Voice Clone.
    - Name it and upload the file (each file under 10 MB; the sample was 9.4 MB).
    - Tick "Remove background noise" only if the audio is noisy.
    - Optionally add a description, tick the consent box and click Add Voice, then go to Studio and start from scratch.
    - The UI says sample quality beats quantity, and more than 5 minutes of audio gives little gain.
20. **Baseline first, then tweak.** For the second script he generated once with the defaults to hear the baseline. Defaults were Stability about 50–55, Similarity about 75–80, Style 0, Speaker boost on. He then made a modest change: Stability slightly lower, Similarity about 70, Style 15. Regenerating the new settings and the cleaned text "sounds a lot better". [36:00–42:00]
21. **Clean the script before generating.** [36:00–39:00]
    - Fix punctuation and merge lines.
    - Capitalise emphasis words (THIS, NEVER).
    - Use ellipses for pauses.
    - Cut repeated words ("my man's drink" → "his drink", because "my man" was already said).
    - If a custom voice doesn't show up, refresh the page.
22. **Isolate a voice from music before cloning.** vocalremover.org (free) splits music and vocal stems. Mute the music, save the vocal as mp3, and use that as the clone sample. It is "not 100% perfect, but it really does the job". [39:00–42:00]
23. **Winning-ad formula.** The brand's existing content, an AI-generated script, an AI voiceover, and product B-roll on top: no creators, custom scripts or custom footage. He cites a dozen winning ads made this way. The voiceover must sound authentic and real. [39:00–42:00]

## Editing recipe

This is the voiceover production flow demonstrated. It is not a full video edit.

1. Put the script in a board with Hook 1/2/3 → HOLD → CTA, each card holding its AI VO text.
2. Choose or clone a voice:
   - Browse the Voice Library and Add a voice, or
   - Download a winning ad, remove any music (vocalremover.org), and Instant Voice Clone the speaker.
3. In ElevenLabs Studio, create the project (brand – concept – angle) and a chapter per section.
4. Paste a section's text and select the voice for that chapter. Check that "Override settings" is off if you want the project defaults.
5. Set voice settings (about 35 / 40 / 15 as a start) and Save. Accept the regenerate warning.
6. Generate, then listen.
7. Edit text:
   - Merge fragments and delete stray commas.
   - Capitalise stressed words.
   - Add "!" and dashes, and swap "..." for " - " where needed.
   - Add filler connectors.
   - Remove "(short pause in VO)" or insert a real break.
8. Regenerate only the selected lines, repeating as needed (up to about 4–5 takes per line).
9. Play the full chapter. Regenerate any paragraph that sounds off.
10. Convert chapter, wait, and Download the mp3. Attach it to the script card.
11. Play the downloaded pieces back to back to check how the sections join.
12. Lay the VO under product B-roll with simple captions.

## How an ffmpeg/Python agent applies this

1. **Target and proof (lessons 1–2).** JUDGE: listen to or transcribe the generated VO and check for flat delivery, odd stress, awkward pauses and mispronunciations. A human or audio-capable model should review before it ships.
2. **Find and add voices (3–4).** JUDGE: choose from previews by ear. Browsing the Library and Add is a GUI step, so the agent should use pre-saved voice IDs. SKIP the 30-slot management unless the API is in use.
3. **Pick the tool (5–6).** AUTOMATE: use the ElevenLabs API (an assumption, since the lesson shows only the GUI) with the Multilingual v2 model and Standard 128 kbps/44.1 kHz MP3 output. Keep each request within 5,000 characters. The Studio UI itself is GUI-only, so emulate it with one request per hook/hold/CTA section.
4. **Organise per hook (7, 18).** AUTOMATE: generate one file per section (hook_1.mp3, hook_2.mp3, hold.mp3, cta.mp3) and name files brand_concept_angle. Pass the voice ID explicitly for every request so no default voice sneaks in.
5. **Voice settings (8, 20).** AUTOMATE: pass stability ≈ 0.35, similarity ≈ 0.40, style ≈ 0.15 and speaker boost on. Treat these as starting values and sweep a few variants per voice, then JUDGE the best. Defaults for a baseline were about 0.5 / 0.75 / 0 (a mapping of the UI percentages, so check the API ranges).
6. **Don't ship the first take (9, 10–14).**
   - AUTOMATE a text-prep pass:
     - merge short fragments into one paragraph;
     - strip commas before "taught me";
     - convert list periods to commas plus a dash before the payoff;
     - add "!" to the energetic lines;
     - replace "..." with " - " where wanted;
     - uppercase flagged emphasis words;
     - prepend "And"/"So" connectors to closing lines.
   - Generate several takes per line.
   - JUDGE which take is best. Use a model to rate the energy, the stress on the caps words and the flow.
7. **Selective regeneration (11).** AUTOMATE: regenerate only the sentences you changed, then concatenate. Re-use unchanged audio files. Cost per short line is tiny.
8. **Pauses and directions (15).** AUTOMATE: strip "(short pause in VO)" and similar directions with a regex before sending. For real pauses, insert `<break time="1s" />` (maximum 3 s per break) or splice silence with ffmpeg: `ffmpeg -f lavfi -t 1 -i anullsrc=r=44100:cl=mono …` followed by the concat filter.
9. **Join check and export (16–17).** AUTOMATE: concat the section files with the ffmpeg concat demuxer, matching sample rate and channels. Optionally add `loudnorm`. JUDGE the seams by listening, since this is where the disjointed feel shows up. Measure section lengths with ffprobe and compare to the examples (hook about 6–8 s, whole body about 45 s) to sanity-check pacing.
10. **Script structure (18).** AUTOMATE: keep a JSON of hooks[], hold, cta, copy. Build N ad variants as hook_i + hold + cta (concat), and show the COPY text only during the last line of VO (use the last section's start time for the caption/overlay `enable='between(t,…)'`).
11. **Clone a winning ad voice (19).**
    - AUTOMATE the prep: extract audio with `ffmpeg -i ad.mp4 -vn -ac 1 -ar 44100 sample.mp3`. Keep it under 10 MB and, per the UI, aim for no more than about 5 minutes. If the clone API is available, upload it. The consent box needs a human.
    - JUDGE: check that the clone resembles the source and has no artifacts.
    - Downloading from the Ad Library is a manual step.
12. **Baseline vs tweak (20–21).** AUTOMATE: render a default take and a tweaked take, and keep the comparison. Strip repeated phrases with a text check. JUDGE the final pick by ear.
13. **Music removal (22).** AUTOMATE: use a local stem-separation tool (e.g. Demucs, my suggestion; the lesson names only vocalremover.org) to get a vocal-only mp3 for cloning. The website itself is GUI-driven. JUDGE the result for leftover music.
14. **Winning-ad formula (23).** AUTOMATE the assembly:
    - Lay the VO on the brand's B-roll, cutting to a new clip every sentence or two.
    - Include a bold two-line title card in the first seconds.
    - Burn ASS captions: small white sans-serif with no box, 3–6 words per card, centred at about 2/3 height.
    - JUDGE whether the VO sounds authentic and the B-roll matches each line.
