# A02 — 2 organic ads (Video Ads Mastery)

**Organic ads are the easiest ad type to make, sometimes just text on a video. They come in three types (customer testimonial, social-media style, lifestyle). The goal is that the viewer never feels they are watching an ad, so the edit should look like something an ordinary person made and posted.**

## Key lessons

1. **Organic ads are the simplest type to produce.** They need no fancy transitions, complicated effects or animations. Sometimes the whole ad is text on a video. [00:00]
2. **There are three organic types:** customer testimonials, social-media style ads, and lifestyle content. [00:00]
3. **Choose the type by the customer's feed, not by what advertisers think looks cool.** Ask: What content does the customer consume day to day? What makes them stop scrolling? What are their interests? What feels natural in their feed? [00:00]
4. **Testimonials: keep them raw, authentic and minimally edited.** Imperfect footage (not level, bad lighting, "ugly") is fine and even desirable because it signals "raw, not scripted". Don't make testimonials pretty. The minimum bar is clear audio and a visible face. [01:00]
5. **Social-media style ads: shoot on an iPhone, in a home setting, with a simple POV-style text overlay** that the creator could have typed herself on TikTok. [02:15]
   - Example: a "burned slideboard" ad of a fit mom working out at home beat all of the brand's professional content. It matched what the target audience (busy moms who want to get fit) watches daily.
   - The team replicated it with the same angles and casual feel as the fitness influencers the audience follows.
6. **Don't show off your editing skills.** Make the ad look like a non-professional editor or ordinary Instagram poster made it. [03:00]
   - Peekaboo Pillow ads are made to look like they were edited by moms and posted as Reels.
   - Use trendy music and Instagram-style captions that look typed on a phone.
   - Sometimes make the edit slightly imperfect, because that feels real.
7. **Why it works:** an ad-looking video raises the viewer's guard and triggers "ignore it mode". A badly made ad with a "sponsored" label gets zapped through. Content-looking video makes people stop and watch. [03:00]
8. **Show an "achievable" person from the target audience, in the most organic way possible.** [04:30]
   - Burn: a woman in her 40s working out at home, not a very young, very fit, sexy model.
   - Find the "perfect middle", someone the audience sees as achievable.
   - Peekaboo: real moms with their babies, shot raw and authentic at home.
9. **Copy must sound like the person, not the brand.** [05:30]
   - Bad: "try our revolutionary peekaboo pillow today".
   - Good: "I finally found something that helps my baby with tummy time". [06:15]
10. **Lifestyle content sells a vibe or aesthetic, not just the product.** [06:30]
    - Example: a jewelry brand that was really selling "main character energy".
    - Put the product in beautiful, Instagram-worthy moments and in an environment the customer would love to be in.
    - To edit these ads, find lifestyle or luxury influencers in the niche, study how they edit, and replicate it.
    - Make the brand feel expensive and build a whole vibe around it. [09:00]
11. **When to use each type:** [07:00]
    - Testimonials: you need to build trust, the product solves a specific problem, and real customers have genuine success stories. Take the raw, unscripted testimonials and run them as the ad.
    - Social-media style: the audience is active on social media (example: women 25 to 45) and the niche has clear content trends (example: fitness "get fit at home" tips). Make ads that look exactly like those trends.
    - Lifestyle: the lifestyle around the product matters more than the product itself.
12. **Testimonial edit: run it as is.** Optionally cut silences and mispronounced words, and optionally add basic captions for easier following (even that isn't important). [09:30]
13. **For non-testimonial styles, find an organic Instagram, TikTok or YouTube Shorts video that is "crushing it" in the niche and copy its editing style.** That video is the template. [10:00]
14. **Strategy beats editing skill.** Knowing how to edit is worthless if you don't know what to put in the video. Technical skill comes from reps. [10:30]
15. **Assignment judging criteria:** show that you understand who you're talking to and selling to, and that the ad feels native to the platform. Polish, transitions and fancy effects are not judged. [12:00]

## Editing recipe

The lesson gives no timeline walkthrough. This is the workflow implied across it:

1. Decide the type from the target customer's feed habits (testimonial, social-style, or lifestyle).
2. Find a high-performing organic reference video in the niche (Instagram, TikTok or YouTube Shorts) and use it as the template.
3. Source or shoot casual, phone-style footage with an achievable-looking person from the audience. Keep it raw.
4. Testimonial: run the clip as is, optionally trimming silences and mispronounced words and adding basic captions.
5. Social/lifestyle: add a native-looking text overlay (POV-style, or Instagram text sticker) and trendy music. Copy the reference's cuts and angles.
6. Write the copy in a person's voice, not the brand's.
7. Leave in slight imperfections. Skip complex transitions, effects and animations.

## How an ffmpeg/Python agent applies this

1. **Organic ads need no fancy effects (lesson 1).**
   - AUTOMATE: keep the ffmpeg graph to hard cuts, trim and a caption burn. Don't add xfade transitions, animated zooms or overlays unless the reference has them.
2. **Three types (lesson 2).**
   - JUDGE: pick the type from the brief. A model or human decides which type fits the audience.
3. **Choose the type from the customer's feed (lesson 3).**
   - JUDGE: check that the chosen style matches content the audience actually consumes.
4. **Testimonials raw and minimal (lesson 4).**
   - AUTOMATE: don't color-correct, level or stabilize. Check clear audio with `ffmpeg volumedetect` or `ebur128`, and face visibility with a face-detection pass on sampled frames.
   - JUDGE: confirm audio is intelligible.
5. **Social-media style: iPhone, home, POV text (lesson 5).**
   - AUTOMATE: output 1080x1920. Burn the POV text as an ASS caption, white sans-serif, thin dark outline/shadow (Outline about 2, no box), centered vertically (Alignment 5), about 3 lines wrapped. Use sentence case.
   - JUDGE: check that the footage reads as phone-shot and home-set.
6. **Look non-professional (lesson 6).**
   - AUTOMATE: use native-looking text styles (an ASS BorderStyle=3 box for the white-box/black-serif sticker look). Mix in trending music at a sensible level under any speech.
   - JUDGE: whether music is currently "trendy", and whether the imperfection looks deliberate.
7. **Ad vs content look (lesson 7).**
   - JUDGE: view the finished ad. Does it look like a brand ad or like a person's post? Avoid polished intros and logos.
8. **Achievable person (lesson 8).**
   - JUDGE: needs a model or human to check that the person is realistic for the audience (age, look, setting). When generating or selecting footage, pick relatable people and home settings.
9. **Copy in person's voice (lesson 9).**
   - AUTOMATE: have an LLM rewrite captions and VO scripts in first person using the good/bad example. Reject phrases like "try our revolutionary…". Generate AI VO in a casual tone.
   - JUDGE: read the final copy aloud for naturalness.
10. **Lifestyle: vibe over product (lesson 10).**
    - JUDGE: check that the footage shows aesthetic, Instagram-worthy moments.
    - AUTOMATE: for tight product-on-body clips, scale/crop to 9:16 and add light music. Mimic the reference's cut rhythm, extracted from scene-change detection (`select='gt(scene,0.3)'`).
11. **Choose by situation (lesson 11).**
    - JUDGE: use the brief's audience and niche to select the type.
12. **Testimonial edit (lesson 12).**
    - AUTOMATE: transcribe with Whisper. Cut silences with `silencedetect` and trim. Remove mispronounced words only if word-level timestamps and the transcript make them detectable. Burn basic captions from the transcript as an ASS file (optional).
13. **Copy the reference (lesson 13).**
    - AUTOMATE: analyze the reference video. Extract cut timestamps with scene detection, caption position and style from sampled frames, and music presence. Replicate them in the ffmpeg pipeline.
    - JUDGE: confirm the match visually.
14. **Strategy over editing (lesson 14).**
    - JUDGE: before rendering, check that the ad's content (audience, message) is right.
15. **Assignment criteria (lesson 15).**
    - JUDGE: the review is on audience understanding and platform-native feel. Don't add polish.
- **Software choice (Premiere Pro, Final Cut Pro, CapCut): SKIP.** An agent uses ffmpeg/Python, and the tool doesn't matter.
- **Instructor's title cards and light-leak transitions: SKIP.** They are course production, not ad technique.
