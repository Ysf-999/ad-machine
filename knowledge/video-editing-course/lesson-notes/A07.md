# A07 — 7 vsls (Video Ads Mastery)

**Summary:** VSLs (video sales letters) are long-form, belief-changing ads that work through storytelling. The lesson covers the four-part script structure (hook, problem amplification, hope spot, solution buildup), the "breadcrumb" pacing of reveals, and the build order for editing one (audio, cuts, B-roll, captions and graphics). It closes by setting a hockey protective-gear VSL assignment.

## Key lessons

1. **Use VSLs where you must educate and convince.** Typical length is 1–15 minutes. Some run 30 minutes or 1 hour on Facebook and "crush it". Best fits are health products, products that need customer education, problem-solving products, and older audiences (supplements, weight loss, skincare). The test is "anything where you need to explain a problem and convince people your solution works". [00:00]
2. **Older audiences tolerate longer ads.** They will watch a 5-minute ad because they are less "brain rotted" from scrolling. [00:00]
3. **VSL editing is mini-movie editing.** You juggle voiceover, b-roll, animations and text overlays. If any one is poor, viewers leave within seconds. Most editors won't put in the work to master it, which is why it pays well. [00:00]
4. **The hook has about 3 seconds on social, even in a 3-, 10- or 15-minute VSL.** It must "hit like a truck". [00:00]
5. **A hook must grab attention and qualify the audience.** A clickbaity hook unrelated to the viewer's problem is useless. [03:00]
6. **Hook formula: a relatable personal struggle plus a shocking twist.** [00:00–06:00]
   - Example: "What if I told you the reason you can't sleep is because your brain is actually trying to kill you."
   - Controversy angle: "The real reason you're still awake at 3am isn't what the doctor told you."
   - Belief-questioning angle: "What if everything you know about sleep is actually making you more tired."
7. **Script order has four elements: Hook, Problem Amplification, Hope Spot, Solution Buildup.** [03:00]
8. **Problem amplification makes the problem feel worse than the viewer thought.** [03:00]
   - Find new ways to scare them beyond what they already know.
   - Show why everything they tried before is doomed to fail.
   - Build tension until they are desperate for a solution.
   - Example: "those sleeping pills are making your natural sleep patterns worse."
9. **The hope spot builds credibility with a discovery story before any product.** The story should present something unique that others missed and that isn't common knowledge. The target thought is "I've never heard of this, this sounds promising." Examples: "Everything changed when I met this strange sleep researcher in Japan"; "this ancient herb monks have used for centuries". [03:00]
10. **Solution buildup is gradual.** Don't dump everything at once, such as the full ingredient list. [03:00]
11. **Drop breadcrumbs in this order, like a Netflix episode.** Don't front-load all studies and benefits. [06:00]
    1. Unique mechanism: "what these monks discovered wasn't just another sleeping aid."
    2. Simple science with interesting details: "this herb repairs your brain's sleep-wake cycle."
    3. Studies, stacked proofs, results, testimonials.
    4. Anticipation, then a cliffhanger: "but what happened next shocked even our research team", escalating to "even crazier".
12. **Every section should leave the viewer asking "but what happens next?"** The goal is building belief in the solution, not only retention. [06:00]
13. **A VSL is "glorified UGC".** UGC is short and punchy. A VSL changes beliefs. As a beginner you usually get the brief, script and assets and assemble them. [06:00]
14. **Build order, step 1: fix the audio of the main content (A-roll or voiceover) first.** Everything sits on top of it, and bad audio ruins the piece regardless of B-roll. [09:00]
15. **Step 2: remove all silences, bad words and bad takes** until delivery is clear and smooth. [09:00]
16. **Step 3: add B-roll that amplifies each script beat.** [09:00]
    - Sleep deprivation: someone missing an alarm, or exhausted at work.
    - Japanese researcher: footage that feels authentically Japanese.
    - Ancient herb: exotic locations, traditional methods, herb processing.
    - Customer results: real people and real transformations. They can be ugly and unedited, because that reads as authentic.
17. **Step 4: add text and graphics for context, but don't make a "PowerPoint presentation".** Start with baseline captions. [09:00]
    - Consistent throughout and in the same position.
    - Usually bottom center or slightly above.
    - Clear, readable and plain-font.
    - Solid background or drop shadow so they read on any footage.
    - Readable on a phone. If not, redo them.
18. **Layer emphasis graphics only on key moments.** Key moments are shocking statistics, crucial points and main benefits. Baseline captions carry everything else. Emphasis visuals must be simple enough that "a fifth grader could understand it". Animating every word or part "to help retention" is "working backwards". [12:00]
19. **Match editing style to the audience.** Example: a joint-health supplement for a 60-year-old versus a pre-workout for a 25-year-old gym bro. No parameters are given. [12:00]
20. **Assignment: a full VSL for a hockey protective-gear client.** [12:00]
    - Assets: the actual script, a professional voiceover, product shots and custom hockey footage.
    - Also provided: an inspiration board of edited VSLs with his like/dislike notes.
    - After submission he shows how an agency editor handled it.
    - Expect "seven, eight, nine, 10 hours" or a few days. Don't get discouraged.
21. **Treat the brief as practice.** Put in days of effort, don't expect perfection, and use the feedback. VSLs are "not an ad you can do in an hour", and clients pay premium rates for editors who can handle them. Next lesson: high production ads. [15:00]

## Editing recipe

1. Fix the audio of the main content (talking head or voiceover). [09:00]
2. Cut silences, bad words and bad takes until delivery flows. [09:00]
3. Lay B-roll over each script beat so it illustrates what is said. Use real, unpolished footage for results and testimonials. [09:00]
4. Add baseline captions: consistent, same position, bottom center or slightly above, plain font, solid background or shadow, phone-readable. [09:00]
5. Add simple emphasis graphics or animation only on key points, statistics and main benefits. Leave everything else to the captions. [12:00]
6. Check that the style fits the audience, and check phone readability. [12:00]

## How an ffmpeg/Python agent applies this

1. **VSL fit and length (1–2):** JUDGE. Check brief and niche: does it need education, and is the audience older? Nothing to automate in ffmpeg.
2. **Mini-movie juggling (3):** AUTOMATE structure. Build a per-beat manifest (script beat → VO segment → B-roll clip → caption → optional graphic) in Python, then assemble with ffmpeg concat and overlay. JUDGE the weakest layer by watching the result.
3. **3-second hook (4–6):** JUDGE. A model or human checks that the first 3 seconds contain a personal struggle plus a shocking twist, and that it qualifies the audience. AUTOMATE: render the first 3 s as a separate test clip with the hook caption burned in (ASS).
4. **Script structure and breadcrumbs (7–13):** JUDGE. Use an LLM to tag the script into Hook, Problem, Hope Spot and Solution beats, and check that mechanism, simple science, proof and cliffhanger arrive in that order with no ingredient dump. AUTOMATE: use the tags as section markers for B-roll and graphics density.
5. **Audio first (14):** AUTOMATE. Run highpass, denoise (`afftdn`) and `loudnorm` on the main track before any other step. JUDGE: listen or check spectral and loudness numbers for artefacts.
6. **Cut silences, bad words, bad takes (15):**
   - AUTOMATE silences with `silencedetect`, cutting via `atrim`/`select` or concat.
   - AUTOMATE bad words and retakes with a word-level Whisper transcript, flagging repeated phrases and fillers.
   - JUDGE final flow by listening around the cut points.
7. **B-roll per beat (16):**
   - AUTOMATE by mapping transcript beats to stock or AI-generated clips and overlaying them over the VO with ffmpeg.
   - JUDGE relevance and authenticity (a vision model or human checks that footage matches the line, including Japan, herb and real-customer beats).
   - For results beats, don't over-polish.
8. **Baseline captions (17):** AUTOMATE.
   - Generate ASS from word timestamps, bottom center at about 80–85% frame height.
   - Use a plain font and a box or shadow (BorderStyle 3, or Shadow plus Outline).
   - Keep the same style throughout. Pull-quote style (quote marks, glow, gradient) can be done with an ASS overlay and a drawn gradient.
   - JUDGE phone readability by rendering at about 360–400 px wide and checking size and contrast.
9. **Emphasis only on key moments (18):**
   - AUTOMATE statistic and benefit callouts as PIL or Python-rendered cards or animated infographics, composited with ffmpeg `overlay` and `zoompan` or fades at timestamps from the transcript.
   - JUDGE which moments deserve it (key points, stats, main benefits only), and keep each graphic simple. Cap graphics density so it doesn't become a PowerPoint.
10. **Audience-matched style (19):** JUDGE. Pick pacing, caption style and graphic density from the audience (for example a calmer, larger-caption, slower style for a 60-year-old versus a faster one for a gym-bro 25-year-old). The lesson gives no numbers.
11. **Hockey VSL assignment (20):** AUTOMATE the assembly with the pipeline above (script, VO, product shots, footage). SKIP the Notion inspiration board unless the files are supplied. JUDGE the output against the reference examples.
12. **Effort and premium pay (21):** SKIP. This is motivational and has no technical content.
