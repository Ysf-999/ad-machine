# A03 — 3 video ad safezone guidelines (Video Ads Mastery)

**Meta crops and covers a 9:16 ad differently in Feed, Stories and Reels, so keep all captions, text and product shots inside the green safe-zone overlay and export one 9:16 file (1080×1920) instead of per-placement versions.**

## Key lessons

1. **Safe zones are a major performance lever that many brands skip.** Optimizing for them puts you "a step ahead", because many ads in the Meta Ad Library and Atria ignore them. [00:00]
2. **One ad can run in Feed, Stories and Reels (Facebook and Instagram), and each placement looks different.** In Feed the ad is cropped. In Stories the top and bottom are cut. In Posts it is cropped. In Reels it is full screen, with UI on top. [00:00–03:45]
3. **Don't export twice.** The naive workaround is a 9:16 export plus a duplicate re-optimized for 4:5 (1080×1350). The goal is a single export that is safe in all placements. [00:00, 06:00]
4. **Everything important must be inside the green zone.** All text, captions and product shots must be visible inside it. "Anything that's not green is a no-no." The video itself stays full frame at 1080×1920, 30 fps; only the important elements must sit in the zone. [03:00]
5. **Avoid the right-side button column and the bottom band.** The right column holds like, comment, share and save. The bottom band holds the caption, profile name, description and "Learn more"/CTA button. Placing things there is a huge issue. In Reels the CTA, advertiser name and description hide any text underneath. [03:00, 06:00]
6. **Small spill past the left and right edges is tolerated, but not top or bottom.** It "isn't a huge mistake" on the sides. Even so, keep text away from the left and right edges, because Reels crops the sides slightly, and he states "nothing should ever be outside of these safe zones". [03:00, 09:00]
7. **Feed crops the top and bottom of the 9:16 frame.** Text outside the zone gets cut off. In the bad example, the hook "Ladies, men are OBSESSED with this smart wallet!" is cut in Feed so viewers see only "with this smart wallet". Other captions ("airtag attachment / SO HE NEVER LOSES IT AGAIN", "holds up 12+ cards & cash") are half-visible or lose half a word. [06:00]
8. **Check with a semi-transparent overlay while editing.** If your editor has no custom-overlay feature, put the safe-zone graphic on the top track at 40% opacity. [06:00]
9. **The product must stay visible inside the zone throughout the ad.** Captions and bottom text must be inside too. [09:00]
10. **Meta video ads are either 9:16 or 4:5 (1080×1350).** 4:5 has no safe zones, so you can place things anywhere. It looks as designed in Feed. On Reels and Stories it is cropped to fill the screen, with leftover space filled by colour or UI. For horizontal footage, don't stretch it to vertical, because too much is cropped; export 4:5 instead. [09:00]
11. **The default export is 9:16.** 4:5 is only sometimes run on its own. Following the safe-zone guidelines optimizes the ad for all Meta placements. [12:00]

## Editing recipe

1. Create a 1080×1920, 30 fps project and edit as normal.
2. Load the safe-zone overlay (a green PNG supplied by the course).
   - In Final Cut Pro: View → Custom Overlay (toggleable).
   - Otherwise: place the PNG on the top track at 40% opacity.
3. Place the hook headline, captions and end text inside the green area. Keep them clear of the right button column, the bottom UI band and the top band.
4. Frame the product and talent so they sit inside the green throughout.
5. Scrub the whole timeline and fix any element outside the green. Slight left/right overshoot is tolerable.
6. Remove or toggle off the overlay, then export one 9:16 file at 1080×1920.
7. Optionally check in Meta Ads Manager's Ad preview (Facebook feed, Instagram Stories, Reels, Posts).
8. For horizontal footage or a deliberate 4:5 ad, build a 1080×1350 project instead. There are no safe-zone constraints, but it will be cropped on Reels and Stories.

## How an ffmpeg/Python agent applies this

1. **Sources of the safe zone (lessons 1–2).**
   - JUDGE the strategic point; it needs no action.
   - AUTOMATE the check: load the supplied green PNG and compute the bounding box and mask of the green pixels with Python (PIL/numpy). That mask defines the usable area, including the notch. No numeric margins are given in the lesson, so derive them from the file.
2. **Place captions inside the zone (4, 5, 6, 7).**
   - AUTOMATE: write ASS captions with `MarginL`, `MarginR`, `MarginV` and `\an`/`\pos` derived from the mask's bounding box, with `PlayResX=1080`, `PlayResY=1920`.
   - Wrap lines (`\N`) so text width stays inside the green.
   - Keep captions out of the right button column and bottom band.
   - Verify by rendering caption-only frames (alpha) and checking that all non-transparent pixels fall inside the mask. Fail or shift if not.
3. **Product and face inside the zone (4, 9).** JUDGE: sample frames (e.g. every 0.5 s), overlay the mask, and have a vision model or human confirm the product and face are inside the green. Flag frames where they are not.
4. **Overlay preview at 40% opacity (8).**
   - AUTOMATE: `ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -i safezone.png -filter_complex "[1]format=rgba,colorchannelmixer=aa=0.4[o];[0][o]overlay" qa.mp4`.
   - Use this for QA frames or contact sheets only; never in the final export.
5. **Feed crop simulation (2, 7).** AUTOMATE a rough check: crop the frame to a 4:5 (1080×1350) centered window and see whether text pixels fall in the cropped-away top and bottom bands. This is an inference from the lesson (Feed crops to 4:5), not an exact Meta spec.
6. **Single 9:16 export (3, 11).** AUTOMATE: `-vf scale=1080:1920,fps=30` (when the source differs), H.264, one output file.
7. **4:5 for horizontal footage (10).**
   - AUTOMATE: for deliberate 4:5 or horizontal footage, make a 1080×1350 export. Use `scale` + `crop`/`pad` to 1080×1350 rather than stretching to 9:16.
   - No safe-zone mask is applied. JUDGE whether the crop keeps the subject framed.
8. **Final Cut custom overlay and Ads Manager preview (4, 8).** SKIP: GUI-only. Replace with the mask-based checks above. A human can still preview placements in Ads Manager if available.
