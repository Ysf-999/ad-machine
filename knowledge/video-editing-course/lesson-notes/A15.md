# A15 — 15 my tech stack for editing video ads (Video Ads Mastery)

**Summary:** A tour of the agency's editing tech stack, shown as a checklist in Apple Notes. It covers editing software and plugins, where to get footage, music/SFX and ad inspiration, and the instructor's main point: you need few tools, and volume of reps is what makes you better. There are no timeline edits and no numeric editing parameters. The value is in the sourcing workflows.

## Key lessons

1. **You don't need many tools, the best plugins or secret sites.** Do many ads. You start to see repeating patterns and get better at recognising what content to use and where to get it. [00:00, 27:00–30:00]
2. **Pick any editor.** Final Cut Pro is the instructor's choice (he is on a Mac and it looked simple). It has everything needed but lacks After Effects-level capability. CapCut is similar and fine for beginners. Premiere Pro and After Effects are more flexible and capable, and worth the upfront learning time if you plan to do this long term. The same work can be done in any editor. [00:00–06:00]
3. **Plugins are add-ons for a specific editor that make editing easier and faster.** Examples are effects, transitions, animations, and prebuilt zoom in / zoom out so you don't keyframe them manually. Many free resources exist online. No plugin names are given, and plugin resources are promised in the community. [03:00–06:00]
4. **Footage comes from two places.** One is the brand's own footage (product, creators). The other is extra footage that enhances the video, mostly b-roll supporting what the script says (e.g. coffee b-roll for a coffee brand). [03:00–06:00]
5. **Use as much of the brand's footage as possible.** Use other footage only when you aren't showing the product, e.g. when talking about other solutions. Scrape social media first, then use stock (Pexels, Artlist, Envato) to support the ad. [18:00–21:00]
6. **Source b-roll from TikTok by searching a keyword that matches the script.** Examples are "coffee" or "humidifier". Prefer clips with no captions or text so they drop in cleanly. Match the clip to what the script says (a script line about bad or plastic humidifiers gets plastic-humidifier clips). Whenever you don't know how to show something visually, search TikTok for the topic. [06:00–12:00]
7. **TikTok download workflow:** open the video, click "copy link", paste it into the downloader (ssstik.io), and click "Without watermark / HD". The result is a watermark-free MP4 in the highest quality. [06:00–09:00]
8. **UrleBird searches TikTok by hashtag and by user.** It shows posting date, views, likes and comments counts (not comment text), and each clip has a "download without watermark" option. [09:00–12:00]
9. **Instagram:** search a hashtag or keyword, copy the link and paste it into iGram (igram.world). It auto-detects video, photo, story or reel. The instructor calls it the most efficient tool he has found. [09:00–12:00]
10. **YouTube:** paste the link into y2mate, which "most of the times" gives 1080p. Scraping YouTube documentaries for a niche worked for the Shilajit ads (harvesting documentaries) and "we never got into any trouble". [09:00–15:00]
11. **Finding clips on TikTok, Instagram and YouTube is "a separate skill".** It improves by editing more scripts, hitting the "what clips do I use" problem, solving it and remembering what worked. [12:00–15:00]
12. **Legal risk with creators' footage is low but real.** They've had little trouble. The creator could ask for removal or could sue ("probably"). Usually the creator sees themselves in an ad, doesn't know the brand, and asks for the ad to be taken down. Safest is pure b-roll where creators' faces aren't shown. [06:00–09:00]
13. **Envato Elements is the one paid resource he recommends.** It helped him a lot early on. [12:00–18:00]
    - It offers stock video, usually 4K, with a vertical filter.
    - It also has video templates, music (filterable by theme and genre), SFX, graphics, 3D, fonts and add-ons.
    - Titles and captions work by dragging and dropping a template or plugin and changing the text or number.
    - Logo reveals work by swapping in your image.
    - Templates can be filtered by app: After Effects, Premiere Pro, Apple Motion, Final Cut Pro, DaVinci Resolve. CapCut is not supported.
14. **Pexels is the free version of Envato for photos and video.** It has a vertical filter but no plugins or templates. [15:00–18:00]
15. **Artlist is mainly music and SFX, but it recently added high-quality stock footage with a "Vertical Only" filter** (useful for 9:16). It has no templates comparable to Envato's. [18:00–21:00]
16. **Music research:** search the brand's niche on TikTok (e.g. "jewelry"), open several videos, and listen to which music and sounds trend. If TikTok won't let you download, identify the song (title and artist) and search YouTube. [18:00–21:00]
17. **Trending music download:** search YouTube for the song plus "tiktok version". Copy the link, paste it into y2mate, go to the Audio tab and download (MP3, 128kbps, 1.9 MB in the demo). If a TikTok or Reel just says "original sound", use Shazam on a phone to get the name, then repeat the search. [21:00–24:00]
18. **Avoid original versions of big artists' songs (e.g. The Weeknd).** They are flagged more easily. Use the TikTok version, slowed version, reverb version or other slightly edited versions, which are "not as easily flagged by Facebook's algorithm". He says this hack "has been working well so far". [21:00–24:00]
19. **Stock music and SFX come from Artlist.** The Music section filters by genre, instrument, mood and video theme. Sound Effects is a separate section that you search by the effect you need ("if you need a whoosh, you search for whoosh"). The SFX results shown are about 00:02 each. [21:00–27:00]
20. **Inspiration habit:** whenever you see a good hook or video ad on TikTok or Instagram, save it (copy the link or use Instagram saved folders). Go back to the folder when you hit a creative block, then replicate it or take inspiration from it. [24:00–27:00]
21. **Team practice:** keep a daily #ad-inspo channel (Discord) where anyone posts a good ad and explains what they like about it. The community gets an equivalent "Ecom Talent Inspo Channel". [24:00–27:00]
22. **Atria (tryatria.com):** scroll the Discovery feed, filter by niche/industry (his example is "beauty and personal care"), and save ads to named boards such as "good hooks" or "good edits". Reference these boards when editing. [24:00–30:00]
23. **Meta Ad Library:** search any brand (his example is AG1 by Athletic Greens) to see all its ads. Use it when Atria lacks a brand's full set of ads. Filter by active status/type, when the ads were active, and media type (images vs videos). [27:00–30:00]
24. **The list is the basics.** There is no secret tool or resource, and no AI tools are included (AI is covered in a separate module/video). "Everything you need to start making video ads today." [27:00–33:00]

## Editing recipe

This lesson has no timeline edit. The sourcing sequence he follows for a new ad is:

1. Start from the brand's own footage (product, creators).
2. For script lines with no brand footage, search TikTok, Instagram and YouTube for the script topic, and pick clean clips with no captions.
3. Download each clip without a watermark via ssstik.io / UrleBird (TikTok), igram.world (Instagram), or y2mate (YouTube).
4. Fill remaining gaps with stock from Pexels (free), Artlist or Envato Elements.
5. Choose music by checking what trends in the niche on TikTok, then download a TikTok, slowed or reverb version via YouTube + y2mate (Audio tab).
6. Add SFX such as whooshes from Artlist.
7. Use templates or plugins for titles, captions, transitions, zooms and logo reveals.
8. Keep an inspiration folder, Discord channel and Atria boards to return to when stuck.

## How an ffmpeg/Python agent applies this

1. **Tool minimalism / reps (L1, L24):** JUDGE. A mindset lesson. The agent should favour producing many ads and logging which clip sources worked for which niche.
2. **Editor choice (L2):** SKIP. The agent works in ffmpeg + Python and has no GUI editor, so Final Cut/Premiere/CapCut don't apply.
3. **Plugins (L3):** AUTOMATE the equivalents. Zooms via ffmpeg `zoompan`/`crop`+`scale` expressions, transitions via `xfade`, captions via burned-in ASS (`subtitles`/`ass` filter). Packaged plugin/template assets are SKIP (GUI-only).
4. **Brand footage first (L4–5):** AUTOMATE. Ingest brand clips first, and fall back to external/stock clips only for script lines that don't show the product. This can be a script-line-to-clip mapping step with a "brand clip available?" flag.
5. **Keyword-match b-roll search (L6):** JUDGE. A model should take each script line, generate search keywords, and check candidates visually (frame sampling plus a vision model). Reject clips with burned-in text or captions (an OCR check on sampled frames is possible) and clips with identifiable faces.
6. **Downloading from TikTok/Instagram/YouTube (L7–10):** AUTOMATE only with a CLI downloader (e.g. yt-dlp, which isn't named in the lesson). The lesson's click-through web downloaders are GUI-only. Output should be an MP4 at the highest available quality, and the agent should check the result with `ffprobe`. Use only if the user permits, since this violates platform terms and carries creator-complaint risk.
7. **Legal risk (L12):** JUDGE. Prefer faceless b-roll. Flag any clip that shows a recognisable creator's face, and log source URLs so takedown requests can be handled quickly.
8. **Envato/Pexels/Artlist stock (L13–15):**
   - AUTOMATE Pexels via its free API, filtering by orientation=portrait.
   - Envato/Artlist need paid accounts, so the agent only handles files the user supplies (check with `ffprobe` for 9:16 and resolution).
   - Envato/FCP templates are SKIP (GUI/plugin-bound).
   - Where Envato titles, captions or logo reveals are used, rebuild them with ASS captions or ffmpeg overlays.
9. **Music research (L16–18):** JUDGE plus partial AUTOMATE. Trend listening needs a human or audio model, so the agent can only take a user-supplied song title. Searching YouTube and extracting audio can be scripted (yt-dlp `-x`), subject to the same permission caveat. Prefer a slowed/reverb/TikTok variant, and optionally generate one with ffmpeg (`atempo`, `aecho`/reverb) as a copyright-flag hack. The instructor says it works so far, so treat it as unguaranteed. Shazam identification is SKIP (phone app), so ask the user for the song name.
10. **Stock music/SFX (L19):** AUTOMATE the mixing of user-supplied Artlist files. Add SFX such as whooshes at cut points (`adelay` + `amix`), with music ducked under VO (`sidechaincompress` or a fixed volume). Choosing a track by mood is JUDGE.
11. **Inspiration library (L20–23):** JUDGE/SKIP. Atria and Meta Ad Library are web UIs. The agent can keep its own inspo folder of links plus notes on what's liked, and analyse reference ads (shot lengths via `ffmpeg scdet`, caption style by frame sampling). Discord/Atria boards are SKIP unless the user supplies exports or links.
