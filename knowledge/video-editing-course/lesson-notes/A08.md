# A08 — 8 action item vsl ad (Video Ads Mastery)

**Two VSL assignment briefs: (1) a hockey neck-guard VSL (Warroad / TILO) where the editor invents the visuals from a sparse script, and (2) a coffee-cup VSL (Stoke / Tok Melbourne) in a fast, hectic, tension-building style that relaxes at the hold. No editing technique is demonstrated.**

## Key lessons

1. **VSL #1 deliverable: 3 hooks, 1 hold, 2 CTAs, 3 total ads.** The ads are the 3 hook variations. Hook 1 uses "CTA for Hook 1". Hooks 2 and 3 share "CTA for Hook 2 & 3", because the context differs. [00:00-03:00]
2. **VSL #1 hook copy to cut:**
   - Hook 1: "Top 3 Neck Guard Mistakes Hockey Parents Make".
   - Hook 2: "3 big mistakes hockey players make when choosing a neck guard".
   - Hook 3: "Avoid these 3 big mistakes when choosing a neck guard". [00:00-03:00]
3. **Invent the visuals yourself for VSL #1.** The script has only sparse visual suggestions on purpose. The instructor wants you to "come up with your own stuff of how to visualize parts of the script." Each section ends with an empty "VISUALS:" box. [00:00-03:00]
4. **Use the supplied assets for VSL #1.**
   - Footage Google Drive (not yet uploaded at recording time).
   - Logo and brand guidelines.
   - Brand name and product page link.
   - Full script.
   - Separate hook VOs plus one VO for the whole script, titled "3 Big Neck Guards Fails". [00:00-03:00]
5. **Illustrate script claims literally in VSL #1.** The board's visual notes include:
   - "Show this" for the hook-and-loop fastener mistake, with a Bauer neck-protect product link and the Neck Guard Vertical MP4.
   - Fair-use hockey collision video, a skate swinging up in the air, and skate blades being sharpened.
   - An ANSI rating scale graphic (Warroad DCT #86 ANSI Scale Vertical).
   - A cut-resistant gardening hand-protection reference image (ANSI cut level 3).
   - The closing line "Then you want TILO, by Warroad Hockey Co." [00:00-03:00]
6. **The CTAs end by showing or pointing to the link.** Both CTA cards end with "[Show or point to link]". Hook 1 CTA copy begins "Give your players the comfort they deserve…". Hook 2 & 3 CTA copy begins "After all, if you have to wear your neck guard…". [00:00-03:00]
7. **VSLs are tedious but worth it.** They take a lot of work, and making good ones is a separate, valuable skill for a portfolio. The agency's edited VSL #1 was loved by the client but did not perform well. The instructor says the editing is how a good VSL should be edited. After you submit, he sends back the agency editor's version to compare. [00:00-03:00]
8. **VSL #2 is an editing style of its own.** It is "super fast-paced, almost hectic", with "a lot of visuals, a lot of stuff going on", building tension and playing with sound design. It is filed under VSL only because the scripts are longer and more explanatory. [03:00-06:00]
9. **VSL #2 structure: 3 hooks, 1 hold, 3 total ads.**
   - The hooks are not small variations of one short hook. Each is "a completely different angle", a longer lead-in.
   - After the hooks comes the hold (the body, which talks about the product), then the CTA. [03:00-06:00]
10. **Pacing contrast in VSL #2: hectic hooks, tranquil hold and CTA.**
    - Hooks are very fast with hectic music, building tension.
    - At the hold, "chill out the script": slow down and make it more tranquil. Relaxed is the emotion the product should evoke.
    - The board note reads "Will need to slow it down + make it more tranquil in the hold/CTA". [03:00-09:00]
11. **VSL #2 hook visuals are quick and shocking.** Examples: microplastics in water, a landfill overflowing with coffee cups, coffee mugs breaking, and a close-up of someone drinking from a stained, cracked mug. Hooks 2 and 3 use "same vibes as hook 1", reordered or changed to fit their script. [03:00-09:00]
12. **Put captions on parts of the script.** They illustrate figures and make the ad engaging, "as seen in example". In the hold, key phrases get captions too, e.g. "World's First Shatterproof Ceramic Cup". [03:00-09:00]
13. **Colour-coded sticky notes map shot ideas to script highlights.** The highlighted phrases in the hook script (yellow, pink, blue, black) are the ones to illustrate. The opening line is "You are being fooled." Shot ideas by colour:
    - Green: someone drinking or preparing coffee in a plastic cup.
    - Yellow: a quick match-cut scene of about 4–5 coffee-shop videos, made fast, maybe one showing someone grabbing a plastic cup.
    - Pink: coins and money (falling or scattered coins, pennies).
    - Blue: a production line making cups.
    - Black: an "oddly satisfying clip" (ferromagnetic fluid).
    - Grey: a person drinking coffee, plus a barista giving coffee to the person at the end of the video. [03:00-09:00]
14. **Where to source footage for VSL #2.**
    - Stock: Artlist and Envato links on the notes.
    - Social: TikTok reference clips.
    - The client's Dropbox "Marketing Material" folder, which is huge with many subfolders. "Definitely make sure to kind of sniff through that."
    - The brand's Instagram (sttokeofficial, 59.6K followers): reels and the Tagged tab. Clips can be downloaded and used. [03:00-09:00]
15. **VO is pre-supplied as AI audio.** VSL #2 has ElevenLabs / "Jessica AI Voice Jan 16 2025" files (.mp3 and .wav) per hook, hold and CTA. [03:00-09:39]
16. **Put real effort in.** "Definitely, definitely don't half-ass these." Finished ads work as portfolio pieces for beginners. [09:00-09:39]

## Editing recipe

No edit is walked through. The brief implies this order for VSL #2:
1. Lay the hook VO (3 separate hooks, each a different angle).
2. Cut rapid, shocking visuals to the highlighted phrases, with hectic music and tension-building sound design. Add captions on figures.
3. At the hold, switch to relaxing, slow-paced visuals, calmer music and captions on key phrases.
4. Cover product talk using client and brand footage.
5. Add the CTA. For VSL #1, end by showing or pointing to the link.
6. Export one ad per hook. VSL #1 pairs Hook 1 with its own CTA, and Hooks 2 and 3 with the shared CTA.

## How an ffmpeg/Python agent applies this

1. **Deliverable count (1, 2):** AUTOMATE. Build a manifest with one output per hook (VSL #1: hooks 1, 2, 3, with the CTA mapping 1→CTA1, 2 and 3→CTA2) and concat hook + hold + CTA with ffmpeg. Export the sizes the brief ticks, via scale/crop/pad per aspect ratio.
2. **Invent visuals (3, 5):** JUDGE. A model should read the script line by line and propose a visual per claim (e.g. ANSI scale graphic, skate-blade footage). Then check that each cut matches its line.
3. **Supplied assets (4, 14, 15):** AUTOMATE. Ingest the Drive/Dropbox folders, ffprobe all clips, and index them by content (frame sampling plus a vision model or CLIP) so shots can be retrieved per script line. Use the supplied VO as the master audio track. Instagram clips may only be fetched by a human or via permitted tooling.
4. **CTA pointing to the link (6):** AUTOMATE. Overlay the link or URL text and a logo via the ASS/drawtext/overlay filters in the CTA segment. JUDGE whether the "point to" gesture reads clearly.
5. **Study the agency version (7):** SKIP. It is a human review step. The agent can only compare if it is given the reference file, in which case it can diff cut counts and pacing.
6. **Hectic style and tension (8, 10):** AUTOMATE the pacing. Detect the VO word timings (forced alignment or whisper), set a short average shot length in the hooks and a longer one in the hold, and generate cut points from the word timings. Mix a hectic music bed for the hooks and a calmer bed for the hold/CTA with amix and volume/fades. JUDGE whether the tension and calm actually land. The brief gives no numeric shot lengths, so choose them yourself.
7. **Structure with different angles (9):** AUTOMATE the assembly (hook files + hold + CTA). The hook angles come from the supplied scripts.
8. **Shocking visuals and highlighted phrases (11, 13):** AUTOMATE. Map the highlighted phrases to VO timestamps and assign a clip to each from the stock/client library, including the 4–5 fast match-cut coffee-shop clips. JUDGE the clip relevance (microplastics, cracked mug, coins, production line).
9. **Captions on figures and key phrases (12):** AUTOMATE. Burn ASS captions at the timestamps of the figures or key phrases, e.g. "World's First Shatterproof Ceramic Cup". JUDGE legibility against the footage.
10. **Reference-ad look (collage, PiP, cut-out stills over textured background):** AUTOMATE partially. Use overlay for PiP, a textured background loop with PNG cut-outs overlaid, and zoompan for motion. JUDGE the visual quality.
11. **Effort and portfolio (16):** SKIP. It is motivational.
