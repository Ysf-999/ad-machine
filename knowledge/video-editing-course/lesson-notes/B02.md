# B02 — 2 live example editing a video ad (Video Editing Breakdowns)

**One-line summary.** The instructor rebuilds a Stoke ceramic-coffee-cup UGC ad (Arcads AI-creator footage) live in Final Cut Pro, using a fixed layer order: A-roll, then music, then b-roll, then enhancements. The ad is deliberately "organic": creator green-screened over b-roll, TikTok-native captions, no transitions or SFX.

## Key lessons

1. **Divide the edit into layers and finish each one before starting the next.** The order is (1) A-roll/VO, (2) music, (3) b-roll, (4) animations/transitions, (5) captions/text, (6) sound effects. Layers 1–3 are the core content; 4–6 only "enhance the edit". Once a layer is done, don't revisit it. Single-task passes (e.g. 30 minutes of only b-roll) keep you in a "flow zone". The anti-pattern is VO, then b-roll in one spot, then an animation, then back to fix VO, then captions in one place: "very messy" and slower. [00:00–05:00, 51:00–54:00]
2. **Choose the music at the start, even before editing.** If the track has drops, position the video around them. [00:00–03:00]
3. **Read the brief and know the angle before cutting.** Here the angle was "your last sip feels like your first". The brief had three hook scripts, one shared body and optional b-roll ideas per hook, so one body can be tested with different hooks. His stated volume is about 5–6 concepts a day, each with 3 variations. [00:00–09:00]
4. **Fix the audio first.** Voiceover levels should sit between -6 and -12 dB, with -6 dB the maximum. In the example he nudged the gain up in about 0.1 dB steps until the meter peaked at -6. [09:00–12:00]
5. **One hook per variation.** The raw take had three hooks back to back, about 1 s apart. Variation 1 keeps hook 1 and cuts hooks 2 and 3. The body stays identical across variations. [09:00–12:00]
6. **Tighten the A-roll or VO to the bone.** Cut dead air, breaths and bad takes, using the waveform to find silences (one gap was about 0.1 s). Leave "just a bit" of pause at some points so it stays natural. Don't give viewers space to scroll away. If you only have a VO, fill the whole video with b-roll with no dead spots. The timeline went from about 1:04 to about 44:28 (FCP timecode) after the tightening pass. [12:00–18:00]
7. **Make a testimonial feel camouflaged and organic with a "green screen" concept.** The creator is cut out and placed in front of supporting visuals, like TikTok reaction videos, so it looks like she edited it herself. He says this is not a rule; some ads can look obviously edited. [15:00–21:00]
8. **Cut the creator out for free in CapCut, then key her in Final Cut.** [18:00–21:00]
   - Import the video, open Video > Cutout and tick Auto cutout, then check the edges.
   - In Basic, tick Canvas, set it to Color = green (hex 0FFF00), and export.
   - In Final Cut, apply Green Screen Keyer to the exported clip.
   - Newer editors may have built-in subject cutout, but he prefers this free "old way".
9. **Use a dual-track green-screen stack.** [21:00–24:00]
   - Put the keyed copy directly above the unkeyed copy.
   - Make every cut on both layers so they stay perfectly synced.
   - Per scene, toggle layers on or off. Hook: keyed on, with the speaker shrunk and videos behind. Next scene: keyed off, speaker full frame. Following scene: keyed back on.
   - The shrunk speaker was seen at about X 322 px, Y -224 px (lower right of 1080×1920).
10. **Pick music that fits the vibe and what the niche consumes.** He used "Orange Coffee" (Rocketman) for the storytelling beat and the "coffee girlies" audience, found by searching TikTok for "coffee", "day in the life" and aesthetic coffee videos. The default is no vocals (look for an instrumental). If there are vocals under speech, make sure they don't overpower the speaker. Music sat under the full ~47 s edit; the inspector showed -18.0 dB at times (the final level is unclear). [21:00–27:00]
11. **Build the hook from a relatable emotion so viewers feel understood.** [27:00–30:00]
    - Search TikTok with several keyword variants ("cold coffee", "when your coffee gets cold", "cold coffee feeling").
    - The ~7 s clip "When coffee gets cold.." went in as the background with the talent keyed over it.
    - Mute the downloaded clip's audio (-∞ dB) so only the talent's voice plays.
    - Check text against safe zones with a custom overlay at 50% opacity.
12. **Illustrate every script line, and pattern-break constantly.** Listen to what she says and find footage that adds context. "Every few seconds something new should come on." In the ad "something new happens like every two to three seconds". The b-roll lane is dense, with clips about 1–2 s or less. [30:00–33:00, 45:00–48:00]
13. **Don't force product footage you don't have.** If she never recorded herself drinking from the cup, use her on screen for authenticity or use other footage. [30:00–33:00]
14. **Source authentic-looking stills from Pinterest.** For "cold undrinkable last half" he searched Pinterest for photos that look like something she took. [33:00–36:00]
    - Iterate queries ("plastic coffee cup aesthetic", "…trash can", then "coffee cup in trash can" hit).
    - Keep the same cup across shots for continuity.
    - A TikTok spilled-coffee video also works, but he preferred stills.
15. **Mine the brand's own Instagram for UGC and influencer content.** He found a paid-partnership "day in the life" reel and cut it into short clips, one moment of product use each. Time-of-day text (6am, 9:30am, 2pm, 7pm) visualises "lasts all day". Show the product in the first clip. Steaming drink footage shows temperature retention. [36:00–39:00]
16. **For negative claims, show competitors in a bad light.** For "metallic taste" he searched TikTok ("metallic taste", "metallic coffee cups", "bad taste") and used a Yeti clip and a Stanley clip. The clip needn't match exactly. Switch back to product shots when the script pivots to the brand. [39:00–42:00]
17. **When the product has no visible problem/solution demo, sell identification and lifestyle.** "50% of selling this cup or even more is just lifestyle." Close with many aesthetic shots of the target avatar, so viewers can picture themselves with the cup. Still show every product feature. [42:00–48:00]
18. **Vary how the creator appears.** Mix green-screen over b-roll, full screen, green-screen over images, green-screen over video, and none, then back again. [45:00–48:00]
19. **Skip transitions, animations and SFX when the ad must look organic.** The only effect was a "Zoom In Linear" punch-in near the start. [45:00–54:00]
20. **Caption in short, scannable chunks that copy native TikTok/Instagram captions.** "People don't read captions, they scan them." [48:00–54:00]
    - Split "You know that feeling when you take the last sip of your coffee and it's ice cold" into three chunks: "You know that feeling" / "When you take the last sip of your coffee" / "And it's ice cold".
    - Split each chunk into two short lines, e.g. "When you take the / last sip of your coffee".
    - Style: Proxima Nova Semibold, size 28.0 in FCP (size depends on the editor), centered, black outline width 3.0, opacity 100%, blur 0, line spacing 0, tracking 0%, placed lower-middle.
    - To replicate platform captions, write one in an Instagram story, screenshot it and copy it.

## Editing recipe

1. Import the raw creator clip (1080×1920, 30 fps, about 1:04).
2. Set the VO level to peak at -6 dB.
3. Blade out the unused hooks, keeping one hook per variation.
4. Cut every silence and breath through the whole clip (leave a few natural pauses).
5. In CapCut, run Auto cutout, set a green canvas, and export. In FCP, place the export above the original and apply Green Screen Keyer.
6. Cut both layers identically and shrink the keyed one to a small PiP at lower right (about 1/3 width).
7. Add music from a niche-relevant TikTok track, under the speech.
8. Build the hook: a TikTok reaction clip behind the keyed talent, with its audio muted and text inside the safe zones.
9. Go line by line and place b-roll over the creator, toggling keyed and unkeyed per scene:
   - Pinterest stills.
   - Brand Instagram "day in the life" clips with 6am / 9:30am / 2pm / 7pm titles (short, ~1–3 s each).
   - Competitor-bashing TikTok clips (Yeti "leaves metallic taste / coffee stains / can't go in microwave", Stanley).
   - Product and lifestyle shots near the end.
10. Skip transitions, animations and SFX (only a Zoom In Linear on one early clip).
11. Add chunked TikTok-style captions as separate title clips timed to speech.

## How an ffmpeg/Python agent applies this

1. **Layer order** — AUTOMATE as pipeline stages: VO cleanup, music bed, b-roll overlays, optional zoom/transitions, ASS captions, optional SFX. Each stage writes an intermediate file and is never reopened by a later stage.
2. **Music chosen first** — JUDGE: a model or human picks a track matching the niche's taste and drops. AUTOMATE: beat or drop timestamps via librosa.
3. **Brief and hook variants** — AUTOMATE: parse the script into hook and body, and render one output per hook with the same body. JUDGE: check the hooks work.
4. **Level to -6 dB peak** — AUTOMATE: run `volumedetect`, then apply `volume=<-6 − max_volume>dB`, or use `loudnorm` with a `alimiter=limit=-6dB` peak ceiling. Verify with `astats`.
5. **One hook per variation** — AUTOMATE: transcribe with word timestamps (Whisper), detect the hook boundaries, and `trim`/`atrim` plus concat.
6. **Silence and breath removal** — AUTOMATE: `silencedetect` (e.g. noise -35 dB, d≈0.1) to get gaps. Keep a small pad (~60–100 ms) and cut with `trim`/`atrim` + `concat`, applied identically to video and audio. JUDGE: listen for choppiness, since he leaves some natural pauses.
7. **Camouflaged organic look** — JUDGE: check the final ad doesn't look over-produced.
8. **CapCut cutout and keyer** — AUTOMATE a replacement: skip the green canvas, and generate a person matte with `rembg` or MediaPipe selfie segmentation, then output alpha (ProRes 4444 or PNG sequence). If you do use the green canvas, key with `chromakey=0x00FF00:0.1:0.1`, then `despill`. SKIP the CapCut GUI.
9. **Dual-track toggling** — AUTOMATE: render the creator once as keyed PiP and once full frame, and per scene pick which version goes on the timeline from a scene list. In ffmpeg use `overlay=enable='between(t,a,b)'`. Use the same cut points on both, which is automatic if both derive from one cut list. PiP: scale ~1/3 width (`scale=360:-1`, my conversion), bottom-right, near X +322/Y -224 offset from centre of 1080×1920 (FCP coordinates), converted to overlay x/y.
10. **Music mixing** — AUTOMATE: `amix` or `sidechaincompress` so music sits well under speech (start around -18 dB as seen). Pick instrumentals, or detect vocals and attenuate. JUDGE: listen to confirm vocals don't mask speech and VO stays intelligible. The final level in the video is unclear.
11. **Hook from a relatable emotion, safe zones, muted source audio** — JUDGE: choose the reaction clip. AUTOMATE: `volume=0` on the source clip, and verify text bounding boxes against a safe-zone mask (e.g. render the frame and test overlap with the platform UI regions).
12. **B-roll for every line plus new visual every 2–3 s** — AUTOMATE: align the transcript to segments, then enforce a maximum shot length (≈2–3 s) when building the b-roll timeline, and trim each clip to its best part. JUDGE: a vision model checks each clip matches the line. Generate AI images or video where no footage exists.
13. **No forced product footage** — AUTOMATE: fallback rule is to show the creator when no relevant clip scores above a threshold.
14. **Pinterest, Instagram and TikTok sourcing** — JUDGE/SKIP: the agent can't browse as he does and shouldn't scrape. Use clips supplied by the user, the brand's own UGC library, or AI-generated stills or video that look like phone photos (keep the same cup across shots). Bad-light competitor clips need rights and legal review before use.
15. **Time-of-day overlays and day-in-the-life** — AUTOMATE: cut a long source into 1–3 s clips and burn small white lowercase serif labels ("6am", "9:30am", "2pm", "7pm") with `drawtext` or ASS at mid-right.
16. **Identification and lifestyle b-roll** — JUDGE: choose footage showing the target avatar.
17. **Vary creator treatment** — AUTOMATE: a scene plan that alternates full-frame, PiP over video, and PiP over image.
18. **Skip effects and SFX for organic UGC** — AUTOMATE: a flag that disables the transitions, animation and SFX stages. A simple `zoompan` or crop punch-in is fine if wanted.
19. **Captions** — AUTOMATE: split Whisper word timings into chunks of about 3–5 words, two short lines per card, and burn them as ASS. Use Proxima Nova Semibold if licensed, otherwise a close substitute. Style: bold, white fill, black outline width 3, no blur, centered, lower-middle above the bottom UI zone. FCP's size 28 is not an ASS size, so calibrate on a 1080×1920 test frame. JUDGE: check readability and safe zones, and compare against a native Instagram-story screenshot.
