# A14 — 14 music and sfx (Video Ads Mastery)

**Summary:** Use sound effects to back up every visual and claim. Use music to build and release tension, to emphasize key script lines (including by cutting it out entirely), and to match the story, brand and target audience. Keep it simple. The team uses trending music without checking licensing, and the instructor says he does not advise that.

## Key lessons

1. **Sync a sound effect to every on-screen action or event.** In the Dr. Squatch ad (0:41, no music, SFX only), a key entering a lock gets a lock/key sound. A cash sound plays when the VO says "up to 15% off" over the money-bag graphic. "Whenever you have some kind of visual on your ad, you kind of support it with a sound effect." [00:00–05:00]
2. **Give every item in a list its own SFX hit, and make the hits escalate.** When the VO lists bundle combinations, the SFX rise in pitch ("higher and higher") with each added item. This stresses "you can choose so many things". [00:00–03:00]
3. **Match the SFX type to the content.** [03:00–05:00]
   - Benefits: a tick or ding on each one.
   - Negatives: a buzzer.
   - Transitions: a whoosh, swoosh or swipe.
   - The purpose is more retention, more attention, and support for claims and visuals.
4. **Calibrate SFX to what works for your account.** The instructor's team does not use heavy SFX, and more heavily edited ads "just don't work for us". He still says to try it. [03:00–05:00]
5. **Humor helps recall.** His team found research that "90% of people are more likely to remember funny ads" than other ads. His advice is to make more funny ads, such as the fake "No Chicken Salad Sandwich" gag in the Dr. Squatch ad. [00:00–03:00]
6. **Music triggers emotion and should support the script and visuals.** Use it to make people feel something, to emphasize important parts and words, and to build tension. [05:02, 09:00–15:00]
7. **Build tension with calm music, then switch to fast music for the busy section.** In the Oats Overnight ad (25 s), the first ~2–5 s have chill, wholesome music. After the hook, the music gets faster as the footage turns to cooking, lab and packing. [06:00–09:00]
8. **Drop the music completely on the line you want to land.** Pause it right before or on the key line ("our customers"). The silence puts all focus on that line. Restart the music afterwards and repeat the pattern. [06:00–09:00]
   - Stops are described at roughly 6:55–7:36 and 7:59–8:25 of the lesson.
   - Final payoff: the music stops, tension builds, and the customer says "that's delicious" with no music and no supporting sound.
   - The music returns for the CTA, "Try it out at oatsovernight.com". The ad ends at about 25 s.
9. **Change the music at the story pivot.** In the CarePod ad (1:22, edited by Mufaris), the problem section runs on fast, upbeat, hectic music while "weird stuff is happening". [09:00–12:00]
   - At "Gross, right? That's why CarePod created something different" (~11:57 of the lesson), the music changes for the solution.
   - The new music is very chill, aesthetic lo-fi. The switch lands on the "designed by a doctor" moment. It fits a high-end, almost luxury, cozy, warm brand that "elevates your home". [12:00–15:00]
10. **Same script, different music, different ad.** "This could just be a random script... a voiceover with the same music in the background and the ad will feel totally different." [06:00–09:00]
11. **Match music to what the target audience already consumes.** [12:00–18:00]
    - A jewelry ad for about 20–25-year-olds won with a viral TikTok sound.
    - A winning ad for women aged 30, 35, 40 and 45 used Britney Spears. He thinks that was a factor.
    - The question to ask is "what kind of music do they consume?"
12. **Viral or trending music is claimed to perform better.** Familiar sounds stop fast scrollers. [15:00–18:00]
    - The team uses "random", "viral", "trending" and "popular" music without checking whether it is copyright free.
    - They have had no takedowns or issues "to this date", and he will update if that changes.
    - He says "I don't want to advise you to do that" and calls it a legal gray zone. It is a disclosed risk, not a recommendation.
13. **Match music to story emotion.** An ad about an old dog struggling to walk gets something sad, not rock and roll, "to elevate that story even more". [18:00–21:00]
14. **Keep audio simple.** Don't make it hectic or complicated. Small touches are enough. [15:00–21:00]
15. **Find music in two ways.** [18:00–21:00]
    - For royalty-free ad music, he names "our list.io" (spelling uncertain) and will cover it in the next video on his tech stack.
    - For popular sounds, search TikTok by niche keyword (e.g. "jewelry"). Look at what ordinary creators in that niche use and do the same. For the workout example he thinks they searched "women workout", but is not 100% sure.
16. **Use the Dr. Squatch ad library for inspiration** whenever you run out of ad ideas. [00:00–03:00]

## Editing recipe

This is the music-structure recipe taken from the Oats Overnight breakdown [06:00–09:00]:
1. Start with calm, chill, wholesome music under the hook (first ~2–5 s).
2. After the hook, cut to faster, upbeat music as the explanation/production footage begins.
3. Just before or on each key line (e.g. "our customers"), cut the music out completely so the line sits in silence.
4. Bring the upbeat music back for the next process section.
5. For the final proof line (customer: "that's delicious"), stop the music and build tension. Leave the line with no music and no SFX.
6. Bring the music back for the CTA and the end card (~25 s).

SFX pass [00:00–05:00]:
1. Place a hit on every new visual or graphic.
2. Tick or ding on benefits, buzzer on negatives, whoosh on transitions.
3. Use a cash sound on money or savings mentions.
4. Use rising-pitch hits for lists.

Problem/solution music pass [09:00–15:00]:
1. Put fast, hectic music under the problem section.
2. Swap to chill lo-fi at the pivot line ("designed by a doctor").

## How an ffmpeg/Python agent applies this

1. **SFX synced to visuals (lessons 1, 3).**
   - AUTOMATE: build a cue list from the script and shot-change timestamps, with a type for each cue (tick, buzzer, whoosh, cash). Place each file with `adelay=<ms>|<ms>`, then `amix` with `normalize=0`, or overlay in pydub. Detect cut points with scene-detect, or use the known B-roll edit list.
   - JUDGE: check that each hit lands on the frame the visual appears, and that the SFX are not overdone. The instructor's own team under-uses them, so make density a per-account setting.
2. **Rising-pitch hits for lists (lesson 2).**
   - AUTOMATE: generate N variants of one hit with `asetrate` plus `atempo`, or `rubberband=pitch=…` with increasing ratios. Place one per list item at its word timestamp from forced alignment of the VO.
   - JUDGE: listen that the escalation sounds musical and not shrill.
3. **Humor (lesson 5).**
   - JUDGE: this is a creative call, such as a gag image or a joke line. The agent can only insert a gag overlay (`overlay` with `enable='between(t,a,b)'`) if the script calls for one.
4. **Music tension and release, and cutting out on key lines (lessons 7, 8).**
   - AUTOMATE:
     - Mark key-line start and end times from word-level transcription of the VO.
     - Apply a gate to the music track with `volume='if(between(t,a,b),0,1)':eval=frame`. For a softer move, use short `afade` out/in around the line, or sidechain-duck harder.
     - Use two music stems (chill and upbeat) crossfaded at the hook end, e.g. with `acrossfade`.
     - Leave the final proof line free of both music and SFX.
   - JUDGE: whether the right lines were chosen as key lines. This normally means the proof or emotional peak and the line before the CTA.
5. **Music switch at the pivot (lesson 9).**
   - AUTOMATE: find the pivot line in the transcript ("designed by a doctor" or the equivalent) and crossfade from the problem track to the solution track at that timestamp.
   - JUDGE: check that the energy match is right (hectic vs. chill) and that the brand feel fits.
6. **Match music to audience and story emotion (lessons 6, 10, 11, 13).**
   - JUDGE: needs a model or human to read the brief (audience age, brand vibe, story mood) and pick a track. Check that the choice does not read as wrong for the emotion.
7. **Viral or trending music (lesson 12).**
   - SKIP automated sourcing: the agent cannot browse TikTok sounds reliably, and licensing is a real risk the instructor himself disclaims. Use user-supplied tracks or a licensed library, and flag that the use is the user's risk.
8. **Finding music (lesson 15).**
   - AUTOMATE: only if the user supplies a library path or API. Tag tracks by mood and BPM and filter.
   - SKIP: the "our list.io" name is uncertain. Do not guess a service, and ask the user.
9. **Keep it simple (lesson 14).**
   - AUTOMATE: cap SFX per second and keep the music at a sensible mix level under the VO. The lesson gives no numbers, so pick defaults and expose them as parameters.
10. **Dr. Squatch ad library (lesson 16).**
    - SKIP: it is a human inspiration source. The agent can only analyze a reference ad the user supplies.
