# B06 — 6 video editing breakdown part 2 ugc ad (Video Editing Breakdowns)

**One-line summary.** An agency editor walks through a finished 3-hook scripted UGC ad (Zend Wellness "MetaGlow Collagen", postpartum angle, Batch #39, ~1:01, 9:16) from brief to export. He covers folder and project setup, then editing in layers: A-roll silence removal, voice audio chain, b-roll, scale-alternation jump-cut fixes, captions, hook text cards, one SFX, and export.

## Key lessons

1. **Read the brief closely and ask the strategist about anything unclear before editing.** The brief gives the concept and what the ad needs, and he calls asking "one of my best pieces of advice". [00:00]
2. **Keep the audience in mind.** Most spend for this product goes to women, mainly early 30s to early 50s. Keep the avatar and their desire "on the back of your head". [00:00, 12:00]
3. **UGC ad structure here is 3 hooks + 1 shared hold (body VO), giving 3 total ads.** [03:00]
   - Hooks are copy only, with no script or VO.
   - Each ad is hook text on screen plus the shared hold VO.
   - Sizes needed listed 1080x1080, 1080x1350, 1080x1920 and 1920x1080. Only 1080x1920 (9:16) is clearly ticked.
4. **Download all of the creator's content, including previous batches, and reuse old b-roll.** "Running out of b-roll is your worst nightmare." [03:00]
5. **Use a numbered, consistent folder template for every batch.** [03:00–09:00]
   - Folders: `_Export`, `01 Project Files`, `02 Footages`, `03 Assets`, `04 Other`.
   - `03 Assets` holds `_Client` (logos, end cards, brand assets), Audio (music and SFX), Image (safe-zone PNG plus any images) and Video (downloaded b-roll, stock, TikTok, YouTube).
   - `04 Other` is rarely used, for online templates such as motion graphics and graphs.
   - Name files "Client - Batch# - Concept Name - Format - Type - Editor - Variation#", the same for .prproj and .aep.
   - Reasons: saves time, makes client changes easier, builds a good habit, and lets another editor take over.
6. **Sequence settings: 30 fps, 1080×1920 (9:16).** Pick one workspace layout and stick with it (he uses a vertical one), and learn keyboard shortcuts early. [09:00]
7. **Keep a safe-zone PNG on the topmost video track, at about 20% opacity, locked, until the edit is fully done.** It has 10 video and 6 audio tracks available. [09:00, 12:00]
8. **Edit in layers, one task at a time, in this order:** A-roll/VO → Music → B-roll → Animations/Transitions → Captions/Text → SFX. UGC edits should take "just a couple of minutes". [12:00]
9. **A-roll rule #1: no silences.** [12:00–18:00]
   - Remove silent gaps between phrases, breaths, and filler words ("uh", "um"). Scrollers "don't have time for that."
   - If the creator recorded the hook several times, keep the best take.
   - On long videos, cut from the back (end toward start) so earlier positions don't shift.
   - Don't trim too tight. His first pass on "Can we stop with the… getting my body back narrative" was "a bit too much", and he loosened it. Leave a small natural breath.
10. **Voice audio chain.** Use the Audio Track Mixer (not Audio Clip Mixer), because an effect on a track applies to all its clips. [18:00–24:00]
    - Target VO level is **-6 dB to -12 dB**. Raw VO sat around -18 dB and was inconsistent.
    - Slot 1: Multiband Compressor with the **Broadcast** preset (podcast-like, crisp). Set gain so the voice doesn't peak above -6 dB.
    - Slot 2: Hard Limiter with the **"Limit to -6dB"** preset, which cuts anything above -6.
    - If the limiter sounds harsh, lower the multiband compressor's output gain so the signal isn't pinned at -6 constantly. Output gain ended at about -6 dB.
    - Settings vary per recording, so experiment.
11. **Music follows the emotion you want**, and no music is valid. He used none, so the ad feels like a person talking to the audience, authentic and raw on a sensitive topic. [24:00]
12. **B-roll emphasizes the A-roll, so show what is being said.** [24:00–27:00]
    - Nobody wants a talking head all video; b-roll "breaks the monotone".
    - Example: "It grew two sets of humans…" got a cutaway of her holding her belly.
13. **In UGC/organic ads, keep b-roll to the same creator**, not a random stranger. [27:00–30:00]
    - It reads as one person's journey, and the agency has seen better results with it.
    - Match b-roll to what the audience is thinking at that moment.
    - Calories line → her eating (older clip from the "Metabolic Gummies" batch).
    - Product line → product pour or pouch.
    - Transformation: "before" (pregnant-looking body) early, "after" late over "Four months later. And I don't want my old body…". The before and after clips share the same room.
    - A few stock clips are OK. Keep it raw.
14. **Fix jump cuts left by silence removal by alternating scale between adjacent clips.** [30:00]
    - First clip 100, next 110. A very abrupt cut got 112.
    - The practical minimum is about 102, 103 or 106. Smaller values make no difference.
    - Don't overdo it: alternating 106 and 112 repeatedly looks "funny".
    - Cover awkward cuts with b-roll ("B-rolls to the rescue").
    - Add a subtle emphasis zoom (112) on key sentences, e.g. "But that doesn't mean I have to accept feeling uncomfortable in it forever."
    - Compare each cut with and without the change.
15. **Give claims authority with an inserted graph, image or meme.** [33:00]
    - For "Prolactin levels stay elevated for 6 to 12 months after nursing…" he dropped in a cropped Google graph (prolactin/progesterone curves).
    - Purpose: the audience thinks "this person did her research."
    - Place it above the speaker's face, in the upper-middle, over the talking head.
16. **Captions: auto-generate, restyle, and keep them in the safe zone.** [33:00–39:00]
    - Premiere Text panel → Captions → Create captions (preset Subtitle default, Format Subtitle, Lines Double).
    - Style: Proxima Nova Semibold, white fill, black outer stroke ~4 px, shadow off, centered. Font size ended at 40 after shrinking (48 was too big).
    - Position: lower-middle or bottom, inside the safe zone with breathing space from its edge.
    - Review card by card against the brief script and by ear, since UGC creators ad-lib fillers ("well", "that", "this").
    - Split long cards into shorter ones (~5–8 words on 2 lines) because "most people might not read" a lot of text.
17. **Captions needn't be 100% polished.** A deliberate mistake, such as a caption changing slightly after the cut or while she isn't speaking, adds UGC authenticity if it stays clear. Normally the cut and the caption change are on the same line. [39:00]
18. **Hook text is a PNG made in Figma, not a native text layer**, because Windows emojis look bad. [39:00–45:00]
    - Use a TikTok-style rounded text box template (white box/black text or red box/white text).
    - Frame: auto layout, padding 40 horizontal/20 vertical, gap 10, radius ~20–22, fill #FFFFFF, Proxima Nova, export 1x PNG.
    - Save to `03 Assets/Image` as HOOK 1.png etc.
    - To edit copy, change it in Figma and re-export over the same filename. Premiere's linked file updates after reverting.
19. **Place the hook inside the safe zone, tilted slightly so it looks phone-made.** [42:00]
    - Rotation about 0.6, anything under 1.5, "even 1.2". Final values: Position ≈ 550.4/736.0, Scale ≈ 67.8, Rotation ≈ 1.2°.
    - The hook is the opening ~4–5 s of speech, cut at "…for a second, because that's not what this is about."
    - Colour-label hook clips (he uses blue) to tell them from the hold. Label colours can be mapped to shortcuts like Shift+F1/F2.
20. **Make hook variations by duplicating the sequence and swapping only the hook PNG.** [42:00–48:00]
    - Rename the copy (BREAKDOWN V2, V3).
    - Alt-drag the new PNG onto the old one (highlights blue) to replace instead of stack.
    - The hold is identical across variants.
21. **SFX draw attention to a specific action.** [45:00]
    - He used only one: a pop on the prolactin graph reveal (Premiere Composer free → Starter Pack → Sounds → Pop → "Hollow Pop 06"; alt "Small Pop 01").
    - Make it natural: Essential Sound → tag as SFX → Creative → Reverb → "Room Reverb" (speaker is indoors).
    - It was too strong, so he lowered the clip level and reverb Amount (~5.0, then ~2.5, then "decrease a tad").
    - Sound design is "an art of its own."
22. **Export checklist.** [48:00]
    - Set source in/out: go to the end, step back 2 frames, set the out point so no trailing black is exported.
    - Turn off the safe-zone overlay ("No one wants their safe zones in their final version").
    - Export H.264 with the Match Source – Adaptive High Bitrate preset ("good enough").
    - Review the whole edit before export, and review the exported file too (checked in VLC), since rendering can add artifacts.
23. **Questions go in the general chat so everyone learns; tag the instructor if there is no reply.** [51:00]

## Editing recipe

1. Read the Milanote brief: concept, hook copy ×3, hold VO script, sizes, variations, footage link. Ask the strategist about doubts.
2. Download the creator's footage, including past batches. Create the batch folder from the template and name it with the convention.
3. Open Premiere. Create a 1080×1920, 30 fps sequence, with 10 V and 6 A tracks.
   - Place the safe-zone PNG on V10 at ~20% opacity and lock it.
   - Set up a "Fav" effects bin (Auto Reframe, Crop, Drop Shadow, Gaussian Blur, Horizontal Flip, Invert, Lens Distortion, Mosaic, Noise, Transform, Vertical Flip, Warp Stabilizer).
4. **A-roll:** trim silences, breaths and filler from the raw ~1:15 clip, from the back to the front, keeping the best hook take. This ends around 1:01.
5. **VO audio:** on A1, Multiband Compressor (Broadcast) then Hard Limiter (Limit to -6dB). Lower the compressor output gain if harsh. Aim for -6 to -12 dB.
6. **Music:** decide. Here, none.
7. **B-roll:** add same-creator cutaways on V2 matched to lines (belly, eating/Oreo, before/after, product pour, pouch). Insert the prolactin graph (~3 s) over the prolactin line.
8. **Animations/transitions:** alternate clip scale 100/~106–112 on jump cuts. Add an emphasis zoom (112) on the key sentence. Cover bad cuts with b-roll.
9. **Captions:** auto-create, restyle (Proxima Nova Semibold 40, white, 4 px black outer stroke, no shadow, centered, 2 lines), move inside the safe zone, then proof and split cards. Optionally offset one caption change for authenticity.
10. **Hook text:** build the PNG in Figma. Place it in the upper-middle inside the safe zone (Scale ~67.8, Rotation ~1.2°). Label the hook clips blue.
11. **SFX:** one pop on the graph entrance with Room Reverb, level reduced.
12. Duplicate the sequence for hooks 2 and 3 and swap the PNG with Alt-drag.
13. Set in/out (end minus 2 frames), hide the safe-zone layer, export H.264 with Match Source – Adaptive High Bitrate, and watch the file.

## How an ffmpeg/Python agent applies this

1. **Brief reading / asking the strategist.** JUDGE. A model should parse the brief (hooks, script, sizes, variations) and flag ambiguities for a human. Check the target aspect ratio (here 9:16) and the hook count.
2. **Hook + hold structure.** AUTOMATE. Render N variants by concatenating or overlaying each hook PNG on the same hold with ffmpeg overlay, parameterized in Python.
3. **Download creator content and reuse past b-roll.** AUTOMATE the cataloguing (a Python index of clips by batch). JUDGE which clips fit a given line.
4. **Folder template and naming.** AUTOMATE. Python `os.makedirs` for `_Export`, `01 Project Files`, `02 Footages`, `03 Assets/{_Client,Audio,Image,Video}`, `04 Other`. Format the name from the convention.
5. **Sequence 1080×1920 @ 30 fps.** AUTOMATE. Use `scale=1080:1920,fps=30` and `-r 30` for every render.
6. **Safe-zone overlay.** AUTOMATE as QA only. Keep captions and hook inside the safe zone (green area, notch at the lower right) by computing positions, and never burn the overlay into the export. Optionally render a preview with the PNG at ~20% opacity (`overlay` with `colorchannelmixer=aa=0.2`) for checking.
7. **Edit in layers.** AUTOMATE. Structure the Python pipeline in the same order (A-roll → audio → b-roll → scale/zoom → captions → hook → SFX → export).
8. **Remove silences and fillers.**
   - AUTOMATE silence removal: `silencedetect` (or auto-editor), then trim and concat. Keep a small pad (~0.1–0.2 s) to avoid cutting too tight.
   - AUTOMATE filler detection with word-level transcription (Whisper timestamps for "uh/um").
   - JUDGE which hook take is best, and whether the cut feels too tight. Listen or check waveforms.
   - Process cut points back-to-front or compute all times from the original timeline.
9. **Voice audio chain.**
   - Premiere's multiband isn't in ffmpeg. Approximate with `acompressor` (e.g., ratio 3, a threshold near -20 dB), or `loudnorm`/`dynaudnorm`, then `alimiter=limit=0.5` (≈ -6 dBFS).
   - To tame harshness, lower the pre-limiter gain with `volume`.
   - AUTOMATE a check with `astats` or `volumedetect` that peaks are ≤ -6 dB and the average is within -6 to -12 dB. Aim there by iterating gain.
   - JUDGE the harshness by ear or a model.
10. **Music.** JUDGE. The default for raw, sensitive UGC is none. Otherwise AUTOMATE mixing with `amix` and `sidechaincompress` ducking.
11. **B-roll matching.** JUDGE with a model. Map script lines to clips (same creator, matching action). AUTOMATE the overlay: `overlay=enable='between(t,a,b)'` on the voice track. Keep b-roll cuts ~1–2 s and spread evenly. Keep the same creator, and use before early and after late.
12. **Alternate-scale jump-cut fix.**
    - AUTOMATE. Per A-roll segment, apply `crop=iw/s:ih/s,scale=1080:1920` with s = 1.0 on odd segments and 1.06–1.12 on even ones (minimum 1.02–1.06). Use zoompan or a per-segment `scale+crop` for the emphasis zoom (1.12).
    - Don't alternate 1.06/1.12 continuously. Insert b-roll over bad cuts instead.
    - JUDGE the result visually.
13. **Authority graph.** AUTOMATE the overlay: a PNG scaled to ~40% width at y ≈ 170–320 (upper-middle, clear of the face), ~3 s, timed to the claim. JUDGE or sourcing: a model must find or generate a relevant chart and avoid fabricating data.
