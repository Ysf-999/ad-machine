# A05 — 5 ugc (Video Ads Mastery)

**UGC ads are built as a persuasion sequence (hook → problem → twist the knife → product intro → feature/benefit → bad alternative → results → CTA) that sounds like an honest friend's recommendation. The editor's job is to assemble the creator's script and B-roll so it feels natural and holds attention.**

## Key lessons

1. Master UGC first. Once you can make good UGC ads you can make any other ad type, and it is the most common and most in-demand type. [00:00]
2. UGC replaced polished branded ads (product shots, background music, benefit/feature text) because people trust other people more than brands. Social proof is the underlying principle. [00:00]
3. Organic is casual, with no agenda. UGC is authentic but strategic: it lays out every reason the viewer needs the product, like convincing a friend to buy a game by listing graphics, storyline and multiplayer. Make it feel like a real person's recommendation, not a brand spot. [00:00]
4. Use this eight-beat structure, in order: Hook → Problem → Twist the knife → Product intro → Feature/Benefit → Bad alternative → Results → CTA. [03:00]
5. Hook: it is the scroll stopper. Qualify the viewer by calling out the target audience or saying something that makes them think "this is made for me." [03:00]
6. Problem: describe the main problem in the customer's own language and frustrations so it feels real. [03:00]
7. Twist the knife: dig into the emotional impact of the problem so the viewer realises how big it is and feels a bit fearful. [03:00]
8. Product intro: the product must feel like the natural next step, an "aha moment" in the story, never forced. A pre-emptive objection handle works, e.g. "before you scroll past thinking, oh great, another overpriced office chair, this is different." [03:00, 06:00]
9. Feature/Benefit: tie every feature to a specific benefit by answering "why should they care and how does it make their life better?" [03:00]
10. Bad alternative: address what they already tried and why it fell short, without necessarily bashing competitors by name. Acknowledging their experience builds credibility and trust. [03:00]
11. Results: show specific, believable proof (personal results, before/afters, customer results). [03:00]
12. CTA: tell the viewer exactly what to do next, simply and clearly, usually with urgency or an incentive. The sample CTA is "Click the link below to get limited time 20% off." [06:00]
13. The structure mirrors the customer's thought process: they realise they have a problem, see why other solutions failed, then look for proof the product is different. [06:00]
14. It is one of hundreds of workable structures, and the same skeleton works for skincare, fitness equipment and gadgets. Keep the core and swap the details. [06:00]
15. Variants: [06:00]
    - For very results-oriented products, lead with results immediately (for the chair, a split screen of bad posture before and good posture after).
    - Drop the "bad alternatives" beat if the product is unique with no competitors.
    - Show the product in action for about 30 seconds with minimal talking (nonstick pan: cook several foods, nothing sticks, no explanation).
16. "There's no one size fits all template." Understand the product, know the audience, and structure the content to feel natural. [06:00]
17. The production pipeline is research → ideation → script and shot list → production → editing. Each script section has dedicated shots and B-roll. Most briefs the editor receives will look like this. [09:00]
18. When studying reference ads, look at how they place the footage, how they match B-roll to the script, and how they transition between shots. [09:00]
19. Practice: edit a real UGC brief, creator script and B-roll into an ad, then submit it to the instructor for feedback. [09:00]

## Editing recipe

Based on the sample chair ad and the timeline mockup:
1. Start from the brief, the creator script and the creator's B-roll. Map each script beat to its own clip or segment (1 Hook … 8 CTA).
2. Lay the creator's voiceover or talking-head audio as one continuous bed across the whole ad.
3. Cut the visuals to follow each beat, switching shot type with the beat:
   - Hook and Problem: talking-head selfie at the desk.
   - Product intro: handheld wide shot of the product in the room.
   - Features: close-ups of the specific features.
   - Results: split screen (before on top, after below).
   - CTA: close talking head to camera.
4. Overlay B-roll on top of the voiceover rather than cutting sequentially. Clips overlap across several video tracks.
5. Burn in centred captions, 2–3 words at a time.
6. Add a section label or on-screen text where useful (e.g. 20% off at the CTA).
7. Export 9:16.

## How an ffmpeg/Python agent applies this

1. **UGC replaced polished branded ads, and trust in people over brands (lessons 1–3).**
   - JUDGE: check the rough cut for brand-spot qualities (stock music, product-shot montage, feature text). It should look like a person talking.
2. **Eight-beat structure and the beat definitions (lessons 4–11).**
   - AUTOMATE: transcribe the creator audio (Whisper) with word timestamps, then have an LLM tag each sentence with one of the 8 beats and output segment boundaries as JSON. Use that to drive the cuts and B-roll matching.
   - JUDGE: check the hook qualifies the viewer, the problem is in customer language, the benefits are tied to features, and the proof is specific. A model or human reads the tagged script for these.
3. **CTA with urgency or an incentive (lesson 12).**
   - AUTOMATE: check the last segment's transcript for an action verb plus an offer, and burn the offer text (e.g. "20% off") over the CTA clip with an ASS caption or drawtext.
4. **Structure follows the customer's thoughts, with variants (lessons 13–16).**
   - JUDGE: pick the variant from the brief. If results-heavy, move the results clip to the front. If there are no competitors, drop the bad-alternative segment. For a demo-only product, keep about 30 s of footage with the audio mostly muted or on music.
   - AUTOMATE: reordering is a concat of segments in the new order with ffmpeg.
5. **Pipeline and per-section B-roll (lesson 17).**
   - AUTOMATE: for each beat, match B-roll files by filename or CLIP/vision-model tags to the segment. Overlay each with `overlay=enable='between(t,a,b)'` on top of the continuous voiceover, which stays untouched.
6. **Study references (lesson 18).**
   - JUDGE: sample frames and cuts of reference ads with a vision model. Log shot-length distribution, B-roll placement relative to the script and transition types, and use them as targets.
7. **Practice exercise submission (lesson 19).**
   - SKIP: this is feedback from the human instructor.
8. **Captions and the split-screen result (on-screen specifics).**
   - AUTOMATE: write an ASS file with 2–3 words per event from the word timestamps, centred (`\an5`), bold white with outline. For the before/after, stack with `vstack` on a 1080×1920 canvas with each clip scaled to 1080×960.
   - JUDGE: confirm the captions aren't covered by the overlays.
9. **Browser-based editor, Layers panel.**
   - SKIP: GUI-only. Recreate the layering in an ffmpeg filter graph.
