# B07 — 7 video editing breakdown part 3: organic ad (Video Editing Breakdowns)

**One-line summary.** A full breakdown of an organic, AI-voiceover "negative framing" ad for Dog Friendly Co's no-pull harness (3 hooks, 1 hold, 1 CTA), covering the brief, cloning and prompting the ElevenLabs voice, and then the layered Premiere edit: VO, music, b-roll, split screen, transitions, captions and SFX.

## Key lessons

1. **Read the brief first, then pick the voice.** The brief tells you whether the ad needs an organic female voice or a male podcast-style voice. This ad needs an organic female voice. Knowing this up front saves time and AI credits. [03:00–03:30]
2. **The concept is negative framing: positives reframed as regrets.** [00:00–03:00]
   - "No more arm workout" = the dog stopped pulling.
   - "I have to stop every two minutes" = everyone asks about it.
   - "Zero excuse to buy a new one" = lifetime warranty.
   - The hold ends on "these are the kind of problems I can live with."
3. **Structure is 3 hook variations + 1 hold + 1 CTA.** [00:00–03:00]
   - Hook 1: "3 reasons I regret getting this for my dog."
   - Hook 2: "New dog owners – don't make the same mistake as me! Let me show you why I regret buying this harness…"
   - Hook 3: "I made a huge mistake buying one of these for my dog, let me show you why."
   - CTA: "Join over 400,000 happy dog parents today."
   - The brief's total-ad count is unclear in the notes: one view reads "2 total ads", another "3 total ads". Treat it as 3 hooks sharing 1 hold and 1 CTA.
4. **Most ad spend targets women aged about 25 to their 60s.** Keep this audience in mind when reading the brief. [00:00–03:00]
5. **Source the voice for cloning in this order.** [03:00–06:00]
   - The client's own UGC library first, looking for a matching voice.
   - Otherwise TikTok, searching ad-related keywords (here "dog harness"). The clone source was the third result, a Dog Friendly Co video.
   - Only the audio matters, so any download method works. The file must be under 10 MB for ElevenLabs. His was about 8 MB and 00:34 long.
6. **ElevenLabs Instant Voice Clone steps.** [04:00–06:00]
   - Go to Voices > Create or Clone a Voice > Instant Voice Clone.
   - Drag in the file.
   - Tick "Remove background noise from audio recordings" (keep it on when the clip has background music).
   - Click Next, then Preview voice. The preview line was "A dream without a plan is just a wish."
   - Name the voice and Save.
   - Tick the rights/consent checkbox; labels are optional.
   - Go to Studio.
   - The UI changes constantly (he has seen about 8 variations), but the process is the same.
7. **Studio project setup.** [06:00–09:00]
   - Studio > New blank project > audio project.
   - Enable chapters: three dots, top right > project settings > Enable chapters.
   - Model: **Eleven Multilingual v2**. Avoid v3, which is still alpha: "stability over new features."
   - Create a new chapter for a fresh start (he named them "OG" and "NEW").
8. **Script layout in Studio.** [06:00–09:00]
   - Paste hook 1, a blank line, hook 2, a blank line, hook 3.
   - Then a blank space and the entire hold, then a blank space and the CTA.
   - The blank lines make hook, hold and CTA obvious when editing later.
   - Each paragraph becomes its own clip on the Studio timeline. The whole read ran about 1:05–1:10 (about 59 s in the final OG chapter), with hooks 2–9 s each.
9. **Calibrate voice settings once, then never change them mid-generation.** [09:00–12:00]
   - The settings are Voice, Alias, Volume, Speed, Stability, Similarity, Style Exaggeration and Speaker boost.
   - Changing them mid-generation cancels everything generated so far and wastes credits.
   - Volume is the exception: it doesn't need regenerating (volume range -30 to +5 dB).
   - The generate button defaults to "playing until end", which generates the whole script. Switch to "generate selection" while testing lines.
   - Before the final run, set it back to "playing until end".
10. **Prompt the read inside the script.** [09:00–12:00]
    - CAPITALIZE a word for strong emphasis (REGRET, HUGE, EVERYONE, LIFETIME, ZERO, NOT). He didn't like the capitalized take of one line and reverted to the original; compare both and choose, using the OG/NEW chapters instead of regenerating.
    - A dash " - " creates a pause. It sometimes fails, so regenerate if it does. Without a pause there is sometimes no audio gap, which is annoying to fix in the edit.
    - Workflow: go through the whole script, add caps, add dashes, set mode to "playing until end", generate.
11. **Fix bad reads per paragraph.** [12:00–15:00]
    - Switch to "playing selection" and regenerate sentence by sentence.
    - Lock each paragraph once it's good (padlock icon).
    - Use "Override settings" to change settings for one paragraph only.
    - When done, click Export, pick the audio format and download.
12. **Premiere layering order is A-roll/VO → music → b-roll → animation/transitions → captions/text → SFX.** SFX is the final layer. [12:00–15:00, 36:00–39:00]
    - Bring in a safe-zone PNG, extend it over the whole sequence, set opacity to 20, lock the track.
    - Enlarge the audio track height to see the waveform.
13. **Cut silences from the VO.** [12:00–15:00]
    - He cuts silences and breaths (keys mentioned were garbled: Shift+C, then Q/E/Q; presumably ripple-trim edits).
    - Breaths matter less because the VO is AI-generated, but silences still go.
    - Colour-code VO clips: blue = hook, green = hold, teal = CTA.
14. **Music "gives soul to your ad."** [12:00–18:00]
    - The target feel here is light-hearted and happy, which has worked recently for dog-friendly creatives.
    - There are three sourcing options. All should be instrumental, since "I don't want any vocals messing up with my voiceover."
      - **Option 1, TikTok niche search.** Search keywords like "best dog harnesses". Pick a video with decent views and engagement, identify the song with Shazam or Google, then find the instrumental version.
      - **Option 2, stock sites.** Artlist is his pick for variety of good music, plus footage and AI tools. Epidemic Sound is also good. Free sites like Pixabay "aren't the best"; paying is "worth your buck". In Artlist, use the Genre and Mood filters. Include "Happy"; click the minus on a mood such as "Serious" to exclude it.
      - **Option 3, TikTok Creative Center** (bookmark it). Go to Trends > Songs and pick your target country (US here). The default is the last 7 days; the dropdown also offers 30 and 120 days. Clicking a song shows related videos. Warning: previews can be very loud.
15. **Music selection and placement used in this ad.** [15:00–21:00]
    - He chose the last-120-days list and the song "Aesthetic" (Tollan Kim, rank 7), because it was trending.
    - Check "See analytics" for interest over time, example videos, age range and related interests. Here interest was mostly in ads (Advertisement 740).
    - Google the song name plus artist to download it. Sped-up, slowed or reverb versions are also worth trying.
    - Import the audio, find the beat drop on the waveform, cut there and delete the part before it.
    - Move the music so the **beat drop lands at the start of the hold**, and extend the track through the rest of the hook.
    - Set the level to about -18 to -22 dB; he uses **-20 dB**.
    - Leave the music unprocessed for an organic feel: "I'm not going to do any edits on the audio."
    - Placement is situational. The drop could start in the hook or later in the hold. Experiment and share findings.
16. **Use clips from one creator only for an organic ad.** [21:00–24:00]
    - It should look as though she filmed and edited it herself.
    - A second creator might make viewers skeptical or confused, so they scroll away.
    - He could do this because he knew the client's library well enough to trust one creator would cover it.
17. **Keep b-roll in a separate "PANCAKE" sequence stacked above the main edit.** [21:00–24:00]
    - Method 1: skim the pancake timeline and drag clips into the main sequence. Toggle off Linked Selection first so only video is selected and dragged.
    - Method 2: put the playhead on the clip and press F (match frame) to load it in the source monitor. Press I and O for in/out, then use drag-video-only and drop the pre-cut clip on the timeline.
    - The choice is preference.
18. **The opening split screen shows the difference right at the start and must be coherent with the spoken line.** [21:00–27:00]
    - The line is "First off. My dog suddenly stopped yanking on the lead."
    - Top half = bad example (dog pulling her). Bottom half = good example (calm walk in the harness).
19. **Split-screen mechanics for a 1080x1920 frame.** [24:00–30:00]
    - Midpoint = 1920 / 2 = **960**.
    - Show rulers with View > Show Rulers or Ctrl+R. Unlock guides (Ctrl+Alt+Shift+R may differ on Mac), drag a guide from the horizontal ruler, then right-click > Edit Guide and enter 960.
    - Give both clips the same Motion settings (copy and paste attributes). Seen values: Scale 50, Position X 540, anchor 1080/1920, top-clip Position Y about 606–686 (hard to read), bottom-clip Position about 540/960 and later nudged.
    - Crop Bottom on the top clip is **31.6%** (it was about 27.3% mid-drag). The same value is applied to the other clip. One frame showed bottom-clip Crop Top about 50%.
    - Hold Ctrl (Cmd on Mac) while dragging a value for fine increments.
    - Zoom in on the Program Monitor to check the seam lands at the centre.
    - Keep both halves inside the safe zone and visible at all times. Nudge the bottom clip's Position Y up a little if it sits near the safe-zone edge.
20. **Cover each line with a b-roll clip that matches its literal meaning.** [27:00–33:00]
    - "I no longer get that arm workout" → first-person clip of her being pulled by the dog.
    - "Everyone at the dog park keeps asking where I got it… I have to stop every two minutes to show them" → happy dogs in a park.
    - "Lifetime warranty" → close-ups of material quality (stitching, the NOVA-labelled strap).
    - "These are the kind of problems I can live with" → person and dog both happy with the harness. This is the video's turning point: it signals she doesn't regret the purchase.
    - "Shock absorption leash… if your dog's a puller" → the leash, then the product in action.
    - After the turning point, the b-roll shows the solution and how it works.
    - Keep the body simple, with no fancy effects.
21. **Extend short b-roll with Premiere's Generative Extend, sparingly.** [27:00–30:00]
    - Drag the clip edge to the needed length. Premiere conforms to 30 fps and, on Continue, uploads the clip to Adobe servers.
    - It costs credits. Use it only when necessary, or when close to the monthly credit renewal.
    - Trim start and end cleanly first. If a bit of the next frame bleeds in at the end, it extends from that last frame and looks bad.
22. **Spend real time on the hooks.** [30:00–33:00]
    - Most editors spend hours on the hold and about five minutes on the hook.
    - His line: "If your hook is shit, your hold is wasted."
23. **Pair a negative VO with a positive visual in hooks.** [33:00–36:00]
    - Hook 1: the line "I regret getting this" over the dog putting on the harness, which takes about 3 seconds. This creates cognitive dissonance and an open loop, and viewers stay for the answer.
    - Hooks 2 and 3 keep positive visuals under the negative lines (e.g. the dog chilling, then putting on the harness, a happy dog).
24. **Keep screen direction consistent between similar clips.** [33:00–36:00]
    - If a front-attachment clip is followed by the same action filmed from the opposite side, the viewer's eye jumps and the cut feels off.
    - Fix: apply Horizontal Flip to the mismatched clip. Toggling fx on and off showed the hand and D-ring staying on the same side once flipped.
    - Train yourself to spot these continuity issues.
25. **Captions.** [36:00–39:00]
    - In Premiere, open the Text panel and click "Create captions from transcript".
    - Use a brand-matched font: Proxima Nova Semibold, size 38, white, centred, Y position about -454, two lines per card, low inside the safe zone.
    - Check the captions against the script and fix errors.
    - The style being imitated (finished ad): 2 lines, centred, roughly mid-lower frame, short phrases of about 4–6 words, small white text with no visible box. The Teabox inspo ad used white rounded boxes with black text, 2–4 words. The TikTok clone-source video used bold white uppercase with a pink active-word highlight.
26. **Hook and CTA cards come from Figma.** [36:00–39:00]
    - Both are exported as PNGs in one sweep: the hook at the start, the CTA at the end (about 3 s).
    - The hook card is orange/peach rounded text. The CTA card reads "Join 400,000+ happy dog parents / Shop @dogfriendlyco" over a beach/sunset clip.
    - Keep everything inside the safe zone.
27. **SFX: minimal.** [36:00–39:00]
    - The only SFX is a seatbelt click each time the harness clips in ("works like a charm").
    - Jarring or "uneasy" effects belong only where the context calls for panic or a bad feeling, not every time.
    - Finish by making variations, double-checking, **turning off the safe-zone overlay before export**, and exporting.

## Editing recipe

1. Read the brief board (Milanote): concept, hook/hold/CTA script, size list, visual notes per script line.
2. Pick the voice type from the brief (organic female). Find a UGC voice sample (client library, then TikTok search). Download it, keep it under 10 MB, and clone it with ElevenLabs Instant Voice Clone (background-noise removal on).
3. In ElevenLabs Studio: new audio project, enable chapters, choose Multilingual v2.
4. Paste the script (hook 1, hook 2, hook 3, hold, CTA), each separated by a blank line.
5. Set voice settings once. Capitalize emphasis words, add " - " pauses, and generate selections to test.
6. Regenerate bad paragraphs individually, lock good ones, use per-paragraph override if needed, then export the audio.
7. In Premiere (vertical 1080x1920, 30 fps): import the safe-zone PNG at 20% opacity and lock it, then place the VO on A1.
8. Cut silences. Colour-code the VO (blue hook, green hold, teal CTA).
9. Find music (TikTok Creative Center, Artlist or a TikTok niche search), preferring instrumental. Align the beat drop to the start of the hold, set -20 dB, add no effects, and cut between the hook and hold sections.
10. Build b-roll from one creator only. Keep the pancake sequence above the main edit and pull clips by drag or by F/I/O.
11. Build the opening split screen (guide at 960, same Motion on both clips, Crop Bottom 31.6%). Match each spoken line to a literal clip. Extend short clips only if necessary.
12. Spend extra time on the hooks: negative VO over positive visuals. Flip mismatched clips so the action direction stays consistent.
13. Create captions from the transcript, restyle them (Proxima Nova Semibold, 38), proofread, and place the Figma hook and CTA PNGs.
14. Add the seatbelt-click SFX on harness clip-in moments.
15. Make hook variations, double-check, turn off the safe-zone overlay, and export.
