# A16 — 16 ai content upscaling (Video Ads Mastery)

**Summary:** Low-quality footage (about 360p) and weak product images can be made usable, up to roughly 4K, with Topaz Gigapixel AI for stills and Topaz Video AI for clips. The lesson shows a 2x image upscale and 4x video upscales, picks the Iris model for faces, and compares original and upscale side by side.

## Key lessons

1. **Use upscaling in two cases.** One is low-quality footage pulled from the internet. The other is bad product images that need enhancing. The claimed range is 360p up to "even like 4k". [00:00–01:30]
2. **Use Topaz Gigapixel AI for images and Topaz Video AI for video.** Both are desktop apps for Mac or PC. You import the file rather than upload it. The instructor says other AI tools do the same job, and he only shows what he and his team use. He will update the video if something better appears. [01:30–03:30]
3. **Upscale a still to enable punch-ins.** After upscaling you can zoom into the image. The demo took a 696x914 px image to 1392x1828 px at 2x. [02:15–03:30]
4. **Judge the result with a before/after split.** Drag or hover left to right to see how much noise was removed and how much crisper the image is. [02:15–02:45]
5. **Choose the Gigapixel model deliberately.**
   - Models offered: Standard, Lines, Art & CG, Low Resolution, Very Compressed.
   - Auto model lets the AI pick, but "sometimes you have to choose manually to get the best results."
   - On the test image, Low Resolution gave a "decent" result. The only flaw he noticed was the Apple logo.
   - Scale options: 0.5x, 2x, 4x, 6x, custom. [03:00–05:00]
6. **Turn Face Recovery on when faces are present.** In the demo it was on at strength 49. [03:00–05:00]
7. **Treat 360x640 @ 30 FPS as unusable on social media.** He says "You can't use that on social media nowadays." He upscales such clips 4x, to 1440x2560. Output can be 2x or 4x. [03:00–06:00]
8. **Use the basic enhancement model with Parameters on Auto for general footage.** "These are the settings I use." In the example this was Proteus – Enhance MQ. Then export. [04:30–06:00]
9. **Use the optional Video AI features only when needed.**
   - Stabilization is for shaky footage.
   - Motion deblur is available.
   - Frame interpolation is slow motion: you pick how much to slow the shot.
   - None of these were used in the examples. [03:30–06:00]
10. **Use the Iris model for faces and talking heads.** Iris is "for face and … recovering face and … upscaling faces." Settings used: Enhancement on, Progressive, Iris (Face / LQ-MQ), Input Condition Low Quality, Parameters Auto. Stabilization, Motion Deblur, Frame Interpolation and Grain were off. The result was "crisp and smooth". [06:00–09:00]
11. **Expect the model to fail on faces with little detail.** In the action clip the man's face wasn't reconstructed because it "didn't really had a lot to model on". The background text and the punching bag still got visibly sharper. [06:00–07:30]
12. **Expect short render times for short clips.** A 3–4 s clip should export in a couple of seconds (his estimate). Measured: about 18 s for a clip of about 2 s, and about 30 s for a clip of about 3 s. The on-screen export queue showed 26 s for the first export and about 31–44 s for the second. [04:30–08:30]
13. **Always compare original and upscale side by side before using the result.** He did this in QuickTime Player. [07:00–09:44]
14. **Accept minor artifacts if the clip is still usable.** The upscaled talking head still has some artifacts or "effects going on", but he calls it "still usable". The version he runs is about two years old and still works well. "If it works it works." [08:30–09:44]
15. **Keep upscaled clips short.** "As you saw, like 10 seconds, that's what we use." The demo files were 2–4 s. [09:00–09:44]
16. **Goal:** take content "from unusable to 4k". [09:30]

## Editing recipe

**Image (Gigapixel AI):**
1. Import the weak image (e.g. a Pinterest image or product shot).
2. Set Resize Mode to Scale, 2x.
3. Pick the AI model. Standard was the default; Low Resolution also looked good.
4. Set Suppress Noise and Remove Blur. Turn Face Recovery on if there are faces.
5. Check the before/after split.
6. Apply or export, then zoom in as needed.

**Video (Video AI):**
1. Load the low-res clip (360x640 @ 30 FPS).
2. Set the output to 4x (1440x2560).
3. Leave stabilization, motion deblur and slow motion off unless needed.
4. Pick the model: Proteus – Enhance MQ with Auto parameters for general footage, or Iris for faces.
5. Export as H264 (mov). The default save location was Downloads.
6. Play the original and the upscale side by side (QuickTime) and decide whether it is usable.

## How an ffmpeg/Python agent applies this

1. **Use cases (lesson 1)** — JUDGE. Check whether footage or a product image is too soft or low-res for the final canvas. Compare its pixel size with the target size, e.g. 1080x1920. Flag anything around 360x640. Then AUTOMATE the check with ffprobe on width and height.
2. **Tool choice (lesson 2)** — SKIP the Topaz GUIs.
   - They are commercial desktop apps. If a licensed install is available, check whether it exposes a command-line interface. The lesson does not say.
   - The agent could instead use a scripted AI upscaler. Real-ESRGAN for general content and a face-restoration model such as GFPGAN for faces are my suggestions, not from the lesson.
   - The fallback is an ffmpeg Lanczos upscale (`scale=1440:2560:flags=lanczos`) plus light `unsharp` and `hqdn3d`. Quality will be well below Topaz.
3. **Still upscale for punch-ins (lesson 3)** — AUTOMATE. Upscale 2x, then use `zoompan` or a scale+crop on the upscaled image.
4. **Before/after check (lesson 4)** — AUTOMATE the render with ffmpeg: `hstack` the original and upscaled frames, or extract frames. JUDGE with a vision model or human for crispness, noise, and artifacts.
5. **Model choice (lesson 5)** — JUDGE. Content type decides the model: lines or CG, low-res, or compressed. Try two models and compare.
6. **Face Recovery and Iris (lessons 6 and 10)** — AUTOMATE detection of faces with a face detector, then route those clips to a face-capable model. JUDGE the output for waxy or wrong faces.
7. **Resolution targets (lesson 7)** — AUTOMATE. Upscale 4x to 1440x2560 for 360x640 input. Downscale to the delivery size if needed.
8. **Enhancement defaults (lesson 8)** — AUTOMATE by fixing the parameters once. Keep the frame rate at the original 30 FPS.
9. **Optional features (lesson 9)**
   - Stabilization: AUTOMATE with ffmpeg `vidstabdetect` and `vidstabtransform`.
   - Slow motion: AUTOMATE with `setpts` plus `minterpolate`. Quality is lower than Topaz Apollo.
   - Use these only if the footage needs them.
10. **Face failures (lesson 11)** — JUDGE. Check whether faces look reconstructed. If not, consider cropping, covering, or choosing another clip.
11. **Render time (lesson 12)** — AUTOMATE. Budget roughly 10–15 s of processing per second of footage, from the measured examples. Keep clips short.
12. **Side-by-side comparison (lesson 13)** — AUTOMATE with `ffmpeg -i orig -i up -filter_complex hstack` to build a review clip.
13. **Artifact tolerance (lesson 14)** — JUDGE. Check whether remaining artifacts are acceptable once the clip is full-screen on a phone.
14. **Clip length (lesson 15)** — AUTOMATE. Trim to the usable segment, ideally ≤ about 10 s, before upscaling to save time.
