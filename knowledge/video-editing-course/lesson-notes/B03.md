# B03 — 3 video editing breakdown part 1 organic ad 1.1 (Video Editing Breakdowns)

**Summary.** Kamariddin (21, e-com editor at Karlo's agency, 4 months in) walks through the first half of an organic-style ad for Dog Friendly Co (Batch #95, "Watch How I Outsmart My Dog"). The finished ad runs about 43 s. This part covers reading the brief, organising the folders, generating the ElevenLabs voiceover, and cleaning, levelling and checking it in Premiere Pro. It stops where music selection begins. The workflow order is brief → VO → music → clips → captions → VFX → SFX.

## Key lessons

1. **Read the brief for ad type and the ad example first.** The example tells you the required captions, style, rhythm and pacing. Do this before reading the copywriter's text. [03:00–05:45]
2. **Brief spec for this job.** Format is 9:16 (1080x1920). Variations are 3 hooks plus 1 hold (body) = 3 total ads. Ad structure is Hook 1/2/3 → HOLD 1 (AI VO + copy, female, generic) → CTA. [04:30–05:15]
   - Hook 1: "This 1 change stops pulling forever" (visuals "similar to OG ad").
   - Hook 2: "Watch me outsmart my dog" ("same idea as V1 but diff clip").
   - Hook 3: "Watch my dog train himself". Swap to "herself" if the dog wears pink or has a feminine name.
   - CTA: "Shop the sale before it ends at dogfriendlyco.com".
3. **Judge what drives the reference ad's energy.** In the 0:42 reference, a rock-ish music track "artificially boosts" the pace and pushes the viewer to absorb information faster, more than the cutting does. Pick the music style deliberately for energy. [06:00–09:00]
4. **Read only the colour-coded comments in the brief.** They carry the copywriter's visual direction, and each script line gets one clear visual idea. [06:00–09:00]
   - Pink: "back clip closeup / red arrow to it – to highlight it", on "and that back clip harness is making it worse".
   - Green (yellow in some frames): "dramatic drag clip", on "lets them drag you down the street".
   - Orange: "demo this mechanism", on "Every time they try to lunge forward, the front attachment redirects that energy back toward you automatically".
   - The brand's one mechanism is the front-bearing attachment that redirects the dog's momentum. Force-show it.
5. **Organise the folders before anything else.** [06:00–12:00]
   - Make a project folder named exactly like the brief/batch title, and pin it to Quick Access.
   - Default subfolders are `stock` (clips found later) and `VO`.
   - Add `music` only if more than two music tracks are used. He usually skips it, but it keeps the project better structured.
   - Every video is its own project.
6. **Voiceover is the first layer of the edit.** Pick the voice the brief asks for ("AI VO + COPY (female, generic)"). He keeps a shortlist of pre-approved voices under ElevenLabs "Recently used" and auditions them by clicking play. He uses Brittney – Social Media Voice, model Eleven Multilingual v2, speaker boost on. [09:00–12:00]
7. **Tune ElevenLabs by ear, with trial and error.** [12:00–15:00]
   - A downward-sloping end intonation ("watch me outsmart my dog" falling) sounds generic. A more monotonic read, or one that fluctuates a bit but stays near one line, is better.
   - Stability makes the model less random. Style Exaggeration adds randomness in intonation.
   - Too much stability sounds robotic. Too much exaggeration sounds tired, too fast or too loud.
   - Settings seen in the hook-tuning sequence:
     - Start: Stability ~75–80%, Style ~5%.
     - Style to ~40% gave "too generic/robotic".
     - Stability down to 45%, then ~30%, gave "more life in it".
     - Style up to 50%, then 54% (the UI warns "Over 50% may lead to instability") gave poor takes.
     - Speed was nudged up a little at ~14:45 after a "too fast/too loud" take. His reason is unclear.
   - Settings at other moments:
     - Body/CTA takes: Stability 20–30% (the UI warns under 30% may lead to instability), Similarity ~50–65%, Style ~50%, Speed ~60–65% toward Faster.
     - Later regenerated lines: Stability ~50%, Similarity ~75%, Style ~10%.
     - One short phrase used Speed 0.95.
   - Credits are spent per generation (about 1 credit per character). Short hooks of 24–35 characters were regenerated many times.
8. **Generate the hooks as separate files and the body as one block.** [15:00–18:00]
   - Each hook is its own mp3 ("h1 hook dogs", "h2 hook dogs", "h3 hook dogs").
   - The body was pasted whole because the ad is only ~40–45 s. It was 673 characters (limit 5,000) and came out at 0:36.
   - Keep the CTA on one line so pronunciation works.
   - Punctuation can wreck pronunciation (the colon in "front:" and the dash). The first take was "good but not ideal". He downloaded it and fixed weak lines later.
9. **Use ChatGPT to fix TTS pronunciation of a domain.** [18:00–21:00]
   - ElevenLabs dropped the "co" in "dogfriendlyco.com".
   - ChatGPT (temporary chat) suggested spaces, phonetic spelling, caps or quotes, and SSML (`<say-as interpret-as="characters">c o</say-as>`).
   - What worked was writing the domain as words, phonetically: "Shop the sale before it ends at dog friendly koh dot com" (56 characters, ~2–3 s).
   - A second prompt that pasted the whole line worked better than a generic question.
10. **Premiere setup.** [21:00–24:00]
    - Make one plain sequence per hook (h1, h2, h3), 30 fps vertical. Start with h1.
    - Import the VO folder. Order for h1: hook VO, then "hold dogs" (the body), then the CTA.
    - Shortcuts: **D** deletes a selected gap or clip, **C** is razor, **A** is track select forward, **Shift+A** is track select backward.
11. **Level the VO first.** Raw hook peaks were about −15 to −21 dB, which is too quiet for phones played without headphones. [24:00–27:00]
    - Rule of thumb: A-roll (VO) peaks at **−3 to −6 dB** on the meter. Music, SFX and everything else can sit lower.
    - Occasional dips to about −9 dB are acceptable.
    - Add gain equal to the gap to the target. He added +11 dB, then +12 dB ("just right"). +15 dB drove the meter to 0 dB (clipping).
    - Gain is per clip, and the hook gets more than the body. Final values seen were hook 13 dB and body 12 dB.
12. **Flatten loud peaks with keyframes.** [27:00–30:00, 54:00–57:00]
    - Ctrl-click the clip's rubber band to add volume keyframes.
    - For one loud word, set 3 keyframes around it and drag the middle one down until it matches the surrounding VO height.
    - Ctrl-click the keyframes to ease them. This turns the "triangle" dip into a smoother "U", and a second Ctrl-click smooths it further.
    - Examples: "one change stops pulling forever", "drag you down the street", "automatically", and "Shop the sale".
    - Ceiling: peaks should not overreach about −3 dB.
13. **Delete all pauses, then re-add them deliberately.** [27:00–33:00]
    - In Text → Transcript, use the filter icon, choose Pauses, then Delete → Delete all.
    - Then go line by line and word by word until the read sounds natural. Check pause, intonation, and natural speaking sounds (inhales/exhales) that don't "mess with the vibe".
14. **Trim over-long pauses to about half, not to zero.** [30:00–36:00]
    - A hook pause that is too long stops viewers digesting what the video is about.
    - Use Track Select Forward to drag everything left after a trim.
    - To remove an inhale but keep the pause, cut at the breath, delete the unwanted part and leave silence.
    - Add fade-in and fade-out handles on both sides of a cut. Re-listen from the start after each edit.
    - Pauses were kept at "built for pulling", and after "down the street" before "But here's what happens".
    - Sequence length fell from about 42:11 to 40:23 after pause removal, then to about 40:17.
15. **Use Show Audio Time Units for sub-frame cuts.** Turn it on when the cut point you want falls between frames, and turn it off afterwards. [33:00–36:00]
16. **Regenerate a bad line instead of patching it.** Regenerating is "not a must-have, but this is the way how I like to go." [36:00–45:00]
    - Fix punctuation in the script. A stray mark clipped the ending of "…clip to the front", so he put a plain full stop after "front" and split the line.
    - To make a phrase sound like a continuation, start it with a lowercase letter ("so you're stopping their pulling without pain.", with "their" as a spelling choice for the sound).
    - Name the saved file after its text.
    - Pull files in via the Media Browser, so everything stays in the same view.
    - Delete the old part, insert the new clip, copy the old clip, then right-click → Paste Attributes.
    - Dialog checkboxes seen: Scale Attribute Times, Volume, Channel Volume, Panner, Essential Sound sections. Audio Effects was unticked.
    - Then remove the leftover gap and trim the tail.
17. **Hide joins with short crossfades.** [45:00–48:00]
    - Drag the right clip's edge left so it overlaps the previous clip, and Premiere builds the audio crossfade automatically.
    - One tooltip read "Duration 00:00:00:01, Fade Cosine Value 8". He later widened a crossfade to roughly 10–15 frames by eye.
    - He says these audio-transition features need Premiere 2025 (not in 2023; he is unsure about 2024).
    - If an inhale remains, make the fade cover it rather than deleting the pause.
18. **Leave a bigger pause before the CTA.** The viewer should sense the story is finished before the CTA arrives. He first made it "a bit too big" and trimmed by ear. [45:00–51:00]
19. **Blind-listen review.** [48:00–54:00]
    - Go to the start, look away from the timeline, and judge only by ear. This removes bias toward your own work.
    - Check that the read is organic and natural, and consistent in intonation and pronunciation.
    - Replay any part that feels off.
20. **Raise weak sections by hand, and do at least three passes.** [51:00–60:00]
    - Judge by waveform and meter, not by ear alone.
    - Meter at −12/−15 dB is the sign to raise the clip.
    - Sections sitting at −6 to −12 dB are too low.
    - The VO is split into about 10–15 chunks, and each gets its own level and fades.
    - The third pass is by ear with eyes on the volume only. It flagged "Shop the sale", "automatically" and "without pain" as slightly too loud.
    - Final VO length was 40:05 (00:00:40:05).
21. **Leave a small pacing doubt for the music stage.** Once the VO is clean, move on to music. A slightly off pace can be judged again with music in: "Maybe it would be adjusted when we add our music." [60:00–63:00]

## Editing recipe

1. Open the brief. Read the ad type and the ad example, then the colour-coded visual comments. Note the 9:16 format and the 3 hooks + 1 hold structure.
2. Create the project folder named as the brief, pin it, and add `stock`, `VO` (and `music` if needed).
3. In ElevenLabs, choose a female generic voice (Brittney, Multilingual v2). Generate each hook several times and judge the intonation. Download them as "h1/h2/h3 hook dogs.mp3".
4. Generate the whole body in one go ("hold dogs.mp3"). Generate the CTA on one line, using the phonetic domain "dog friendly koh dot com". Save everything in `VO`.
5. In Premiere, create sequence h1 (30 fps, vertical). Import the VO and place hook, hold and CTA on A1.
6. Set loudness: add per-clip gain so peaks sit at −3 to −6 dB. The hook clip got about +13 dB and the body about +12 dB.
7. Add keyframes to flatten any too-loud words.
8. Run Transcript → filter Pauses → Delete all.
9. Walk through the read line by line. Re-add and trim pauses to about half. Use Track Select Forward to close gaps. Cut and fade breaths. Use Show Audio Time Units when a cut is blocked.
10. Regenerate weak lines (fix punctuation, lowercase first letter if it should continue the previous phrase). Import them, swap them in and use Paste Attributes.
11. Add crossfades or fades at every join. Cover any remaining inhales.
12. Make a deliberate, bigger pause before the CTA.
13. Do three review passes. At least one is blind-listen, and the last is watching only the meter. Raise weak clips and lower peaks. Check the sequence length (about 40:05).
14. Move on to music selection.
