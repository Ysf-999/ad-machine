# A09 — 9 high production ads (Video Ads Mastery)

**One-line summary.** A strategy lesson, not an edit walkthrough: premium, cinematic ads position a brand as the market leader, but only when executed very well. Use them for premium pricing, visually bought products, or long-term brand building. Edit the footage to fit the brand (dynamic for tech, slow and moody for luxury), and note that even the most premium ad is worthless if no one stops to watch it.

## Key lessons

1. **Premium production is a positioning tool.** It makes the brand look like "the premium choice, the market leader" and makes competitors look "amateur and cheap". [00:00–03:00]
2. **Viewers equate better production with a better product.** With two brands at the same price (the on-screen example is $97 vs $97), viewers pick the one with the better-looking ad. Brand A runs typical UGC: a random person, basic features, a discount code at the end. Brand B runs cinematic, movie-like footage. [~01:15]
3. **The quality bar is high.** "Mediocre high production is actually worse than good organic content." Organic/UGC can scale without high production, so premium is an extra asset, not a requirement. [00:00–03:00]
4. **Premium ads serve a multi-touch journey.** The ideal is see ad → click → buy. In reality a viewer sees organic content or an ad, checks the brand's Instagram and website, may see another ad days later, reads reviews, then buys. Premium assets convince them the brand is "the real deal" at the checking stage. [~02:30, 03:06–03:35]
5. **Go high production in three situations.** [03:35–05:00]
   - Establishing a premium position. His example is skincare priced about 3x the market. The ad must "look as premium as your price tag", and basic Android footage won't carry a premium price.
   - The product is bought with the eyes: fashion, designer pieces, beauty, lifestyle products, glasses. Here the ad's production quality demonstrates the product's quality.
   - Long-term brand building. The footage is a reusable asset for a brand that runs for years.
6. **Reuse premium footage everywhere.** [05:02–05:24]
   - As B-roll mixed into UGC.
   - In VSLs.
   - As clips on the website.
   - As a "premium toolkit" to pull from whenever something needs to feel more expensive.
   - He recommends brands invest in at least some high-production content.
7. **There is no one-size-fits-all edit.** Some studio footage needs only "subtle color grading and clean cuts". [05:49]
8. **Match editing style to the brand.** [06:00–09:00]
   - Tech or startup (his example is an electric toothbrush): more dynamic editing, motion graphics and energy. Reference Apple and Samsung ads on YouTube.
   - Luxury (watch): perfect, cinematic cuts, luxurious slow motion, a moody vibe.
9. **Don't over-complicate it.** Editing high-production footage is the same as clipping UGC or organic footage together, just with more premium footage. Use it strategically, for impact and to position the brand where you want it. [06:00–09:00]
10. **Attention comes first.** "Even the most premium and luxurious looking ad is worthless if no one stops to watch it", which leads into the next lesson on hooks. [09:00–09:39]

## Editing recipe
The lesson does not walk through an edit. The assignment edit is only previewed:
1. The student receives a creative brief, raw studio footage and the ad copy.
2. Goal: a premium ad that positions the brand as an authority in the market.
3. After submitting, the student watches the instructor's walkthrough of his original edit. That ad "did okay" but was not a scaling winner.

## How an ffmpeg/Python agent applies this
1. **Premium positioning (lessons 1–3).**
   - JUDGE: whether footage actually looks premium (lighting, depth of field, color, stability). A vision model or human should reject mediocre footage rather than ship it, since it can hurt more than good UGC.
   - AUTOMATE: nothing here can make weak footage premium. Do not label weak footage "high production".
2. **Multi-touch journey (4).**
   - SKIP for editing. It is media-planning strategy.
   - Optionally AUTOMATE producing several variants (UGC, premium) as separate files for different funnel stages.
3. **When to use premium (5).**
   - JUDGE: a brief-analysis step decides whether the product is price-premium, visual, or brand-building. This is a text decision from the brief.
4. **Reuse footage (6).**
   - AUTOMATE: keep a library of premium clips (a folder plus a JSON index with tags). Cut them to 9:16 with ffmpeg crop/scale and drop them as B-roll overlays or inserts into UGC or VSL timelines.
5. **Subtle grade and clean cuts (7).**
   - AUTOMATE: light grade with ffmpeg `eq`, `curves` or `lut3d` (a LUT), and hard cuts with no flashy transitions.
   - JUDGE: check the grade doesn't clip or shift skin tones.
6. **Style by brand (8).**
   - AUTOMATE:
     - Tech: faster cut cadence, ASS motion-graphic callouts, zoom punches (`zoompan`/`scale` + `crop`), and energetic music.
     - Luxury: slow motion via `setpts` or `minterpolate`, longer shots, restrained or no captions, darker moody grade.
   - JUDGE: whether the result feels on-brand.
7. **Don't over-complicate (9).**
   - AUTOMATE: a simple concat of selected clips with copy-based or lightweight edits.
8. **Hook/attention (10).**
   - JUDGE: check that the first seconds are scroll-stopping, not just pretty. Details come in the hooks lesson.
